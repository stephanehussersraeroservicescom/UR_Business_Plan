## C.2 South Korea's Waste Management System

### C.2.1 Volume-Based Waste Fee (VBWF) System

**Implementation**: 1995 (national rollout)

**How It Works**:
- **Pay-as-you-throw**: Residents must buy official bags for general waste
- **Bag prices**: Vary by size and municipality
  - Example (Seoul): 20L bag = ₩490 (~€0.35); 50L bag = ₩1,230 (~€0.85)
- **Separated waste**: Free collection (incentive to separate recyclables)
- **Mandatory separation**:
  - General waste (paid bags)
  - Food waste (separate paid bags or bins with RFID)
  - Recyclables (paper, plastics, metals, glass) - free
  - Bulky waste (separate fee, scheduled pickup)

**Impact**:
- **Waste reduction**: 30% decrease in general waste after implementation
- **Recycling increase**: From 15% (1995) to 61% (2021)
- **Behavior change**: Strong incentive to separate and recycle

**Source**: Korea MOE; Seoul Metropolitan Government

---

### C.2.2 Food Waste Management

**Mandatory Separation**: Nationwide since 2005

**Food Waste Volume**:
- **~5.3 million tons/year** (15,000 tons/day)
- **Per capita**: 103 kg/year (among highest globally)
- **Source**: 70% household, 20% restaurants, 10% food service/institutional

**Treatment Methods**:

| Method | % of Food Waste | Tons/Year | End Product |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Composting (aerobic)** | 48% | 2.5M | Compost/fertilizer |
| **Anaerobic digestion (biogas)** | 24% | 1.3M | Biogas + digestate |
| **Animal feed** | 22% | 1.2M | Livestock feed |
| **Incineration** | 5% | 0.3M | Energy |
| **Other** | 1% | 0.05M | Waste-to-energy trials |

**Source**: Korea MOE; KECO

**Food Waste Collection Fee**:
- **RFID-based systems**: Increasing (especially Seoul, Incheon)
- **Residents weigh food waste** → charged by weight
- **Fee**: ~₩60-100/kg (~€0.04-0.07/kg)
- **Result**: 20-30% reduction in food waste generation

**Challenges**:
- **High moisture** (60-80%) → low energy value for incineration
- **Odor and leachate** → composting/AD have operational issues
- **Quality variability** → contamination affects compost/feed quality

---

### C.2.3 Plastic Waste Crisis (Korea)

**Plastic Waste Generation**:
- **4.2 million tons/year** (2021)
- **Growth**: +50% since 2010
- **COVID-19 impact**: +20% (2020-2021) due to delivery food, online shopping

**Plastic Composition**:
- **Packaging**: 60% (food, e-commerce)
- **Single-use**: 25% (bags, containers, cups)
- **Durables**: 15% (products, construction)

**Recycling Challenges**:

| Plastic Type | Collection Rate | Actual Recycling Rate | Main Issues |
|--------------|----------------|----------------------|-------------|
| **PET bottles** | 90% | 85% | Success story (deposit system) |
| **PE/PP (clean)** | 75% | 60% | Requires washing |
| **PS (styrofoam)** | 50% | 30% | Contamination, low value |
| **PVC** | 30% | 10% | Toxic concerns |
| **Multi-layer/film** | 40% | <5% | Not recyclable (complex structure) |
| **Average** | 65% | **~35% actual** | Gap = exported or downcycled |

**Source**: Korea MOE; Korea Recycling Industry Association

**The 2018 Crisis**:
- **China import ban** (January 2018): China stopped accepting foreign plastic waste
- **Korea impact**:
  - Exported ~50,000 tons/month to China → suddenly no destination
  - Waste piles at apartment complexes (refused by collectors)
  - National crisis, government emergency response
- **Response**:
  - Increase domestic recycling capacity
  - Ban on difficult-to-recycle plastics (colored PET, multi-layer)
  - Explore alternative technologies (chemical recycling, pyrolysis)

## C.4 Regulatory Framework (South Korea)

### C.4.1 National Waste Policy

**Waste Management Act** (Enforcement 2016, ongoing amendments):

**Key Provisions**:
- **Mandatory separation**: Recyclables, food waste, general waste
- **Producer responsibility**: Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for packaging, electronics, tires, batteries
- **Landfill restrictions**: New landfills require rigorous approval; no organic waste in landfills (since 2005)
- **Incineration standards**: Strict emission limits

**Targets** (2020-2035):
- **Waste reduction**: 20% reduction per capita by 2030 (vs 2017)
- **Recycling rate**: Maintain 60%+ (actual material recycling, not including WTE)
- **Landfilling**: Reduce to <5% by 2030
- **Plastic waste**: 50% reduction in single-use plastics by 2030
- **Food waste**: 30% reduction by 2030

**Source**: Korea MOE, "5th National Waste Management Plan (2022-2026)"

---

### C.4.2 Environmental Permitting for Waste Facilities

**Relevant Laws**:
- **Waste Management Act**: Facility licensing
- **Clean Air Conservation Act**: Air emissions
- **Water Environment Conservation Act**: Wastewater discharge
- **Environmental Impact Assessment Act**: EIA required for large facilities

**Facility Classification** (for Urban Rig):
- **Pyrolysis/thermal treatment**: Classified as "intermediate waste treatment facility"
- **Capacity threshold**: >100 tons/day requires full licensing + EIA
- **Licensing authority**: Provincial government (e.g., Seoul, Gyeonggi)

**Approval Process**:
1. **Pre-consultation**: Environmental authorities, local government
2. **Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)**: 6-12 months
   - Air quality modeling
   - Traffic impact
   - Noise/odor assessment
   - Public hearing (mandatory)
3. **Business plan approval**: MOE or provincial government
4. **Facility installation permit**: Provincial government
5. **Inspection and operation permit**: After construction
6. **Total timeline**: **18-30 months**

**Source**: Korea Waste Management Act; MOE guidelines

### C.5.3 Carbon Credits and Climate Policy

**Korea Emissions Trading Scheme (K-ETS)**:
- **Launch**: 2015 (world's 2nd largest national ETS after EU)
- **Carbon price**: ₩20,000-40,000/ton CO₂ (~€14-28/ton, much lower than EU)
- **Waste sector**: Not currently covered, but expansion possible
- **Offset credits**: Limited eligibility (forest, renewable energy)

**Carbon Neutrality Goals**:
- **Target**: Net-zero by 2050 (announced 2020)
- **2030 interim**: 40% reduction (vs 2018)
- **Waste sector responsibility**: Reduce emissions by 50% (vs 2018)

**Potential for Urban Rig**:
- **Not currently eligible** for K-ETS credits (waste sector excluded)
- **Future potential**: If waste sector added, significant credit generation
- **Voluntary markets**: Limited in Korea (underdeveloped)

**Source**: Korea Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy (MOTIE); K-ETS registry

## C.7 Urban Rig Opportunity Assessment (South Korea)

### C.7.1 Target Waste Streams

**Residual General Waste**:
- **Volume**: ~2.7 million tons/year landfilled + ~3 million tons/year residual to incineration
- **Total addressable**: ~5-6 million tons/year
- **Characteristics**: Mixed, contaminated, post-separation residual
- **Current fate**: Landfilling (declining), incineration

**Non-Recyclable Plastics**:
- **Volume**: ~2.5 million tons/year (60% of plastic waste)
- **Characteristics**: Multi-layer films, contaminated packaging, colored plastics
- **Current fate**: Incineration, export (declining), low-quality recycling

**Food Waste (Alternative Approach)**:
- **Volume**: 5.3 million tons/year
- **Challenge**: High moisture (60-80%), low energy value for incineration
- **Current**: Composting (odor, leachate), AD (limited capacity)
- **Urban Rig potential**: Biochar output valuable (soil amendment, energy)
- **Note**: May face regulatory classification challenges (food waste = organics recycling mandate)

**Total Addressable**: **~8-10 million tons/year** in South Korea
