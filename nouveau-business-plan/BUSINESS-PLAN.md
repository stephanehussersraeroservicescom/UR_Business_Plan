## How to Read This Plan

This plan summarises the annexes. It holds no figure of its own: every figure is followed by a link to its evidence, either a source of the project register [ID], a registered calculation [calc:ID] or a dated assumption or decision of the sponsor [hyp:D-xxx]. The evidence sheet gives the document, the page and the exact quotation. Where a figure is missing, the plan says so; it is not estimated.

OneWorld Corporation, the manufacturer of the Urban Rig, is the sponsor of this plan [hyp:D-027]. Its documentation is the design basis of the project and is marked "OneWorld design data"; these are the company's own figures and have not been verified independently [hyp:D-016] [hyp:D-020]. The scope is France only, with four feedstocks: residual household waste, mixed business waste, plastic waste and, later, airport waste [hyp:D-017].

The financial sections (Section 7) list the inputs set in the annexes. The results (revenue, costs, cash flow, returns) are to be completed from the financial model (Annexes 10 to 13).

The open points that affect the plan are listed in the project's reconciliation register of 25 November 2026; until they are settled, the plan states both readings where the annexes differ. <!-- nosrc -->

## 1. Executive Summary

**What the Urban Rig is.** According to OneWorld design data, the Urban Rig heats waste with superheated steam in an oxygen-free reactor and recovers fuel oil, char, metals and a combustible gas [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. The reference plant, the URC-2000, is a continuous line rated at 200 t per day [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC]; the plan feeds it at 140 t per day, 200 m³ at 0.7 t/m³ [hyp:D-035], or 51,100 t a year before any downtime [calc:CALC-A8-CAPACITE-AN]; the financial model uses 330 operating days a year [hyp:D-073] (Annexes 1 and 9). Whether 140 t per day is the tonnage received or the reactor feed after pre-sorting is an open point of the reconciliation register [hyp:D-035].

**What has been tested.** Batch tests filed with the Chiba prefectural government in 2022 gave a weighted average of 73.1 % oil on the 10 runs retained of clean PE, PP and PS [calc:CALC-A2-HUILE10-MOY-POND], and 73 % to 81 % on PE and PP [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES]. The Urban Rig has not been tested on raw household waste, and the performance of the continuous URC-2000 is OneWorld design data, to be secured by contractual guarantees and an acceptance test on the target feed (Annex 2, request R-009).

**The opportunity in France.** Ile-de-France collected 3.32 Mt of residual household waste in 2022 and sends 94 % of its residual household and similar waste to 18 incinerators [ORDIF-DMA-2022], which ran at 93 % of their technical capacity [A8-CST-ORDIF-UIDND-2023]. One URC-2000 would need about 1.5 % of this waste [calc:CALC-A4-PART-OMR-IDF]. Volume is not the constraint; a supply contract for a stable feed of known quality is (Annex 4).

**Why waste holders would pay.** The Syctom charges its member authorities 109.4 EUR/t for residual household waste in 2026 [A8-CST-SYCTOM-BP-2026], and French energy-from-waste plants charged private clients 120 EUR/t on average in 2023 [A8-PRIX-AMORCE-DT156-2023]. The landfill tax rises from 69 EUR/t in 2026 to 85 EUR/t in 2030 [CIBS-TGAP-2026]. The model takes a default entry price of 100 EUR/t for residual household waste, changeable by the operator [hyp:D-039] [hyp:D-042].

**What one tonne gives depends on the feed.** Clean plastics give mostly oil (Annex 2). Residual household waste gives, in the standard case, 87 kg of hydrocarbon oil and 107 kg of char per tonne received [calc:CALC-A5-HUILE-TOT-CENT-KG-T] [calc:CALC-A5-CHAR2-CENT-KG-T], and 421 kg of water [calc:CALC-A5-EAU-KG-T] (Annex 5). Mixed business waste gives 36.5 kg of oil per tonne in the standard case [calc:CALC-A7-HUILE-CENT-KG-T] (Annex 7). Household and business waste are therefore paid feeds with a modest oil yield, not oil feeds.

**The products are wastes until proven otherwise.** As measured on municipal plastics, the oil has a flash point below 40 °C [CHIBA-JLOS-HUILES] and is a hazardous waste fuel [A3-REG1357-2014-HP3]; the French end-of-waste route for pyrolysis oil applies only to sorted plastic waste sent to a steam cracker [A3-ARRETE-SSD-HUILE-PYROLYSE-2024] (Annexes 3 and 4). No carbon revenue is counted [hyp:D-028].

**Capital cost base.** The URC-2000 costs 50 MEUR with its options, installation included, shipping not included [hyp:D-050] [hyp:D-051]. With about 5 MEUR of pre-treatment and 5 MEUR for land, building, civil works, engineering and permits [hyp:D-051], the capital cost base is 60 MEUR per line before shipping [calc:CALC-A9-CAPEX-LIGNE]. This is not the total financing need: shipping, connections, contingency and working capital come on top (Annex 9).

**Permit.** Pyrolysis of non-hazardous waste falls under heading 2771 of the classified installations, under authorisation [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026] [A4-DGPR-NOTE-DECHETS-2022]. The plan assumes, by prudence, that the plant also falls under the Industrial Emissions Directive [hyp:D-052]. The review takes at least about 5.7 months after a complete file is lodged [calc:CALC-A4-DELAI-MIN-MOIS] (Annex 4).

**Conditions before investment.** A test of the Urban Rig on the target French feed with a full mass balance; contractual performance guarantees for the continuous plant; a position of the inspectorate on the classification; letters of intent from waste suppliers and from buyers of the oil and the char (Sections 9 and 10).

**Financial results.** To be completed from the financial model (Annexes 10 to 13).

## 3. Market Opportunity

### 3.1 Global Waste Context

Not covered: the scope of the plan is France only [hyp:D-017].

### 3.2 France as Target Market

French law aims to halve non-hazardous waste sent to landfill by 2025 compared with 2010, and to recover energy from at least 70 % of the waste that cannot be recycled [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021]. France collected 14,752,013 t of residual household waste in the 2024 campaign figures [RP2-004]. France recycled 25.7 % of its plastic packaging waste in 2023 [EUROSTAT-WASPAC]. Businesses produced 57 Mt of waste in 2021, excluding construction [A7-ADEME-TRI-DAE-2025]. Details: Annex 4.

### 3.3 Ile-de-France as Initial Deployment Region

Ile-de-France is the region studied in the annexes; the first site is not decided (Section 9). Its incinerators burned 3,810,761 t in 2022 [A8-CST-ORDIF-UIDND-2023], the Syctom diverted about 150,000 t to outside plants in 2025 [A8-CST-SYCTOM-BP-2026], and 2,228,079 t were landfilled in the region in 2022 [A8-CST-ORDIF-ISDND-2023]. The regional plan aims to send all mixed business waste to a sorting line in 2025 [A7-PRPGD-IDF-SUIVI3-2023], which creates sorting residues (Annex 7).

Incineration and landfill are the incumbent routes: 18 incineration plants with 4,270,900 t per year authorised and 7 landfill sites with 2,984,000 t per year authorised in Ile-de-France in 2023 [A8-CST-ORDIF-UIDND-2023] [A8-CST-ORDIF-ISDND-2023]. In chemical recycling, the Grandpuits pyrolysis plant of TotalEnergies, 15,000 t per year of sorted household plastics, started in March 2026 [A8-CST-TTE-RACHAT-2026]; Viridor closed its three plastic pyrolysis plants in 2026 [A8-CST-VIRIDOR-2026]. No pyrolysis project for mixed household or business waste in France was found in the sources read (Annex 4, request R-051).

## 4. Technology

### 4.1 Process Overview

According to OneWorld design data, the URC-2000 is a line of 7 units of about 12 t, built in series and assembled on site [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES]. Buckets of waste move through a heating zone at 150, 300, 450 and 600 °C and a cooling zone [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. Steam at 600 °C displaces oxygen; the oil fraction is vaporised and condensed, the rest carbonises [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. Details: Annex 1.

### 4.2 Process Characteristics

- Continuous operation, each unit at a constant temperature [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE].
- Mixed waste as feed, according to OneWorld design data; the plant layouts nevertheless include a crushing hopper and a sorting machine [OW-ENG-P04-P07-SPEC-PLANS] (request R-023).
- Process heat: on resin-rich feeds, the gas is expected to cover the burner demand under a working assumption [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-RESINE] [hyp:D-038]; on household and business waste, self-sufficiency is not established [hyp:D-029]. Electricity is bought from the grid [OW-CONSO-ENERGIE].
- OneWorld documents contradict each other on electricity (600 kW or 20 kW) and on fuel (10,000 L per day, or none after start-up) [OW-CONSO-ENERGIE] [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC] [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS] (requests R-015, R-060).

### 4.3 Oil Fractions and Their Markets

In OneWorld's design balance, gas oil makes up 80 % of the oil, light oil 10 % and heavy oil 10 % [calc:CALC-A1-OW16-PART-GAZOLE] [calc:CALC-A1-OW16-PART-HEATING] [calc:CALC-A1-OW16-PART-HEAVY]; the output of each zone has not been measured (R-013). The measured oils from municipal plastics fail the flash point of finished fuels, and their chlorine, 0.137 % and 0.526 % [CHIBA-JLOS-HUILES], closes the steam cracker without purification (Annex 3).

### 4.4 Environmental Performance

Measured dioxins were 0.011 and 0.025 ng-TEQ/m³ in 2023 [DIOXINES-2023] and 0.067 ng-TEQ/m³ in 2022 [CHIBA-OOSUMI-FUMEES], numerically below the EU limit of 0.1 [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3]. The limits apply at 11 % oxygen [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3], which the 2022 and 2023 reports do not give; compliance is not yet demonstrated and needs a full campaign on the target feed (Annex 2, request R-016). The process water of the test unit was untreated and above discharge standards [CHIBA-CONSTATS] (request R-017).

### 4.5 Safety

According to OneWorld design data, the continuous plants are controlled by microcomputer, with valves and sensors as safety devices [OW-ENG-P04-P07-SPEC-PLANS]. The parameters controlled, the safety interlocks and the operating staff are not documented (request R-026). Hazardous items in household waste (batteries, gas cartridges) must be removed before the reactor (Annex 5).

### 4.7 FORCE 9 Companion Technology

FORCE 9 is a catalytic stabilisation of the residues at about 400 °C, according to OneWorld [OW-ENG-P03-P19-GAMME-DELAIS]. It is included in the machine price with PRIMO (exhaust gas treatment) and SEA CORAL (water treatment) [hyp:D-050]. Whether it is needed, and how it performs on Urban Rig char, is not established (request R-017).

## 5. Product Line and Scalability

OneWorld offers continuous lines of 200, 100, 40 and 15 t per day and batch units of 5 t and 1 t per day [OW-ENG-P03-P19-GAMME-DELAIS]. The plan uses the URC-2000. Larger capacities combine several 200 t lines; a 1,000 t per day layout uses 5 URC-2000 lines [UR2000-JP-P09-P13-PLANS]. Lead times differ between OneWorld documents, about 10 months of manufacturing or 4 months from contract to test run; the plan uses at least 10 months, plus French permitting [OW-ENG-P03-P19-GAMME-DELAIS] [UR2000-JP-P09-P13-PLANS] (Annex 9).

## 6. Accepted Feedstocks

**Tested on Urban Rig equipment, in batch:** PE, PP and PS, alone and mixed; two municipal plastic-rich streams from Noda; mixed plastics with some PVC and FRP; tyres [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES] [OWJ-DEMO-2021] [OWT-DEMO-2020].

**Not tested:** raw household waste, paper and board, wood, food waste, textiles, PET, foams, sorting rejects, multilayer packaging and electronic plastics (requests R-021, R-040). PVC stopped at the melting stage in the 2021 runs [OWJ-DEMO-2021]; its acceptance awaits the chlorine investigation (R-013).

**Excluded from the plan:** shredder residues [hyp:D-037]. Brominated and electronic plastics are outside the end-of-waste route for the oil [A3-ARRETE-SSD-HUILE-PYROLYSE-2024].

## 7. Financial Plan

### 7.1 Revenue Model

Five revenue streams: entry price paid by waste holders, oil, char, metals and, on a plastic-only feed, surplus gas or hydrogen (Annex 1). Default entry prices are set per stream in Annex 8 [hyp:D-042]; no default is decided yet for mixed business waste (Annex 7). Revenue projections: to be completed from the financial model (Annexes 10 to 13).

### 7.2 Oil Pricing

Each fraction is priced at its fossil benchmark multiplied by a quality factor to be decided [hyp:D-030]. At the latest prices the weighted benchmark is 1,121 EUR/t; at a Brent of 80 USD it is 714 EUR/t, and at 50 USD 446 EUR/t [calc:CALC-A3-HUILE-MOY-REF] [calc:CALC-A3-HUILE-MOY-BRENT80] [calc:CALC-A3-HUILE-MOY-BRENT50]. These are benchmarks before any discount; the oil of household and business waste is off-specification (Annex 3). The model sells the oil at 0.80 of the weighted benchmark by default [hyp:D-067] and the char at 0 EUR/t by default [hyp:D-068]; hydrogen is not counted by default, and the metals of household and business waste are at 0 by default [hyp:D-073]. Revenues: to be completed from the financial model (Annexes 10 to 13).

### 7.5 Operating Costs

To be completed from the financial model (Annexes 10 to 13). Inputs set in the annexes: electricity at 0.1212 EUR/kWh, excluding VAT [PRM-EUROSTAT-NRGPC205-ID-2025]; non-road diesel at 1.3405 EUR/L, excluding taxes [A3-DGEC-NPG-2026-10-09-PRIX-FRANCE]; disposal of pre-sorting rejects and process water (Annexes 5 and 9). Working assumptions of the sponsor, without a source read: 330 operating days a year, 20 full-time staff, maintenance at 1 % of the capital cost in years 1 to 4 and 4 % after, insurance at 1 % of the capital cost, ramp-up to 70 % in year 1 and 85 % in year 2, waste tax paid by the plant at 16 EUR/t [hyp:D-073] [CIBS-TGAP-2026].

### 7.6 Energy Supply Model

Electricity is bought from the grid [hyp:D-028]. On a resin-rich feed, the process heat is expected to come from the gas [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-RESINE] [hyp:D-038]; on household and business waste, the deficit is covered by default by burning part of the oil, then by purchased gas oil [hyp:D-029] [hyp:D-069]. The model takes OneWorld's consumption figures per day: a burner demand of 2,152 MJ per tonne at 140 t per day [calc:CALC-MOD-BRULEUR-MJ-T-140] [hyp:D-072] and 600 kW of electricity, 14,400 kWh per day per line [hyp:D-073]. OneWorld is to confirm this basis and its stated 10,000 L of fuel per day [OW-CONSO-ENERGIE] (request R-060). Results: to be completed from the financial model (Annexes 10 to 13).

## 10. Risk Analysis

### 10.1 Technology Risk

The yields come from batch tests; their transfer to the continuous URC-2000 is not demonstrated and needs a performance guarantee (Annex 2, R-009). No test on raw household or business waste exists (R-021). The process heat balance on these feeds is not established [hyp:D-029].

### 10.2 Regulatory Risk

Classification under the Industrial Emissions Directive is assumed by prudence [hyp:D-052]; emission compliance at 11 % oxygen is not demonstrated [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3] (R-016). The oil and the char remain wastes in the base case, and the oil of municipal plastics is a hazardous waste [A3-REG1357-2014-HP3]; the classification of the char as hazardous or not is not established [A3-FR-R541-8-LISTE-DECHETS] (R-034).

### 10.3 Market and Pricing Risk

Oil benchmarks are at crisis levels in 2026: the Brent averaged 69.0 USD per barrel in 2025 and 116.8 USD in September 2026 [A3-BM-PINKSHEET-2026-10]. No public price exists for pyrolysis oil; the only European seller found stopped its plant in 2026 [A3F-PRYME-RAPPORT-ANNUEL-2025]. The sense of payment for the char is disputed (Annex 3). No carbon credit is available today [hyp:D-028].

### 10.4 Feedstock Supply Risk

Volume is available, but no supply contract exists (R-051). Mixed business waste has no published moisture, calorific value or chlorine (R-048), and about 21 % of it is class B wood [calc:CALC-A7-BOISB-PCT]. Clean plastics are bought by recyclers, so a plastic feed has a cost (Annex 8).

### 10.5 Operational Risk

Pre-treatment scope (R-023), off-gas routing (R-025), process water treatment (R-017), sodium from nappy superabsorbent forming low-melting ashes [A5-MALSEGNA-2021-ENERGIES-AHP], and contradictory OneWorld figures on electricity, fuel, size and lead time (Annex 9).
