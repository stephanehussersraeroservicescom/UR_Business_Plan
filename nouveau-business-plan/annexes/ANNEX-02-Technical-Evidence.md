## Key Messages for Investors

**1. The Urban Rig converts plastics into fuel at high rates.**
- In 11 batch runs on PE, PP and PS filed with the Chiba prefectural government in 2022, one mixed-plastic run is excluded from the averages (Section 2.2.1) ([Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [hyp:D-034] -->. Of the 10 runs retained, 8 produced between 72 % and 81 % of their input weight as liquid oil ([Annex 2.1.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-1-a-chiba-test-sheets.md#chiba-essais-fiches), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES] -->, with a weighted average of 73.1 % ([Annex 2.5.f](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-f-calc-other.md#calc-a2-huile10-moy-pond), 90 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-HUILE10-MOY-POND] -->.
- The two lower results (61 % and 55 %) were both on polystyrene, which leaves more solid residue ([Annex 2.1.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-1-a-chiba-test-sheets.md#chiba-essais-fiches), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES] -->.
- **Polyolefins.** On PE and PP, the dominant plastics in packaging, oil yields ranged from 73 % to 81 % ([Annex 2.1.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-1-a-chiba-test-sheets.md#chiba-essais-fiches), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES] -->.
- **Oil plus gas by difference.** The operator counts as gas everything that leaves the reactor neither as oil nor as solid residue (operator definition, Chiba report p. 27) ([Annex 2.1.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-1-a-chiba-test-sheets.md#chiba-essais-fiches), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES] -->. On PE and PP, oil plus gas by difference was 87.8 % to 99.6 % of the input ([Annex 2.5.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-a-calc-conversion-to-fuel.md#calc-a2-conv-pp-palettes), 95 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-CONV-PP-PALETTES] [calc:CALC-A2-CONV-PP-CHUTES] -->. On the 10 runs retained, the gas by difference averaged 16.3 % of the input, weighted by mass ([Annex 2.5.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-a-calc-conversion-to-fuel.md#calc-a2-gazdiff10-moy-pond), 90 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-GAZDIFF10-MOY-POND] -->. For mixed waste, the project takes a prudent 10 % of the plastic input as gas ([Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [hyp:D-029] -->.

**2. The manufacturer's design figures are consistent with the tests.**
- For a feed of 90 % resin-based waste, OneWorld design data state a daily output of 76.5 % oil and a further 11.5 % "recovery loss", mainly discharged as flue gas ([Annex 2.5.f](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-f-calc-other.md#calc-a2-ow16-huile-totale), 95 %; [Annex 2.3.b](annexe-02-preuves/A2-3-b-oneworld-english-brochure.md#ow-eng-p16-bilan), 95 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-OW16-HUILE-TOTALE] [OW-ENG-P16-BILAN] -->. OneWorld's daily table counts 1 litre as 1 kg; Section 2.8 shows it corrected ([Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [hyp:D-035] -->.
- OneWorld design data also state an oil recovery of "about 85%" from resin-based waste ([Annex 2.3.b](annexe-02-preuves/A2-3-b-oneworld-english-brochure.md#ow-eng-p16-bilan), 95 %)<!-- [OW-ENG-P16-BILAN] -->.
- OneWorld's 2021 demonstration runs on mixed plastics produced 83 % oil over three days (70 %, 88 % and 91 % per day) ([Annex 2.5.f](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-f-calc-other.md#calc-a2-owj-huile), 95 %; [Annex 2.3.d](annexe-02-preuves/A2-3-d-oneworld-demonstrations.md#owj-demo-2021), 95 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-OWJ-HUILE] [OWJ-DEMO-2021] -->; oil plus gas by difference, the weight reduction, reached 96 % ([Annex 2.3.d](annexe-02-preuves/A2-3-d-oneworld-demonstrations.md#owj-demo-2021), 95 %)<!-- [OWJ-DEMO-2021] -->. The three days are not independent repetitions (Section 2.3.1).

**3. All three products carry energy.**
- **Oil.** A third-party laboratory, the Japan Lubricating Oil Society, commissioned by Asada Shokai, reported a gross calorific value of 46,000 J/g (calculated) on oil from municipal packaging plastics, with a density of 0.8642 g/cm³ and a sulphur content below 0.05 % ([Annex 2.2.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-2-a-jlos-oil-analyses.md#chiba-jlos-huiles), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-JLOS-HUILES] -->.
- **Gas.** From its measured composition, the pyrolysis gas has a net calorific value of 37.4 MJ/kg ([Annex 2.5.c](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-c-calc-gas-calorific-value.md#calc-a2-gaz-pci), 90 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-GAZ-PCI] -->.
- **Char.** Two values are used, by feed ([Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [hyp:D-038] -->: 24.6 MJ/kg for char from plastic residues, the factor applied in the Chiba mass balances ([Annex 2.5.c](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-c-calc-gas-calorific-value.md#calc-a2-pci-char-plastique), 95 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-PCI-CHAR-PLASTIQUE] -->, and 32.09 MJ/kg for char from household waste, measured on a urethane char ([Annex 2.2.j](annexe-02-preuves/A2-2-j-izumitec-char-oil.md#izumitec-r35-339439), 100 %)<!-- [IZUMITEC-R35-339439] -->. Both are well above the 12,000 kJ/kg minimum for a French solid recovered fuel ([Annex 2.4.c](annexe-02-preuves/A2-4-c-char-outlet-references.md#arrete-csr-2016), 100 %)<!-- [ARRETE-CSR-2016] -->.

**4. On resin-rich feeds, the gas is expected to cover the heat demand of the continuous plant (working assumption).**
- OneWorld's burner rating corresponds to a heat demand of 1,507 MJ per tonne treated ([Annex 2.5.d](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-d-calc-energy-balance.md#calc-a2-bruleur-mj-t), 90 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-BRULEUR-MJ-T] -->.
- On OneWorld's resin-rich example, the gas would provide 2.9 times this demand if OneWorld's 11.5 % "recovery loss", which OneWorld says is mainly discharged as flue gas, is taken as pyrolysis gas ([Annex 2.5.d](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-d-calc-energy-balance.md#calc-a2-couv-bruleur-resine), 90 %; [Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-RESINE] [hyp:D-038] -->; and 3.7 times on pure plastic with 15 % gas, a rounded value of the measured gas by difference ([Annex 2.5.d](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-d-calc-energy-balance.md#calc-a2-couv-bruleur-plastique15), 90 %; [Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-PLASTIQUE15] [hyp:D-038] -->.
- The oil and the char remain available for sale.
- On household and municipal waste, self-sufficiency is not established: the tests did not measure their gas, which includes the moisture of the feed. Their energy balance will be built in Annex 5, according to the feed chosen ([Annex 2.5.0](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-0-working-assumptions.md), assumption)<!-- [hyp:D-029] -->.

**5. Measured emissions are encouraging; compliance with the EU limits is not yet demonstrated.**
- The raw values reported by the laboratories are below the numerical limit values of Directive 2010/75/EU for nitrogen oxides, sulphur oxides, dust, hydrogen chloride and dioxins ([Annex 2.4.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-4-a-eu-emission-limits.md#ied-2010-75-anx6-p3), 100 %)<!-- [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3] -->. These limits apply at 11 % oxygen and, except for dioxins, as daily averages ([Annex 2.4.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-4-a-eu-emission-limits.md#ied-2010-75-anx6-p3), 100 %)<!-- [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3] -->.
- Only the 2020 campaign reports the oxygen; after correction to 11 % oxygen, its measured values stay within the limits ([Annex 2.5.e](annexe-02-preuves/A2-5-e-calc-emissions.md#calc-a2-nox-2020-corrige), 95 %)<!-- [calc:CALC-A2-NOX-2020-CORRIGE] -->. The 2022 and 2023 reports give no oxygen and spot values only, so they do not demonstrate compliance; they do not show an exceedance either (Section 2.7.2).
- Most raw values are also within or below the stricter best-available-technique levels for new plants ([Annex 2.4.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-4-a-eu-emission-limits.md#bat-wi-2019-2010), 100 %)<!-- [BAT-WI-2019-2010] --> (Section 2.7). A full campaign on the target feed is required for a permit (R-016).

**6. Points to secure with OneWorld before financial close** (Section 2.9.1):
- performance guarantees for the continuous URC-2000 on the target waste mix;
- the chlorine level of the oil. The oils made from municipal plastics contain 0.137 % and 0.526 % chlorine ([Annex 2.2.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-2-a-jlos-oil-analyses.md#chiba-jlos-huiles), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-JLOS-HUILES] -->, below the 1 % of halogenated organic substances, expressed as chlorine, above which EU rules require hazardous waste to be incinerated at 1,100 °C or more ([Annex 2.4.b](annexe-02-preuves/A2-4-b-chlorine-references.md#ied-2010-75-art50), 100 %)<!-- [IED-2010-75-ART50] -->. This threshold is only a temperature trigger, not a fuel acceptance limit: combustion in a permitted plant remains possible if the permit and the buyer accept the oil (Section 2.4.1). According to OneWorld design data, the chlorine comes from PVC and similar materials in the feed ([Annex 2.3.a](annexe-02-preuves/A2-3-a-oneworld-urc2000-brochure.md#ur2000-jp-p03-p04-chlore), 95 %)<!-- [UR2000-JP-P03-P04-CHLORE] -->. With a PVC-free feed, or after dechlorination, chlorine would no longer close the higher-value outlets; they remain subject to other conditions set out in Annex 3: the end-of-waste route to a steam cracker is open only to sorted plastic feeds, the flash point and sulphur require refining before use as a finished fuel, and jet fuel is not open in the short term. A chlorine measurement on oil from a PVC-free feed is requested to confirm it (R-013).

**Reading for investors**

Without oxygen and without daily averages, the 2022 and 2023 results cannot be qualified as compliant: compliance is undocumented, not disproved.
- **Against the stricter BAT levels for new plants,** all values are within or below the ranges except the 2022 dioxin result (0.067 against an upper BAT level of 0.04).   These are requested from OneWorld (R-016). The French permitting framework is covered in Annex 4.
- **On the "no dioxins" statement.** According to OneWorld design data, the reactor is oxygen-free, so dioxins and CO₂ are not generated inside it ([Annex 2.3.b](annexe-02-preuves/A2-3-b-oneworld-english-brochure.md#ow-eng-p02-p14-affirmations), 95 %)<!-- [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS] -->. - **On the dioxin unit.** The Japanese reports give dioxins as toxic equivalents (TEQ) without stating the scheme; the BAT levels use I-TEQ. The comparison is therefore indicative.
- **Why the 2022 and 2023 dioxin results differ.** They do not measure the same thing, so they do not contradict each other:
  - 2022: one value of 0.067 ng-TEQ/m³ on the Noda test unit, during the run on non-combustible municipal residue of 2022-09-15, over the three furnace stages (350, 450 and 550 °C), in a provisional report ([Annex 2.2.d](annexe-02-preuves/A2-2-d-oosumi-exhaust-2022.md#chiba-oosumi-fumees), 100 %; [Annex 2.1.b](annexe-02-preuves/A2-1-b-chiba-mass-energy-balances.md#chiba-constats), 100 %)<!-- [CHIBA-OOSUMI-FUMEES] [CHIBA-CONSTATS] -->;
