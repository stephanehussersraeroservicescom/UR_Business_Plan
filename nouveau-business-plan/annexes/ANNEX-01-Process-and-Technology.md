## Key Messages

- **Waste in, fuels and char out.** According to OneWorld design data, the Urban Rig heats waste with superheated steam in an oxygen-free reactor and recovers fuel oil, char, metals and a combustible gas [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE].
- **A line of reactors, not a single kiln.** The reference plant, the URC-2000, treats 200 t per day [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC]. According to OneWorld design data, buckets of waste move through a line of 7 heated and cooled units [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES].
- **The process heat comes from the waste on resin-rich feeds.** The measured gas, once air is removed, covers the burner heat demand 2.9 times on OneWorld's resin-rich example, with 11.5 % gas [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-RESINE], and 3.7 times on pure plastic with 15 % gas, a rounded value of the measured gas by difference [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-PLASTIQUE15] [hyp:D-038]. On household and municipal waste, self-sufficiency is not established; their energy balance is built in Annex 5, according to the feed chosen [hyp:D-029]. Electricity is bought from the grid [OW-CONSO-ENERGIE].
- **Very low dioxin levels.** Measured dioxins were 0.011 and 0.025 ng-TEQ/m³ in 2023 [DIOXINES-2023] and 0.067 ng-TEQ/m³ in 2022 [CHIBA-OOSUMI-FUMEES], below the EU limit of 0.1 [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3].
- **Three points to secure with OneWorld:** the pre-treatment actually required (the plant layouts include a crusher and a sorting machine), the route of the off-gas in the plant, and the acceptance of PVC-rich waste (Section 1.6).

## 1.1 What the Urban Rig Does

According to OneWorld design data, the Urban Rig makes the inside of the reactor hot and oxygen-free with superheated steam, and recovers fuel oil from resin-based mixed waste, including waste mixed with food residue and mud [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. The organic part that does not become oil or gas leaves as char; metals leave in a non-oxidised state, according to the same source [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE].

The plant proposed for the project is the **URC-2000**, a continuous plant rated at 200 t per day [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC]. OneWorld designed it for household waste with a high moisture content, such as food residue, sludge and soil [OW-ENG-P04-P07-SPEC-PLANS].

## 1.2 How It Works

### 1.2.1 Reactor Design and Temperature Zones

According to OneWorld design data, a continuous plant is a line of batch-type reactor units, installed at 7 or 4 places depending on the purpose, each held at its own temperature [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. The waste stays in its bucket; the buckets move from one unit to the next [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES].

For the URC-2000, OneWorld design data state:

- 7 units of about 12 t, built in series in the factory [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES];
- buckets of 4.5 m³, moved 3 at a time, every 90 minutes [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE];
- a total time of 10.5 hours from inlet to outlet [calc:CALC-A1-UR2000-TEMPS-TOTAL];
- 48 buckets per 24 hours, each of at most 4.17 t, which gives 200.16 t per day [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC] [calc:CALC-A2-UR2000-CADENCE];
- a heating zone at 150, 300, 450 and 600 °C, then a cooling zone at 400, 150 and 60 °C [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE];
- a decomposition temperature of 600 °C at most, at a pressure below 0.2 MPa [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC].

The number of units and the temperature steps depend on the machine. Other OneWorld documents show a 2014 drawing with a maximum zone temperature of 500 °C [UR2000-JP-P09-P13-PLANS], a 2024 animation with steps at 250, 450, 600 and 350 °C [OW-VIDEO-ANIMATION-2024], and a 9-unit continuous machine at Toyohashi [OW-STEP-TOYOHASHI-9]. The configuration to be delivered is to be confirmed with OneWorld (R-024).

### 1.2.2 Superheated Steam Environment

According to OneWorld design data, a steam boiler (165 °C at most) feeds a superheated steam generator (600 °C at most), heated by a burner; the steam at 600 °C is injected into the reactor, where it displaces oxygen [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. In this oxygen-free, reducing atmosphere the waste is not burned: its oil fraction is distilled and vaporised, and the rest carbonises [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE].

The vapours then follow this path, according to OneWorld design data [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]:

1. a catalytic layer that adsorbs chlorine;
2. a condenser that liquefies the vapours;
3. a solenoid valve that separates gas oil and heavy oil according to the temperature of the vapour;
4. an oil and water separation tank, then a filtration tank.

The Noda test unit had the same sequence: the gas from the pyrolysis furnace passed through a catalytic reaction tank before the condenser [CHIBA-PROCEDE-HUILE]. OneWorld design data also state that chlorine from PVC becomes dilute hydrochloric acid in the steam and does not damage the reactor [UR2000-JP-P03-P04-CHLORE].

### 1.2.3 Off-Gas Recycling

The gas that does not condense is a fuel. The only analysis available shows hydrogen, methane, ethane, ethylene, propane, propylene and carbon monoxide, diluted with air at the sampling point [KANSO-B2002600]. Once oxygen and nitrogen are removed, 91.4 % of it is combustible [calc:CALC-A2-GAZ-PART-COMBUSTIBLE] and its net calorific value is 37.4 MJ/kg [calc:CALC-A2-GAZ-PCI].

According to OneWorld design data, the plant burns its own recovered fuel in the boiler and the superheater [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS]. How the off-gas is routed in a URC-2000 (to the superheater burner, to the boiler, or to a separate combustion stage) is not described in the documents read; the parts list of the Toyohashi machine includes a combustion tower connected to the gas-liquid separator [OW-STEP-TOYOHASHI-9]. This is to be confirmed with OneWorld (R-025), because it also decides how the plant is permitted (Section 1.5).

### 1.2.4 Continuous Operation

In a continuous line, every unit holds a bucket at all times. Each step, the buckets advance by one unit: a bucket of fresh waste enters and a bucket of char and metals leaves [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. After a cold start, the first 8 batches give no output while the line fills [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. Each unit stays at a constant temperature, which, according to OneWorld design data, keeps the vapour stable and avoids the heating and cooling losses of a batch reactor [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE].

### 1.3.1 Pyrolysis Oil and Its Fractions

In OneWorld's design balance for a resin-rich feed, gas oil makes up 80 % of the oil, heating oil 10 % and heavy oil 10 % [calc:CALC-A1-OW16-PART-GAZOLE] [calc:CALC-A1-OW16-PART-HEATING] [calc:CALC-A1-OW16-PART-HEAVY]. These shares are design figures; the output of each unit of a continuous line has not been measured (R-013). The measured oils are described in Annex 2: calorific value, density, sulphur, and chlorine of 0.137 % and 0.526 % in the oils made from municipal waste [CHIBA-JLOS-HUILES].

### 1.3.2 Char (Biochar, Carbide)

Two calorific values are used for the char, by feed [hyp:D-038]: 24.6 MJ/kg for char from plastic residues, the factor applied in the Chiba mass balances [calc:CALC-A2-PCI-CHAR-PLASTIQUE], and 32,090 J/g for char from household waste, measured on a urethane char [IZUMITEC-R35-339439]. Both are well above the 12,000 kJ/kg minimum of a French solid recovered fuel [ARRETE-CSR-2016]. Its intended outlet is as a solid fuel, for example blended with coke or coal [hyp:D-026]; specifications and prices are in Annex 3. It is not intended as a fertiliser: EU rules on pyrolysis materials in fertilising products exclude inputs from mixed municipal waste [FPR-2019-1009].

### 1.3.3 Recovered Metals

According to OneWorld design data, metals are extracted in a non-oxidised state, because the reactor contains no oxygen [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. In the 2020 tyre test, the steel wire was recovered and weighed separately (111 kg from 450 kg of tyres) [OWT-DEMO-2020]. The cleanliness and outlet of the metals are to be documented (R-031).

### 1.3.4 Off-Gas

See Section 1.2.3. On the household waste case, OneWorld's design gives 5 % of the input as off-gas [UR2000-JP-P05-BILAN]; the gas of the Chiba runs is calculated by difference and, for municipal feeds, includes the moisture of the waste; for mixed waste, the project takes the gas produced by the plastic fraction at 10 % of the plastic input [hyp:D-029].

## 1.4 Key Characteristics

### 1.4.1 Feedstock Acceptance and Pre-Sorting Needs

**What OneWorld design data state.** The Urban Rig does not require waste to be sorted or cleaned: plastics with mud, residues, metals or pesticides can be loaded after one day of storage to remove moisture [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. The buckets take the waste as it is [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES].

**What the plant layouts show.** The site layouts of the same documents include an input throat with a crushing function and a sorting machine [OW-ENG-P04-P07-SPEC-PLANS] [UR2000-JP-P09-P13-PLANS]. A pre-treatment step is therefore part of the plant. Its specification (size, removal of inerts, bulky items and hazardous objects) is to be obtained from OneWorld (R-023) and costed in Annex 9.

**What has been tested** (Annex 2):

- PE, PP and PS, alone and mixed, and two municipal waste streams from Noda (packaging plastics and non-combustible residue) [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES];
- mixed plastics including some PVC pipe and FRP, on the URT-10 [OWJ-DEMO-2021];
- waste tyres [OWT-DEMO-2020];
- shredder residue, which gave an oil that could not be measured [CHIBA-ESSAIS-FICHES].

**PVC.** In the 2021 runs, the PVC stopped at the melting stage [OWJ-DEMO-2021]. PVC also brings chlorine into the oil. Its acceptance will be settled with the chlorine investigation (R-013, R-021) [hyp:D-026].

**Not yet tested:** raw household waste, paper and board, wood, food waste, textiles and PET (R-021).

### 1.4.2 Emissions, Including Dioxins

**Dioxin levels are very low.** The measurements gave 0.011 and 0.025 ng-TEQ/m³ in 2023 [DIOXINES-2023] and 0.067 ng-TEQ/m³ in 2022 [CHIBA-OOSUMI-FUMEES]. All are below the EU limit for waste incineration of 0.1 ng/Nm³ [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3]. The 2023 values are within the best-available-technique range for new plants, below 0.01 to 0.04 ng I-TEQ/Nm³ [BAT-WI-2019-2010].

OneWorld explains this by the absence of oxygen in the reactor, where dioxins cannot form [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS]. The stack gas comes from the boiler, superheaters and heaters [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS], so the plant does emit combustion gas.

**Other pollutants.** Nitrogen oxides, sulphur oxides, dust and hydrogen chloride were all below the EU daily limits on the values as reported (Annex 2, Section 2.7). The values are not yet corrected to the EU reference of 11 % oxygen [IED-2010-75-ANX6-P3], and the 2022 and 2023 reports are provisional; final reports are requested (R-016).

**Flue gas treatment.** OneWorld sells a flue gas treatment unit, PRIMO (removal of nitrogen oxides, sulphur oxides, dust and odour), as an option [OW-ENG-P03-P19-GAMME-DELAIS]. The configuration for a French plant is to be specified (R-029).

### 1.4.3 Fractional Oil Separation

Each unit of a continuous line is held at its own temperature, so each releases a different cut of vapour: according to OneWorld design data, kerosene-type oil, gas oil, heavy oil and asphaltenes from the coolest to the hottest step [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC]. A solenoid valve then separates gas oil and heavy oil according to the vapour temperature [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. The plant therefore delivers more than one oil grade, not a single crude oil.

The measured composition of an oil sample is analysed in Annex 2, Section 2.4.3. The share of each grade on a continuous line has not been measured (R-013).

### 1.4.4 Process Control and Automation

According to OneWorld design data, the continuous plants are controlled by microcomputer, from the loading slot to the unloading slot, with valves and sensors as safety devices [OW-ENG-P04-P07-SPEC-PLANS]. The documents do not describe the parameters controlled, the safety interlocks, remote supervision or the operating staff (R-026).

### 1.4.5 Energy Requirements and Fuel Input

Annex 2 (Section 2.6) builds the energy balance on OneWorld's figures [hyp:D-021] [hyp:D-038]:

- **Heat.** The superheating burner corresponds to 1,507 MJ per tonne treated [calc:CALC-A2-BRULEUR-MJ-T]. The gas covers this demand 2.9 times on OneWorld's resin-rich example, with 11.5 % gas [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-RESINE], and 3.7 times on pure plastic with 15 % gas [calc:CALC-A2-COUV-BRULEUR-PLASTIQUE15] [hyp:D-038]. This supports OneWorld's statement that no fuel is needed after start-up [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS]. On household and municipal waste, self-sufficiency is not established: their gas was not measured and includes the moisture of the feed; their energy balance is built in Annex 5, according to the feed chosen [hyp:D-029]. A start-up fuel is needed.
- **Electricity.** Bought from the grid: 600 kW for a 200 t plant according to OneWorld's consumption table [OW-CONSO-ENERGIE], or 259 MJ per tonne [calc:CALC-A2-ELEC-MJ-T]. The brochure gives 20 kW [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC]; the gap is an open point of Annex 2 (R-015).

The batch tests at Noda used gas oil for heating and for a diesel generator [CHIBA-BILANS]. A small batch reactor heated for each run does not represent the consumption of a continuous plant [hyp:D-021].

### 1.4.6 Modularity and Scalability

According to OneWorld design data [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES]:

- the URC-2000 is made of 7 units of about 12 t, produced in series [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES];
- the machine is split into 7 parts, carried by truck and assembled on site [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES];
- larger capacities are reached by combining several 200 t plants [UR2000-JP-P06-P07-MODULES]. A 1,000 t per day layout uses 5 URC-2000 lines [UR2000-JP-P09-P13-PLANS].

Delivery and installation, according to OneWorld documents: about 10 months of manufacturing for a continuous plant [OW-ENG-P03-P19-GAMME-DELAIS], or 4 months from contract to test run [UR2000-JP-P09-P13-PLANS]; 30 days of installation on site and maintenance of 3 days every 3 months for a 200 t plant [OW-TRANSPORT-INSTALLATION]. These figures differ between documents and are to be confirmed (R-027).

### 1.4.7 Revenue Streams

The plant can earn from five sources. This annex names them only; amounts are set in Annexes 3 and 5 to 8 and in the financial model.

1. **Treatment fee** paid by the holders of the waste (Annexes 5 to 8).
2. **Oils:** light oil, gas oil and heavy oil, sold separately [OW-ENG-P16-BILAN].
3. **Char,** as a high-calorific solid fuel, for example blended with coke or coal; not as a fertiliser [hyp:D-026].
4. **Metals and glass,** sold for recycling according to OneWorld design data [UR2000-JP-P05-BILAN].
5. **Surplus gas,** where the gas exceeds the process heat demand; OneWorld lists the off-gas among the saleable resources [IGES-AEPW-PROPOSITION].

## 1.5 Comparison with Other Treatment Routes

**The EU waste hierarchy** ranks, in order: prevention, preparing for re-use, recycling, other recovery (for example energy recovery), and disposal [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4]. Recycling excludes the reprocessing of waste into materials to be used as fuels [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4].

**Consequence for the Urban Rig.** Oil sold as fuel counts as recovery, not recycling. Oil sold as a raw material, for example to a steam cracker, would count as recycling; steam crackers accept about 3 ppm of chlorine [KUSENBERG-AICHE-2021], far below the oils measured so far [CHIBA-JLOS-HUILES].

**Permitting.** Under the Industrial Emissions Directive, a pyrolysis or gasification plant is a waste incineration plant when the substances it produces are then incinerated [IED-2010-75-ART3-42]. The incineration chapter does not apply if the gases are purified until they are no longer waste and emit no more than natural gas [IED-2010-75-ART3-42]. How the off-gas is treated and burned (R-025) therefore decides the permitting route; Annex 2 already compares the measured emissions with the incineration limits.

**Recycling against energy recovery.** The European Commission's Joint Research Centre found recycling, whether mechanical, physical or chemical, preferable to energy recovery in all the pathways it analysed, and could not establish a clear ranking between mechanical and chemical recycling [JRC-2023-PLASTIC-RECYCLING].

| Route | What happens to the waste | Status in the EU waste hierarchy | Status under the Industrial Emissions Directive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incineration with energy recovery | Burned with oxygen; heat recovered as steam, electricity or heat | Other recovery [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4] | Waste incineration plant [IED-2010-75-ART3-42] |
| Gasification | Converted into a synthesis gas of small molecules [JRC-2023-PLASTIC-RECYCLING] | Recycling if the product is a material; recovery if it is burned [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4] | Incineration plant if the products are incinerated, unless the gas is purified [IED-2010-75-ART3-42] |
| Mechanical recycling | Plastics reprocessed into plastic material | Recycling [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4] | Not a thermal treatment |
| Pyrolysis, including the Urban Rig | Heated without oxygen into oil, gas and char [JRC-2023-PLASTIC-RECYCLING] | Recycling if the oil is a material; recovery if it is burned [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4] | Incineration plant if the products are incinerated, unless the gas is purified [IED-2010-75-ART3-42] |

**What sets the Urban Rig apart from other pyrolysis plants, according to OneWorld design data:** superheated steam as heat carrier and oxygen barrier, a line of units at fixed temperatures that separates oil grades, and mixed waste as feed rather than sorted plastics [OW-ENG-P14-P15-PROCEDE]. These features are the manufacturer's; no independent comparison of pyrolysis technologies was found.

**Not included here.** The quantities of residues from incineration (bottom ash and flue gas residues) were not found in a document read (R-032). Costs are compared in Annex 9.
