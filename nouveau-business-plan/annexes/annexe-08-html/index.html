Annex 8. Plastic Waste Streams (France)

This annex describes the plastic waste streams of France, stream by stream: how much exists, how much is really recycled, what happens to the rest, who pays for it today, and which streams could feed an Urban Rig. It covers household and industrial plastics alike, with priority to the streams that are little or not recycledD-018. Residual household waste as a whole is covered by Annex 5, mixed commercial and industrial waste by Annex 7 and the CDG airport by Annex 6. Shredder residues are excluded from the planD-037.

How to Read This Annex

OneWorld, the manufacturer of the Urban Rig, is the sponsor of this business planD-027. Its documentation is marked "OneWorld design data"; these are the company's own figures and have not been verified independently.

Each figure or fact is followed by a small link. It opens the evidence sheet: what the evidence shows, its confidence, the source document and its location, or the calculation. Sheets numbered 8.x belong to this annex; sheets numbered 1.x, 2.x and 3.x are those of Annexes 1 to 3. All sheets are listed in the index of evidence sheets.

Recycling rates are never collection or sorting rates. Each rate below states its scope (household or not, packaging or all plastics, year) and its definition.

Entry prices are inputs that the operator of the model can changeD-041. When a stream is selected, its default entry price is applied; the operator can overwrite it. A positive price means the plant is paid to take the waste; a negative price means the plant buys itD-042. Section 8.6 gives the defaults and their basis.

Reading of the sources. Most documents were read online by research sub-agents in October 2026, with short quotations. The key figures of this annex were then re-opened by an independent checker, who found them all in the documents; their sheets say so.

Revision. This version was revised after an independent check of the annex. The changes are listed, point by point, in the project's register of discrepancies for Annex 8.

Key Messages

  • France recycles about a quarter of its plastic packaging. In 2023, 625,275 t of the 2,428,987 t of plastic packaging waste were recycled, 25.7 %; 1,213,829 t went to energy recovery8.P.a. The EU average was 41.7 %8.P.b. About 49 % of the tonnage counted as recycled was recycled outside France8.C.a.
  • Clean plastic has a price; dirty plastic has a cost. Sorted, washed and shredded mixed plastic bales sold at 248.81 EUR/t delivered in North-West Europe in June 20258.L.a, and clean white expanded polystyrene is bought from waste centres at 55 EUR/t8.L.b. Holders pay 121 to 159 EUR/t to dispose of household sorting rejects8.L.c8.R.a, but these rejects contain only about 12 % plastic8.C.b.
  • A large intermediate group is taken back at a price of zero. In 2024, 164,879 t of household plastic packaging, 37 % of the tonnage taken back, had a take-back price of zero: films, the mixed "development" stream, polystyrene pots and trays, expanded polystyrene8.C.c8.C.d.
  • Polystyrene is very little recycled. Of the 60,000 t of polystyrene pots placed on the market each year8.L.d, about 5,000 t reach a sorting centre according to Citeo, as reported by the press8.L.e. Household expanded polystyrene, about 15,000 t, is sorted at 1,200 t per year and has no outlet of its own after sorting8.L.f.
  • Disposal costs rise. In 2026 the landfill tax is 69 EUR/t and the incineration tax 16 or 29 EUR/t, rising to 85 and 20 or 45 EUR/t in 20303.R.y. In Ile-de-France, incineration prices for commercial waste rose by about 18.6 EUR/t, excluding tax, between 2023 and 20268.C.e.
  • Chlorine and bromine set the limits. The oil can leave waste status for a steam cracker only from plastic waste with at least 85 % of PE, PP and PS and at most 3 % of PVC3.R.b. PVC has not been tested on the Urban Rig beyond its melting stage2.3.f, and no OneWorld document covers bromine (requests R-021, R-039).

8.1 The French Plastic Waste Picture

8.1.1 Tonnages

The official statistics count 2,482 kt of plastic waste in France in 2022, of which 380 kt from households8.P.c. This total covers only plastic collected as such: plastic collected in mixed waste is counted elsewhere8.P.d. It is not the full amount of plastic discarded.

The plastics industry federation estimates that 4,160 kt of post-consumer plastic waste were collected in France in 2024, by application8.L.g:

No public French source gives this breakdown. The ADEME study of the plastic waste that chemical recycling could treat is available only through a registration form and has not been read (request R-001).

Under the EU method, plastic packaging counts as recycled when, sorted by polymer, it enters pelletising, extrusion or moulding8.R.b. Fuel production is not recycling8.R.c.

The targets are 50 % for plastic packaging by end 2025 and 55 % by end 20308.R.c. The French law aimed to tend towards 100 % recycled plastic by 1 January 20258.R.d. The government's interim review found that 19 % of single-use plastic packaging had no recycling route8.L.i.

8.1.3 What Happens to the Rest

  • Energy recovery takes about half of plastic packaging waste: 1,213,829 t in 20238.P.a.
  • For all post-consumer plastics, the federation estimates incineration at 49.2 % and landfill at 27.6 % in 20248.L.g.
  • Exports of plastic waste, scraps and parings reached about 404,000 t in 20258.C.f, mostly to other EU countries, first Spain, Germany and Belgium8.P.e.

8.1.4 The EU Contribution on Non-Recycled Plastic Packaging

Each Member State pays the EU 0.80 EUR per kilogram of non-recycled plastic packaging waste8.L.j. For France this was 1.564 billion EUR in 20238.L.k. It is paid from the State budget and is not a tax charged to households or companies8.L.k. A tax to pass it on, proposed in the 2026 budget bill, was removed by a Senate amendment during the examination of the bill8.L.l; the final law has not been read for this point. It therefore creates no direct payment for a waste holder today.

8.3 Stream by Stream

8.3.1 Household Plastic Packaging, Overall

  • Placed on the market: 1,165 kt in 2024 and 1,174 kt in 2025; recycled 324 kt and 346 kt8.L.w. The scheme estimates the plastic recycling rate at 30 % in 20258.L.h.
  • Who pays. Producers pay Citeo per kilogram placed on the market. In 2026: 70.00 cents for clear PET bottles, 77.01 for rigid PE, 80.51 for rigid PS including expanded PS, 140.01 for complex packaging and 210.01 for PVC8.L.x. Local authorities receive a support of 776 EUR per tonne of plastic sorted and recycled8.L.y, plus the take-back price.
  • Take-back prices in 2024: clear PET bottles 347 EUR/t8.P.g; PE/PP mixed 53 EUR/t8.P.g; PE/PP/PS mixed 39 EUR/t8.P.g; films, the development stream, PS pots and trays and expanded PS 0 EUR/t8.P.g; weighted average 140 EUR/t8.P.g. For films and the development stream, the zero price is an assumption of the source, no price having been observed.
  • Main buyer. Valorplast took back 120,500 t of household plastic packaging in 2025, 76 % recycled in France8.L.z. Its guaranteed floor price is 0 EUR/t for PS and the mixed streams8.P.h.
  • No recycling route. In 2021, 66 % of household plastic packaging was considered recyclable and 17 % was PVC, complex packaging or other resins without a recycling route8.L.aa.

8.3.2 Household Films and the "Development" Stream

  • Tonnage: 97,379 t of household films were taken back in 20248.C.g, and 66,005 t of the development stream, rigid mixed plastics including multilayer trays8.P.g.
  • Recycling rate: not published for France. In Europe, 18 % of flexible films placed on the market were used as input by recyclers in 20208.L.ab.
  • Price: 0 EUR/t taken back (assumed by the source)8.P.g.
  • Urban Rig: PE and PP were tested; soiled films were not (Section 8.4).
  • Competition: over-sorting centres and the Grandpuits chemical recycling plant (Section 8.5).

8.3.3 Sorting Rejects of Separate Collection

  • Tonnage: 800,000 t in 2022, 23.0 % of the input of sorting centres8.L.ac; 750,000 t in another Citeo study8.L.ad; 496 kt in the government cost model8.R.a. In Ile-de-France, 146,368 t in 2024, 27 % of the separate collection, 98 % of it incinerated8.L.ae.
  • Composition: rigid plastics 7.5 % and flexible plastics 4.5 % on average in France8.L.ad, about 12 % in total8.C.b. No composition is published for Ile-de-France (request R-003); calorific value and chlorine were not found.
  • Who pays: the local authority, 159 EUR/t including tax8.R.a, or 121 EUR/t excluding tax in one 2024 contract8.L.c. The 2026 incineration tax falls to 8 EUR/t for residues of efficient sorting3.R.y, which need a calorific value of at least 9 MJ/kg8.L.af.
  • Over-sorting: four centres of 30,000, 25,000, 15,000 and 30,000 t per year, the last one, at Thiverval-Grignon, due to open at the end of 20268.L.ag.
  • Urban Rig: these rejects are a mixed waste, not a plastic stream; not tested (request R-040).

8.3.4 Polystyrene (Compact)

  • Tonnage: 100,000 t of household polystyrene packaging placed on the market each year, of which 60,000 t of pots8.L.d.
  • Real recycling: about 5,000 t reach a sorting centre according to Citeo, as reported by the press8.L.e, about 8 % of the pots8.C.h. Sorted pots were recycled in Spain into flower pots and hangers8.L.ah. Since September 2025, 80 % of the sorted tonnage goes to the Indaver plant in Antwerp, which turns it back into styrene, and 20 % to mechanical recycling in Spain8.L.ai. No recycled tonnage has been published.
  • Rest: the household pots not sorted end in residual waste (Annex 5).
  • Price: taken back at 0 EUR/t8.P.g; producers pay 80.51 cents per kilogram8.L.x.
  • Urban Rig: PS tested, with an oil yield of 55 % to 73 %2.1.b.

8.3.5 Expanded Polystyrene

  • Household packaging: about 15,000 t placed on the market; 1,200 t per year come out of sorting, and this degraded material has no outlet of its own; recyclers accept it only in very small proportions8.L.f.
  • Waste centres: 15 % of French waste centres collect expanded polystyrene8.R.e; more than 600 t were collected at 200 sites8.L.f. Clean white material is bought at 55 EUR/t8.L.b; one service provider charges a local authority 8 EUR per cubic metre instead8.R.f.
  • Commercial packaging: 71 kt placed on the market; taken back at 284 EUR/t in April 20248.L.q. A study for the fisheries authority quotes recycling rates of 33 % for all expanded polystyrene packaging and 50 % for commercial packaging, figures from the industry federation8.L.aj.
  • Building insulation: above the limit set by the EU regulation on persistent organic pollutants, insulation containing the flame retardant HBCDD must be destroyed and cannot be recycled. The limit, 1,000 mg/kg in the 2019 text8.R.g, was lowered to 500 mg/kg, with 200 mg/kg planned after a review8.R.h.
  • Urban Rig: expanded polystyrene waste tested, with 73 % oil2.1.b. Bromine is not documented (request R-039).

The project sponsor's understanding, checked. "Very little recycled": confirmed for household PS and expanded PS. "Compacted or melted into blocks": compactors and thermal densifiers are described8.L.aj, but no French tonnage was found. "No real outlet": confirmed for sorted household expanded PS; contradicted for clean white expanded PS, which has a buyer, and for compact PS, recycled into styrene since 2025.

8.3.6 Commercial and Industrial Packaging

  • Tonnage: 1,074 kt of non-hazardous plastic packaging placed on the market, of which 307 kt of flexible LDPE (stretch and shrink films)8.L.q. In Ile-de-France, 260,000 t per year of plastic waste from businesses, of which 17 % recycled; half of the commercial share is pallet stretch film8.L.ak.
  • Recycling rate: 26 % in 20238.L.q.
  • Who pays: an extended producer responsibility scheme starts for this packaging, with targets of 50 % in 2028 and 55 % in 2030 for plastic8.R.i; its start has been postponed by an order, read at the draft stage8.R.j; the final text is to be cited. Clear flexible LDPE was taken back at 151 EUR/t in April 2024; sorting rejects cost 188 EUR/t in energy recovery8.L.q.
  • Urban Rig: PE and PP tested (Section 8.4).

8.3.7 Agricultural Films and Plastics

  • Tonnage: 84,500 t of agricultural plastics collected in 2025; the overall collection rate of the scheme, all its streams, is about 75 %8.P.i. Used films to collect are estimated at 70,000 to 85,000 t per year8.P.j.
  • Recycling: 90 % of what is collected8.P.i.
  • Who pays: a contribution included in the product price8.P.i.
  • Urban Rig: PE tested. Little of this stream is in Ile-de-France.

8.3.8 Production Scrap

No source gives the tonnage, fate or price of plastic production scrap in France: NOT FOUND (searched: recycling federation, plastics processors' federation, ADEME). The recycling federation reports 1,024,000 t of plastics collected and recycled in 2024, 69 % of industrial origin8.L.al. Clean scrap is resold directly and is not a likely feed.

8.3.9 Plastics from Electrical and Electronic Equipment

Sorted plastics are in scope; shredder residues are excludedD-037.

  • Tonnage: 1,215,345 t of equipment collected in France in 20248.P.k. One producer scheme estimated the plastic in its household equipment at about 315,000 t, 17 % of the mass8.L.am.
  • Brominated plastics: 39,301.93 t of plastics containing brominated flame retardants were extracted by that scheme in 20258.P.l. Plastics are sorted at 2,000 ppm of bromine8.L.an; the brominated fraction goes to hazardous waste incineration, at a cost the scheme calls very significant but does not quantify8.L.am. The EU waste limit for the sum of these flame retardants is 500 mg/kg, lowered to 350 mg/kg from 30 December 2025 and 200 mg/kg from 30 December 20278.R.k.
  • Urban Rig: the polymers (ABS, HIPS) are not tested; bromine is not documentedD-040.

8.3.10 Plastics from End-of-Life Vehicles

  • Tonnage: 1,189,994 t of end-of-life vehicles in 2023; reuse and recycling 89 %, reuse and recovery 96.7 %8.P.m.
  • Dismantled plastic parts: 5,203 t recycled and 9,834 t reused in 20238.P.m. Shredder residues are excludedD-037.
  • Polymers: PP 37 %, PU 15 %, PA 12 %, PVC about 3 % of the plastics of current end-of-life vehicles8.L.ao.
  • Rules: French target of 65 % recovery of PP and PE from 2026 and 70 % from 20288.R.l. The new EU regulation sets 15 % of recycled plastic in new vehicles after 72 months and 25 % after 120 months8.R.m, of which 20 % from end-of-life vehicles, and allows chemical recycling8.R.n.
  • Urban Rig: PP tested.

8.3.11 Building Plastics and PVC

  • Recycling: 20,282 t of plastic from building products and materials were recycled in 2024, a rate of 7.0 % against a target of 17 %; plastic is 8 % of the products placed on the market in this category8.P.n. No breakdown by polymer and no share of PVC were found.
  • PVC: the polymer is 56.7 % chlorine8.L.ap. Europe recycled 765,972 t of PVC in 20258.L.aq. EU rules on lead limit the recycling of old rigid PVC8.R.o.
  • Urban Rig: PVC not tested beyond melting2.3.f; the share of PVC accepted in the feed is open until OneWorld testsD-040.

8.3.12 Textiles

  • Tonnage: 917,604 t of clothing, linen and footwear placed on the market in 2025, 272,479 t collected and 216,264 t sorted8.P.o.
  • Fate of the sorted tonnage: 54.47 % reused, 25.31 % sent to recycling, 11.03 % wiping cloths and 8.4 % solid recovered fuel8.P.o, about 18,000 t8.C.i.
  • Polymers: in non-reusable textiles, polyester 19 % and acrylic 12 %8.L.ar.
  • Fibre-to-fibre recycling is below 1 % in Europe, a secondary figure8.L.as.
  • Who pays: producers, 173.61 EUR per tonne placed on the market8.P.o.
  • Urban Rig: not tested (request R-021).

8.3.13 Furniture and Other Producer Schemes

  • Furniture and household goods: 1,974 kt collected in 2025 by the main scheme across all its streams, of which 1,549 kt of furniture8.P.p; across all its streams, 46 % recycled and 47 % recovered as energy; outputs included 407 kt of solid recovered fuel, 45 kt of foams and 13 kt of plastics8.P.p.
  • Urban Rig: foams (PU) are not tested.

8.3.14 Plastic in Residual Household Waste

Plastics make up 15.8 % of residual household waste, 2,329,258 t8.M.b. This stream is treated in Annex 5.

  • Chlorine. According to OneWorld design data, the chlorine of PVC turns into dilute hydrochloric acid in the steam and is captured by a catalytic layer2.3.h. The oils made from municipal plastics contained 0.137 % and 0.526 % chlorine2.2.a.
  • Outlet of the oil. To leave waste status for a steam cracker, the oil must come from plastic waste with at least 85 % of PE, PP and PS and at most 3 % of PVC, and contain at most 500 ppm of halogens without purification3.R.b. Each incoming batch must also be free of electrical and electronic waste and of waste that may contain brominated flame retardants3.R.b. Sorted electronic plastics and brominated plastics are therefore outside this route, whatever the purification. Otherwise, and for those streams, the oil is a fuel sold as waste to a permitted plant (Annex 3).
  • Bromine. No OneWorld document covers bromine. The project sponsor's working assumption is that bromine is removed largely as chlorine is; it is not documented and awaits testsD-040.

8.5 Competition for the Feedstock

  • Mechanical recycling: French and European recyclers take back the valuable streams (8.3.1).
  • Chemical recycling in France: the Grandpuits plant of TotalEnergies, 15,000 t per year, started in March 20268.L.at and is now wholly owned by TotalEnergies3.L.o. A financing company of its technology partner, Plastic Energy Finco Ltd, was placed in administration in the United Kingdom in April 20263.R.o. The Eastman polyester project in Normandy, more than 110,000 t per year in a first phase, is paused8.L.au.
  • Chemical recycling in Europe: Viridor closed its three plastic pyrolysis plants in 2026, citing weak demand and cheaper virgin plastic8.L.av.
  • Buyers of pyrolysis feed: they buy sorted, washed and shredded bales with at least 86 % polyolefins and at most 1 % PVC8.L.aw, at 248.81 EUR/t in June 20258.L.a.
  • Solid recovered fuel and cement plants: see Section 8.2.
  • Incineration: the default outlet, nearly saturated in Ile-de-France (Section 8.2).
