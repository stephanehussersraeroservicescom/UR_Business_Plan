# Annex 4. Country Study: France

This annex sets out the French context for an Urban Rig plant: the waste available (national and Ile-de-France), the rules that apply (waste law, environmental permitting, tax), the market (gate fees, outlets for the products), the competition and the main actors. The scope is France only [hyp:D-017].

## How to Read This Annex

Each figure or fact is followed by a small link to its evidence sheet. Sheets numbered 4.x belong to this annex; sheets numbered 3.x, 5.x, 7.x, 8.x and 9.x are those of other annexes. All sheets are listed in the index of evidence sheets.

**Legal texts.** The permitting and tax rules are read from the official texts or from documents of the administration. Their application to an Urban Rig plant has not been confirmed by the authorities. Where this annex says how a rule would apply, it is an assumption of the business plan, stated as such, until the regional inspectorate gives its view (request R-050).

**Revision.** This version was revised after an independent check of the annex. The changes are listed, point by point, in the project's register of discrepancies for Annex 4.

## Key Messages

- **The waste exists, and capacity in Ile-de-France is tight.** Ile-de-France collected 3.32 Mt of residual household waste in 2022 and sends 94 % of its residual household and similar waste to 18 incinerators [ORDIF-DMA-2022]. These incinerators burned 3,810,761 t in 2022, 93 % of their technical capacity [A8-CST-ORDIF-UIDND-2023]. The Syctom diverted about 150,000 t to outside plants in 2025 [A8-CST-SYCTOM-BP-2026].
- **One machine needs a small share of this waste.** A URC-2000 at 140 t per day would treat about 1.5 % of the residual household waste of Ile-de-France [calc:CALC-A4-PART-OMR-IDF], or 2.3 % of the waste landfilled in the region [calc:CALC-A4-PART-ENFOUI-IDF]. The volume is not the constraint; the constraint is a supply contract for a stable feed of suitable quality, at an acceptable distance (request R-051).
- **French law pushes waste away from landfill.** It aims to halve non-hazardous waste sent to landfill by 2025 compared with 2010, and to recover energy from at least 70 % of the waste that cannot be recycled [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021].
- **An Urban Rig plant needs an environmental permit.** Thermal treatment of non-hazardous waste, which includes pyrolysis, falls under heading 2771 of the classified installations, under the authorisation regime [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026] [A4-DGPR-NOTE-DECHETS-2022].
- **It may also fall under the Industrial Emissions Directive.** At 5.8 to 8.3 t per hour, the plant is above the 3 t per hour threshold of heading 3520 [calc:CALC-A4-DEBIT-TH-CHARGE] [calc:CALC-A4-DEBIT-TH-NOMINAL] [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026]. The plan assumes, by prudence, that it does, and budgets the best available techniques for incineration [hyp:D-052]; this is to be confirmed (R-050).
- **The permit takes time.** After a complete file is lodged, the review takes at least about 5.7 months with parallel consultation [calc:CALC-A4-DELAI-MIN-MOIS]; the time to prepare the file is not included.
- **Published reference gate fees: 109.4 and 120 EUR per tonne.** The Syctom charges its member authorities 109.4 EUR/t for residual household waste in 2026 [A8-CST-SYCTOM-BP-2026]; the average price in French energy-from-waste plants was 120 EUR/t for private clients in 2023 [A8-PRIX-AMORCE-DT156-2023]. These are reference points, not a price offered to Urban Rig; the gate fee is an input of the model, to be confirmed by offers on the same stream and territory.
- **The landfill tax rises; the incineration tax is lower.** The tax on landfill of non-hazardous waste goes from 69 EUR/t in 2026 to 85 EUR/t in 2030; the tax on efficient thermal treatment goes from 16 to 20 EUR/t [CIBS-TGAP-2026]. These rates set the cost of the alternatives. What the Urban Rig plant itself would pay depends on its classification, its feeds and the exemptions of the law (Section 4.3.3, request R-050).
- **In the base case, oil from mixed waste remains a waste.** The French end-of-waste order for pyrolysis oil applies only to sorted plastic waste sent to a steam cracker [A3-ARRETE-SSD-HUILE-PYROLYSE-2024]. Another legal route, for a product made from waste that is similar to the product made without waste, exists in the Environmental Code but has not been examined for this oil [A3-CODE-ENV-L541-4-3] (request R-050).

## 4.3 Regulatory Framework

### 4.3.1 Waste Law and the Waste Hierarchy

**European framework.** The Waste Framework Directive sets the order of priority: prevention, preparing for re-use, recycling, other recovery (notably energy recovery), disposal [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4]. Recycling does not include energy recovery or the reprocessing into materials to be used as fuels [WFD-2008-98-ART3-4]. An Urban Rig plant whose oil is burned as fuel is therefore "other recovery", not recycling.

**French objectives.** They are set by the Environmental Code, as amended by the AGEC law of 2020 [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021].

| Objective | Target | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Non-hazardous, non-inert waste sent to material recovery | 55 % in 2020, 65 % in 2025 | [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021] |
| Household waste prepared for re-use or recycled | 55 % in 2025, 60 % in 2030, 65 % in 2035 | [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021] |
| Non-hazardous, non-inert waste admitted to landfill, compared with 2010 | minus 30 % in 2020, minus 50 % in 2025 | [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021] |
| Household waste admitted to landfill | 10 % of household waste produced, in 2035 | [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021] |
| Energy recovery of waste that cannot be recovered as material | at least 70 % by 2025, notably through solid recovered fuel | [A4-CENV-L541-1-2021] |

**End of waste status.** French law sets the conditions for a waste to cease being a waste [A3-CODE-ENV-L541-4-3]. For pyrolysis oil, the order of 19 February 2024 opens this route only for listed plastic waste with at least 85 % of PE, PP and PS, and for use in a steam cracker [A3-ARRETE-SSD-HUILE-PYROLYSE-2024]. Mixed municipal waste is not on the list [A3-ARRETE-SSD-HUILE-PYROLYSE-2024]. No French end-of-waste criteria exist for a char or a solid recovered fuel [A3-MTE-CATEGORIES-DECHETS-SSD-2024]. The Code also provides that a product made in a plant using waste is not a waste if it is similar to the product made without waste and meets the four conditions [A3-CODE-ENV-L541-4-3]; this route has not been examined for the oil or the char. In the base case, the oil and the char of an Urban Rig fed with household or business waste are sold as waste, to buyers authorised to receive them (Annex 3).

**Solid recovered fuel.** The order of 23 May 2016 requires a net calorific value of at least 12,000 kJ/kg and limits chlorine to 15,000 mg/kg of dry matter [ARRETE-CSR-2016]. An order of 1 December 2025 eased several rules of this sector [A3-BDT-ARRETE-CSR-2025]. These rules matter if the char is sold as a recovered fuel; whether a pyrolysis char can be prepared as one has not been checked (request R-050). The third national low-carbon strategy plans the energy recovery of 4 Mt of solid recovered fuel [A3-BDT-ARRETE-CSR-2025]; this is a policy target, not a demonstrated outlet for the char.

### 4.3.2 Environmental Permitting (ICPE)

**Headings that may apply.**

| Heading | Scope | Threshold and regime | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2771 | Thermal treatment of non-hazardous waste, except recovered fuel boilers (heading 2971) and biomass-only combustion (heading 2910) | Authorisation (A), display radius 2 | [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026] |
| 2791 | Other treatment of non-hazardous waste; excludes installations classified under 2771 and 2971, among others | 10 t per day or more: A, radius 2; below: declaration with controls | [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026] |
| 3520 (IED) | Disposal or recovery of waste in incineration or co-incineration plants | Non-hazardous waste, above 3 t per hour: A, radius 3 | [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026] |
| 3532 (IED) | Recovery of non-hazardous, non-inert waste, by listed activities, including pre-treatment of waste for incineration | Above 75 t per day: A, radius 3 | [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026] |

The brochure prints no unit in its radius column [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026].

**Pyrolysis is thermal treatment.** The ministry's note on the waste headings defines thermal treatment as waste brought to a temperature above 180 °C, and includes thermochemical processes such as pyrolysis, gasification and plasma [A4-DGPR-NOTE-DECHETS-2022]. Heading 3520 corresponds to headings 2770, 2771 and 2971 [A4-DGPR-NOTE-DECHETS-2022].

**Is the plant an incineration plant under the IED?** The directive includes pyrolysis in incineration if the substances it produces are then incinerated [IED-2010-75-ART3-42]. Since its amendment of 2024, the incineration chapter does not apply to a pyrolysis plant if the gases or liquids it produces are treated before being burned so that they emit less than the least polluting fuel that could be burned in the installation [A4-IED-2024-1785-ART42]. Its transposition into French law has not been checked. According to OneWorld design data, the plant burns its own recovered fuel in the boiler and the superheater [OW-ENG-P02-P14-AFFIRMATIONS]; how the off-gas is routed is not described (Annex 1, request R-025). The oil, if sold as a fuel, is burned elsewhere. The answer is therefore uncertain.

**Capacity per hour.**

| Basis | t per day | t per hour | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactor feed used in the plan | 140 | 5.83 | [calc:CALC-A4-DEBIT-TH-CHARGE] [hyp:D-035] |
| Nominal capacity | 200 | 8.33 | [calc:CALC-A4-DEBIT-TH-NOMINAL] [UR2000-JP-P08-SPEC] |

Both are above the 3 t per hour threshold of heading 3520 [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026]. Heading 3532 applies to listed activities above 75 t per day [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026]: it does not apply to the reactor by its capacity alone, but may apply to a separate pre-treatment line handling 140 t per day [hyp:D-035]. Heading 2791 does not apply to an installation classified under 2771 [A4-AIDA-NOMENCLATURE-V58-2026].

**Assumption for the plan.** In plain terms: authorisation under heading 2771, probable [hyp:D-052]; IED heading 3520, assumed by prudence, with the best available techniques for incineration; heading 3532, to be examined for each pre-treatment operation [hyp:D-052]. An uncertain classification does not prevent the project; it affects the permit file and the timetable. The emission levels associated with those techniques are given in Annex 2 [BAT-WI-2019-2010]. This classification is to be confirmed with the inspectorate (request R-050).

**Procedure and time.** For files lodged since 22 October 2024 under the Green Industry law, the regional authority describes two paths [A4-DRIEAT-AENV-2024]:

### 4.3.3 TGAP (General Tax on Polluting Activities)

| Treatment of non-hazardous waste | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Landfill, EUR/t | 69 | 73 | 77 | 81 | 85 | [CIBS-TGAP-2026] |
| Thermal treatment, energy performance 65 % to 100 %, EUR/t | 16 | 17 | 18 | 19 | 20 | [CIBS-TGAP-2026] |
| Thermal treatment, performance below 65 %, EUR/t | 29 | 33 | 37 | 41 | 45 | [CIBS-TGAP-2026] |
| Efficient sorting residues, received in a plant of at least 70 % performance, under the conditions below, EUR/t | 8 | 8.5 | 9 | 9.5 | 10 | [CIBS-TGAP-2026] |

Rates are indexed on inflation from 2027 [CIBS-TGAP-2026]. A municipal surcharge of up to 2 EUR/t is possible [CIBS-TGAP-2026].

**What is taxed and what is exempt.** The tax is due on waste received for thermal treatment in an installation authorised for the thermal treatment of waste [A4-CIBS-L433-74-89-2026]. A plant authorised for co-incineration is exempt for non-hazardous waste [A4-CIBS-L433-74-89-2026]. Waste is also exempt when it is transformed into a fuel intended to cease being a waste or to be used in a co-incineration plant, and, for hydrocarbons, when it undergoes a thermal treatment without combustion [A4-CIBS-L433-74-89-2026]. The reduced rate for sorting residues requires a residue of a sorting operation on separately collected waste, aimed at material recovery, and meeting performance criteria set by order [A4-CIBS-L433-74-89-2026].

**For the project.** The tax raises the cost of the alternatives (landfill and incineration), which supports the gate fee an Urban Rig plant can charge. An Urban Rig authorised under heading 2771 [hyp:D-052] would, in the base case, receive waste in an installation authorised for thermal treatment; whether one of the exemptions applies, and at which rate, is not settled (request R-050). In the model, the tax paid by the plant is a switch, off by default.

## 4.7 Key Success Factors

**To be decided by the sponsor.** The sources read point to the conditions below; their ranking and the plan's answer are for the sponsor to set.

- A classification agreed early with the inspectorate: 2771 probable, 3520 by prudence, 3532 to be examined for each pre-treatment operation [hyp:D-052].
- Waste supply contracts or letters of intent with a treatment syndicate or sorting operators, for at least the annual feed of one machine, with composition analyses (Section 4.4.1).
- Buyers authorised to receive the oil and the char as waste (Section 4.3.1 and Annex 3).
- Emissions within the levels of the best available techniques for incineration (Annex 2).
- Independent test results on French feeds (Annexes 5 and 7).

## 4.8 Deployment Phasing (France)

**To be decided by the sponsor.** No phasing has been decided. The minimum review time of the permit (Section 4.3.2) and the manufacturing lead time of the machine (Annex 9) are the known constraints.
