Annex 4. Country Study: France

This annex sets out the French context for an Urban Rig plant: the waste available (national and Ile-de-France), the rules that apply (waste law, environmental permitting, tax), the market (gate fees, outlets for the products), the competition and the main actors. The scope is France onlyD-017.

How to Read This Annex

Each figure or fact is followed by a small link to its evidence sheet. Sheets numbered 4.x belong to this annex; sheets numbered 3.x, 5.x, 7.x, 8.x and 9.x are those of other annexes. All sheets are listed in the index of evidence sheets.

Legal texts. The permitting and tax rules are read from the official texts or from documents of the administration. Their application to an Urban Rig plant has not been confirmed by the authorities. Where this annex says how a rule would apply, it is an assumption of the business plan, stated as such, until the regional inspectorate gives its view (request R-050).

Revision. This version was revised after an independent check of the annex. The changes are listed, point by point, in the project's register of discrepancies for Annex 4.

Key Messages

  • The waste exists, and capacity in Ile-de-France is tight. Ile-de-France collected 3.32 Mt of residual household waste in 2022 and sends 94 % of its residual household and similar waste to 18 incinerators5.P.a. These incinerators burned 3,810,761 t in 2022, 93 % of their technical capacity8.P.f. The Syctom diverted about 150,000 t to outside plants in 20258.L.o.
  • One machine needs a small share of this waste. A URC-2000 at 140 t per day would treat about 1.5 % of the residual household waste of Ile-de-France5.C.a, or 2.3 % of the waste landfilled in the region4.C.a. The volume is not the constraint; the constraint is a supply contract for a stable feed of suitable quality, at an acceptable distance (request R-051).
  • French law pushes waste away from landfill. It aims to halve non-hazardous waste sent to landfill by 2025 compared with 2010, and to recover energy from at least 70 % of the waste that cannot be recycled4.R.a.
  • An Urban Rig plant needs an environmental permit. Thermal treatment of non-hazardous waste, which includes pyrolysis, falls under heading 2771 of the classified installations, under the authorisation regime4.R.b4.R.c.
  • It may also fall under the Industrial Emissions Directive. At 5.8 to 8.3 t per hour, the plant is above the 3 t per hour threshold of heading 35204.C.b4.C.c4.R.b. The plan assumes, by prudence, that it does, and budgets the best available techniques for incinerationD-052; this is to be confirmed (R-050).
  • The permit takes time. After a complete file is lodged, the review takes at least about 5.7 months with parallel consultation4.C.d; the time to prepare the file is not included.
  • Published reference gate fees: 109.4 and 120 EUR per tonne. The Syctom charges its member authorities 109.4 EUR/t for residual household waste in 20268.L.o; the average price in French energy-from-waste plants was 120 EUR/t for private clients in 20238.L.m. These are reference points, not a price offered to Urban Rig; the gate fee is an input of the model, to be confirmed by offers on the same stream and territory.
  • The landfill tax rises; the incineration tax is lower. The tax on landfill of non-hazardous waste goes from 69 EUR/t in 2026 to 85 EUR/t in 2030; the tax on efficient thermal treatment goes from 16 to 20 EUR/t3.R.v. These rates set the cost of the alternatives. What the Urban Rig plant itself would pay depends on its classification, its feeds and the exemptions of the law (Section 4.3.3, request R-050).
  • In the base case, oil from mixed waste remains a waste. The French end-of-waste order for pyrolysis oil applies only to sorted plastic waste sent to a steam cracker3.R.b. Another legal route, for a product made from waste that is similar to the product made without waste, exists in the Environmental Code but has not been examined for this oil3.R.g (request R-050).

4.3 Regulatory Framework

4.3.1 Waste Law and the Waste Hierarchy

European framework. The Waste Framework Directive sets the order of priority: prevention, preparing for re-use, recycling, other recovery (notably energy recovery), disposal1.R.a. Recycling does not include energy recovery or the reprocessing into materials to be used as fuels1.R.a. An Urban Rig plant whose oil is burned as fuel is therefore "other recovery", not recycling.

French objectives. They are set by the Environmental Code, as amended by the AGEC law of 20204.R.a.

Objective Target Evidence
Non-hazardous, non-inert waste sent to material recovery 55 % in 2020, 65 % in 2025 4.R.a
Household waste prepared for re-use or recycled 55 % in 2025, 60 % in 2030, 65 % in 2035 4.R.a
Non-hazardous, non-inert waste admitted to landfill, compared with 2010 minus 30 % in 2020, minus 50 % in 2025 4.R.a
Household waste admitted to landfill 10 % of household waste produced, in 2035 4.R.a
Energy recovery of waste that cannot be recovered as material at least 70 % by 2025, notably through solid recovered fuel 4.R.a

End of waste status. French law sets the conditions for a waste to cease being a waste3.R.g. For pyrolysis oil, the order of 19 February 2024 opens this route only for listed plastic waste with at least 85 % of PE, PP and PS, and for use in a steam cracker3.R.b. Mixed municipal waste is not on the list3.R.b. No French end-of-waste criteria exist for a char or a solid recovered fuel3.R.t. The Code also provides that a product made in a plant using waste is not a waste if it is similar to the product made without waste and meets the four conditions3.R.g; this route has not been examined for the oil or the char. In the base case, the oil and the char of an Urban Rig fed with household or business waste are sold as waste, to buyers authorised to receive them (Annex 3).

Solid recovered fuel. The order of 23 May 2016 requires a net calorific value of at least 12,000 kJ/kg and limits chlorine to 15,000 mg/kg of dry matter2.4.l. An order of 1 December 2025 eased several rules of this sector4.L.a. These rules matter if the char is sold as a recovered fuel; whether a pyrolysis char can be prepared as one has not been checked (request R-050). The third national low-carbon strategy plans the energy recovery of 4 Mt of solid recovered fuel4.L.a; this is a policy target, not a demonstrated outlet for the char.

The brochure prints no unit in its radius column4.R.b.

Pyrolysis is thermal treatment. The ministry's note on the waste headings defines thermal treatment as waste brought to a temperature above 180 °C, and includes thermochemical processes such as pyrolysis, gasification and plasma4.R.c. Heading 3520 corresponds to headings 2770, 2771 and 29714.R.c.

Is the plant an incineration plant under the IED? The directive includes pyrolysis in incineration if the substances it produces are then incinerated1.R.b. Since its amendment of 2024, the incineration chapter does not apply to a pyrolysis plant if the gases or liquids it produces are treated before being burned so that they emit less than the least polluting fuel that could be burned in the installation1.R.c. Its transposition into French law has not been checked. According to OneWorld design data, the plant burns its own recovered fuel in the boiler and the superheater2.3.d; how the off-gas is routed is not described (Annex 1, request R-025). The oil, if sold as a fuel, is burned elsewhere. The answer is therefore uncertain.

Treatment of non-hazardous waste 2026 2027 2028 2029 2030 Evidence
Landfill, EUR/t 69 73 77 81 85 3.R.v
Thermal treatment, energy performance 65 % to 100 %, EUR/t 16 17 18 19 20 3.R.v
Thermal treatment, performance below 65 %, EUR/t 29 33 37 41 45 3.R.v

Rates are indexed on inflation from 20273.R.v. A municipal surcharge of up to 2 EUR/t is possible3.R.v.

What is taxed and what is exempt. The tax is due on waste received for thermal treatment in an installation authorised for the thermal treatment of waste4.R.e. A plant authorised for co-incineration is exempt for non-hazardous waste4.R.e. Waste is also exempt when it is transformed into a fuel intended to cease being a waste or to be used in a co-incineration plant, and, for hydrocarbons, when it undergoes a thermal treatment without combustion4.R.e. The reduced rate for sorting residues requires a residue of a sorting operation on separately collected waste, aimed at material recovery, and meeting performance criteria set by order4.R.e.

For the project. The tax raises the cost of the alternatives (landfill and incineration), which supports the gate fee an Urban Rig plant can charge. An Urban Rig authorised under heading 2771D-052 would, in the base case, receive waste in an installation authorised for thermal treatment; whether one of the exemptions applies, and at which rate, is not settled (request R-050). In the model, the tax paid by the plant is a switch, off by default.
