This annex sets out what the products of the Urban Rig are worth in France and Europe, who could buy them, under which specifications and legal status, and what the plant pays for the energy it buys. It relies on the measured product properties of Annex 2 and on the process description of Annex 1. Gate fees are covered by feedstock in Annexes 5 to 8.

How much oil the light cut takes. OneWorld design data put the light oil at 10 % of the oil1.C.c. 3.1.3 Regulatory Status

  • The oil is a waste until it meets end-of-waste criteria. French law sets four conditions for a waste to cease being a waste3.R.i. The criteria are set by the minister for the environment3.R.j, under a procedure revised in 20263.R.k. The Code also provides that a product made from waste is not a waste if it is similar to the product made without waste and meets the four conditions3.R.i; this route has not been examined for the oil.
  • End-of-waste for a steam cracker (order of 19 February 2024). It applies to pyrolysis oil used in a steam cracker or a purification unit feeding one3.R.b. The incoming waste must be listed plastic waste with at least 85 % of PE, PP and PS and at most 3 % of PVC3.R.b. Mixed municipal waste is not on the list; separately collected or sorted plastic waste is3.R.b. This route is therefore open only to a plastic-only feed (Annex 8), subject to confirmation with the regional authority.
  • Fuel is not recycling. The order states that producing fuel is not recycling3.R.b, in line with the EU definition1.R.a. In the EU rules on recycled content, the share turned into fuels counts for zero, and the char is excluded3.R.l.
  • REACH. A waste is outside REACH; once it ceases to be a waste, the substance must be registered unless it is identical to a substance already registered and the safety information on that substance is available to the recovery operator3.R.m3.L.h.
  • Certification. Buyers of recycled feedstock use mass balance certification such as ISCC PLUS3.L.i.

3.2 Char

3.2.1 Product Characteristics

The char has a gross calorific value of 32,090 J/g2.2.l, close to petroleum coke (32 GJ/t, net) and above steam coal (26 GJ/t, net)3.P.p. This is the high case of the plan; the prudent values are 24.6 MJ/kg for char from plastic residues2.5.f.1 and 24.55 MJ/kg for a food waste char (Annex 5)3.C.agD-038; the parity values of Section 3.2.4 fall in proportion. 3.4 Surplus Gas and Hydrogen

3.4.1 When There Is a Surplus

On a resin-rich feed, the pyrolysis gas carries 4,299 MJ per tonne of feed2.5.d.10, 2.9 times the heat demand of the burner2.5.d.3. The surplus is 2,793 MJ per tonne3.C.al.

On household and municipal waste, self-sufficiency is not established and no surplus is countedD-029. The surplus gas and the hydrogen option therefore apply only to a plastic-only feedD-031.

3.4.2 Hydrogen in the Gas

Hydrogen is the main component of the pyrolysis gas: 12.7 % by volume of the sample analysed, which is diluted with air2.2.f, and about two thirds of the gas once the air is removed (Annex 2, Section 2.5.1). Because hydrogen is light, it is 11.6 % of the gas by mass3.C.am but 37.2 % of its energy3.C.an.

3.4.3 Status and Price of the Hydrogen

  • Status. Hydrogen made from fossil waste that is not suitable for material recovery is a recycled carbon fuel, not a renewable fuel3.R.aa. The EU method for low-carbon hydrogen excludes recycled carbon fuels3.R.ab. French public support is reserved for hydrogen from water electrolysis3.R.ac. No source establishes that this hydrogen can be sold as "green" or "low-carbon"; it is priced as conventional hydrogen.
  • Price. Conventional hydrogen from natural gas cost a little over 2 EUR/kg in 2025; the HYDRIX market index of "green" hydrogen averaged about 8.2 EUR/kg in 20253.L.ac. The modelled production cost of conventional hydrogen in France was 2.86 EUR/kg in 20243.P.aa.
  • Value per tonne of feed, before purification losses: 27 EUR at 2 EUR/kg3.C.i and 109 EUR at 8.2 EUR/kg3.C.j. The conventional price is the defaultD-031.

3.4.4 Selling the Surplus as Fuel Gas

The simplest alternative is to sell the surplus gas to a neighbouring industrial user. At the price of natural gas for large industrial users in France, 0.0470 EUR/kWh excluding VAT in the first half of 20263.P.ab, the surplus is worth 36.5 EUR per tonne of resin-rich feed3.C.aq. Injection into the gas grid is excluded: the grid accepts less than 2 % hydrogen3.L.ad. Heat bought by French industry cost 48.5 EUR/MWh excluding VAT in 20243.P.ac.
