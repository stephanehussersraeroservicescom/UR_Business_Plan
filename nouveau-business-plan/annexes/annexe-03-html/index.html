Annex 3. Output Markets and Pricing

This annex sets out what the products of the Urban Rig are worth in France and Europe, who could buy them, under which specifications and legal status, and what the plant pays for the energy it buys. It relies on the measured product properties of Annex 2 and on the process description of Annex 1. Gate fees are covered by feedstock in Annexes 5 to 8.

How to Read This Annex

OneWorld, the manufacturer of the Urban Rig, is the sponsor of this business plan (decision D-027). Its documentation is marked "OneWorld design data"; these are the company's own figures and have not been verified independentlyD-027.

Prices are inputs that the operator of the financial model can changeD-030. Each default price is the most recent published value found, shown with its basis: source, date, scope and currency. Prices linked to crude oil can be recalculated for another Brent price, for example 80 or 50 USD per barrel (Section 3.1.6)D-030. The ranges of earlier years are shown so that the operator can judge the default.

Each figure or fact is followed by a small link. It opens the evidence sheet: what the evidence shows, its confidence, the source document and its location, or the calculation. Sheets numbered 3.x belong to this annex; sheets numbered 2.x are those of Annex 2. All sheets are listed in the index of evidence sheets.

A note on 2026 prices. Oil prices are in a period of crisis: the Brent averaged 69.0 USD per barrel in 2025 and 116.8 USD in September 20263.P.a, and 128.53 USD in the week to 9 October 20263.P.b. Defaults based on the latest prices are therefore high; the sensitivity of Section 3.1.6 shows the effect of a lower oil price.

Key Messages

  • The oil is priced by fraction. OneWorld design data split the oil into 80 % gas oil, 10 % light oil and 10 % heavy oil1.C.b1.C.c1.C.d. Each fraction is benchmarked to its fossil equivalent: naphtha, gas oil and low-sulphur fuel oilD-028.
  • At the latest prices, the benchmark value of the oil is 1,121 EUR/t; at a Brent of 80 USD it is 714 EUR/t, and at 50 USD 446 EUR/t3.C.a3.C.b3.C.c. These are benchmarks before any quality discount; the discount is an operator input (Section 3.1.5).
  • As produced from municipal plastics, the oil is an industrial fuel. It does not meet the gas oil or fuel oil specifications without refining, and its chlorine is 2.7 to 10.5 times the limit for use in a steam cracker without purification3.C.d3.C.e. A French end-of-waste order exists for pyrolysis oil sent to a steam cracker, but only for sorted plastic waste3.R.a.
  • The char is a solid fuel for cement kilns. Its energy content of 32,090 J/g2.2.l gives it a value of 123 to 255 EUR/t at parity with petroleum coke or coal3.C.f3.C.g. French cement plants aim to cover 80 % of their energy with alternative fuels by 20303.L.a. Its chlorine content, the decisive acceptance criterion, is still to be measured.
  • Metals are priced at public scrap values: 316.4 EUR/t for shredded steel scrap and 1,723 EUR/t for aluminium scrap, French exports in the first half of 20263.P.c.
  • On a plastic-only feed, the surplus gas can be sold, including its hydrogenD-031. The gas holds about 13 kg of hydrogen per tonne of resin-rich feed3.C.h, worth 27 to 109 EUR per tonne before purification3.C.i3.C.j.
  • No carbon credit is counted. No mechanism open to the plant in France has an approved method for it today (Section 3.5)D-028. The CO2 avoided compared with incineration is shown for information: about 1,973 kgCO2e per tonne of resin-rich feed3.C.k.
  • Purchased energy: electricity at 0.1212 EUR/kWh, excluding VAT3.P.d, or 8.73 EUR per tonne treated3.C.l; fuel for start-up and for household waste feeds, where the gas does not cover the heat demandD-029.

3.1 Pyrolysis Oil

3.1.1 Fractions and Their Outlets

According to OneWorld design data, the continuous plant condenses the vapours in zones heated to 150, 300, 450 and 600 °C2.3.b, and a solenoid valve separates gas oil and heavy oil by vapour temperature1.M.a. For a resin-rich feed, the design balance gives:

Fraction (OneWorld) Share of the oil Outlet stated by OneWorld Benchmark used here
Gas (diesel) oil 80 %1.C.b "Fuel for sale", or electricity sales3.M.a Gas oil, Rotterdam
Heating (light) oil 10 %1.C.c "Sale as heating fuel"3.M.a Naphtha, North-West Europe
Heavy oil 10 %1.C.d "Sale as heating fuel"3.M.a Low-sulphur fuel oil, Rotterdam

Why naphtha for the light fraction. In the Chiba distillations, the light fraction of each oil had a flash point of 16 to 18 °C2.1.e, below the 38 °C minimum of jet fuel2.4.q. It is closer to a naphtha than to a kerosene.

Why the heavy fraction may be worth more. Plastic pyrolysis oils are often waxy. If the heavy fraction proves to be a paraffin wax, crude wax imported into the EU was worth 1,185.3 EUR/t in 20253.P.e. The composition of the heavy fraction has not been analysed (request R-013); the fuel oil benchmark is kept as the default.

The only published precedent of fraction-by-fraction sales is the Sapporo plastic liquefaction plant (data of 2009): light oil sold at the value of imported naphtha, middle oil at 80 to 90 % of the price of A fuel oil, heavy oil at 80 to 90 % of the price of C fuel oil3.L.b. The source is more than eight years old.

Reading.

  • Gas oil and heating oil. The oil fails the flash point, and its sulphur is up to 60 times the gas oil limit3.C.m. It needs refining (stabilisation, hydrotreatment) before it can be sold as a road or heating fuel.
  • Regulated heavy fuel oil. The flash point is also too low for the French heavy fuel oil specification3.R.c.
  • Fuel for a permitted combustion plant. This is the outlet that fits the oil as produced. The plant's permit sets the fuels allowed2.4.j. Below 1 % of halogenated substances, the oil does not trigger the 1,100 °C requirement for waste combustion2.4.i.
  • Steam cracker. Without purification, the chlorine of the municipal oils is 2.7 and 10.5 times the limit3.C.d3.C.e; at the cracker inlet, the industrial threshold is 3 ppm2.4.e. Nitrogen, oxygen and metals, also limited, have not been measured (R-035).
  • Jet fuel. No ASTM D7566 annex approves pyrolysis oil from plastics3.R.d. Recycled carbon aviation fuels must save at least 70 % of emissions3.R.e. This outlet is not open in the short term.

3.1.3 Regulatory Status

  • The oil is a waste until it meets end-of-waste criteria. French law sets four conditions for a waste to cease being a waste3.R.f. Criteria are set by ministerial order, or case by case3.R.g3.R.h.
  • End-of-waste for a steam cracker (order of 19 February 2024). It applies to pyrolysis oil used in a steam cracker or a purification unit feeding one3.R.a. The incoming waste must be listed plastic waste with at least 85 % of PE, PP and PS and at most 3 % of PVC3.R.a. Mixed municipal waste is not on the list; separately collected or sorted plastic waste is3.R.a. This route is therefore open only to a plastic-only feed (Annex 8), subject to confirmation with the regional authority.
  • Fuel is not recycling. The order states that producing fuel is not recycling3.R.a, in line with the EU definition1.R.a. In the EU rules on recycled content, the share turned into fuels counts for zero, and the char is excluded3.R.i.
  • REACH. A waste is outside REACH; once it ceases to be a waste, the substance must be registered unless it is identical to a substance already registered3.R.j3.L.c.
  • Certification. Buyers of recycled feedstock use mass balance certification such as ISCC PLUS3.L.d.

3.2 Char

3.2.1 Product Characteristics

The char has a gross calorific value of 32,090 J/g2.2.l, close to petroleum coke (32 GJ/t, net) and above steam coal (26 GJ/t, net)3.P.n. Its heavy metals were measured on two municipal runs: cadmium 31 and 36 mg/kg, lead 140 and 42 mg/kg, mercury 0.06 and below 0.02 mg/kg2.2.b. Its chlorine, sulphur and ash have not been measured (requests R-012 and R-034).

OneWorld design data describe the char as a high-calorie powder coal mixed with heavy oil3.M.a and as a coal substitute2.3.a.

3.2.2 Outlets

Cement kilns (main outlet). France has 25 cement plants3.L.a. Alternative fuels covered 44 % of their energy in 2021, with targets of 80 % in 2030 and 85 % in 20503.L.a. Waste fuels used rose from 964 kt in 2015 to 1,054 kt in 2021, with 1,528 kt targeted for 20303.L.a. Petroleum coke, which the char would replace, is the main fossil fuel of the sector3.L.a. One plant, Airvault, plans up to 150,000 t per year of solid recovered fuel3.L.m.

3.4 Surplus Gas and Hydrogen

3.4.1 When There Is a Surplus

On a resin-rich feed, the pyrolysis gas carries 4,299 MJ per tonne of feed2.5.d.8, 2.9 times the heat demand of the burner2.5.d.2. The surplus is 2,793 MJ per tonne3.C.ad.

On household and municipal waste, self-sufficiency is not established and no surplus is countedD-029. The surplus gas and the hydrogen option therefore apply only to a plastic-only feedD-031.

3.4.2 Hydrogen in the Gas

Hydrogen is the main component of the pyrolysis gas: 12.7 % by volume of the sample analysed, which is diluted with air2.2.f, and about two thirds of the gas once the air is removed (Annex 2, Section 2.5.1). Because hydrogen is light, it is 11.6 % of the gas by mass3.C.ae but 37.2 % of its energy3.C.af.

Per tonne of resin-rich feed Value
Hydrogen in the gas, before purification 13.3 kg3.C.h
Gas energy left if all the hydrogen is removed 2,702 MJ3.C.ag
Burner demand covered by the remaining gas 1.8 times3.C.ah

The plant therefore stays self-sufficient in heat if it extracts its hydrogen, on this feed3.C.ah.

Purification. Pressure swing adsorption is the usual technique. A review reports 99.9913 % purity with 75.5 % recovery on a gas with 69 % hydrogen, and 80 to 95 % recovery in general (secondary source)3.L.u. A manufacturer states purities up to 99.9999 %3.L.v. The pyrolysis gas also contains C2 and C3 hydrocarbons and carbon monoxide2.2.f, which the purification must remove. The cost of a unit was not found; it belongs to Annex 9.

3.4.3 Status and Price of the Hydrogen

  • Status. Hydrogen made from fossil waste that is not suitable for material recovery is a recycled carbon fuel, not a renewable fuel3.R.v. The EU method for low-carbon hydrogen excludes recycled carbon fuels3.R.w. French public support is reserved for hydrogen from water electrolysis3.R.x. No source establishes that this hydrogen can be sold as "green" or "low-carbon"; it is priced as conventional hydrogen.
  • Price. Conventional hydrogen from natural gas cost a little over 2 EUR/kg in 2025; the HYDRIX market index of "green" hydrogen averaged about 8.2 EUR/kg in 20253.L.w. The modelled production cost of conventional hydrogen in France was 2.86 EUR/kg in 20243.P.z.
  • Value per tonne of feed, before purification losses: 27 EUR at 2 EUR/kg3.C.i and 109 EUR at 8.2 EUR/kg3.C.j. The conventional price is the defaultD-031.

3.4.4 Selling the Surplus as Fuel Gas

The simplest alternative is to sell the surplus gas to a neighbouring industrial user. At the price of natural gas for large industrial users in France, 0.0470 EUR/kWh excluding VAT in the first half of 20263.P.aa, the surplus is worth 36.5 EUR per tonne of resin-rich feed3.C.ai. Injection into the gas grid is excluded: the grid accepts less than 2 % hydrogen3.L.x. Heat bought by French industry cost 48.5 EUR/MWh excluding VAT in 20243.P.ab.
