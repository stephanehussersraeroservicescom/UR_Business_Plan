Evidence 2.5.c.9

Calculation · Pyrolysis gas without air and its calorific value

What this calculation gives

Somme des huit composants du gaz de pyrolyse hors oxygene et azote (air), % vol

Result: 19.7 % vol

Confidence

95 %

This is a direct calculation on the inputs below; its confidence is that of its least reliable input, capped at 95 %.

How it was calculated

The gas analysis (2.2.f) was taken at the outlet of the unit, where the pyrolysis gas mixes with air. Oxygen and nitrogen do not come from the plastic; they come from the air. Following decision D-021 (sheet 2.5.0), the pyrolysis gas is taken as the other eight components, from hydrogen to carbon dioxide, rescaled to 100 %.

The calculation then runs in four steps:

  1. Water vaporisation. Heat needed to vaporise the water formed (2.5.c.1), from the NIST heats of formation of liquid and gaseous water (2.4.m).
  2. Net value of each gas. Heat of combustion per mole minus the vaporisation of the water it forms. For hydrogen, the net value is the heat of formation of water vapour; for carbon monoxide, the difference between the heats of formation of CO2 and CO. Carbon dioxide does not burn (net value 0).
  3. Weighting. Each net value is weighted by the share of the gas in the analysis. The sum is divided by the average molar mass of the mixture, carbon dioxide included, to give the net calorific value per kilogram of pyrolysis gas.
  4. Use. This value is applied to the gas mass of each run or design case (the sheets 2.5.d.x).

Note: the molar shares in the second table of the annex (for example hydrogen 64.5 %) are each component's share divided by their sum of 19.7 %, without oxygen and nitrogen; they do not enter the calculation separately, because the weighting already uses the raw shares.
