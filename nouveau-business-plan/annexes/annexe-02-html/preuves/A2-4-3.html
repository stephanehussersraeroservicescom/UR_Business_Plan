Annex 2.4.3

Oil composition by GC-MS: full analysis and jet fuel reading

What was analysed

  • Sample. A liquid sample labelled "drain", analysed by the Osaka Research Institute of Industrial Science and Technology (Izumi Center). The letter is preliminary, undated, and does not identify the machine, the feed or the sampling date 2.2.n.
  • Where "drain" may come from. On the Noda test unit, the condensed oil passes a drain pot before the oil-water separator 2.1.g. Whether the sample comes from such a pot is not stated; it is asked to OneWorld (R-013).
  • Method. The sample was heated to 350 °C and the vapours analysed by GC-MS. 53.7 % of the sample did not evaporate after 5 minutes at 350 °C 2.2.n, so the GC-MS describes the 46.3 % that evaporated 2.5.g.18.
  • Limits stated by the laboratory. Branched compounds are hard to identify, and compounds with similar names may differ by one or two carbon atoms. Only the "probable" compounds will appear in the formal report 2.2.n.
  • Limits of the reading. The shares are peak area shares, not mass shares. The carbon number of each compound is read from its name.

Reading for sustainable aviation fuel (SAF)

What jet fuel requires. A Jet A-1 specification aligned with the AFQRJOS check list sets, among others 2.4.q:

  • 10 % recovered at 205 °C at most, and an end point of 300 °C at most;
  • a flash point of 38 °C at least;
  • a density at 15 °C of 775 to 840 kg/m³ and a freezing point of −47 °C at most;
  • aromatics of 25 % by volume at most, and a net specific energy of 42.80 MJ/kg at least.

Boiling points of reference compounds (NIST):

  • 1-nonene (9 carbon atoms): 145.85 °C 2.5.g.20;
  • 1-undecene (11): 192.85 °C 2.5.g.23;
  • 1-tridecene (13): 227.85 to 233.85 °C 2.5.g.212.5.g.22;
  • n-hexadecane (16): 280.85 °C 2.5.g.19, below the 300 °C end point 2.4.q.

NIST gives no boiling point for 2,4-dimethyl-1-heptene, one possible isomer of the main peak (dimethylheptene) 2.4.o.

What a refiner would have to do (reading by Claude, to be confirmed with a refiner or a SAF producer):

  • Saturate the olefins. Most of these compounds are alkenes (57.59 % of the peak area) 2.5.g.14. Hydrogenation would turn them into branched alkanes and cycloalkanes of the same carbon number.
  • Cut the light end. The 9-carbon compounds sit at the light end of the range. At Chiba, the light fraction of the oils had a flash point of 16 to 18 °C 2.1.e, below the 38 °C minimum 2.4.q.
  • Remove the oxygenated compounds, the silicon compound and the chlorine. Possible esters and alcohols make 15.49 % of the peak area 2.5.g.15. The municipal waste oils contain 1,370 and 5,260 ppm of chlorine 2.5.f.32.5.f.4.
  • Convert or divert the heavy part. 53.7 % of the sample did not evaporate at 350 °C 2.2.n, above the 300 °C end point 2.4.q. Named compounds with more than 16 carbon atoms make 4.92 % of the peak area 2.5.g.11. This part would need cracking or another outlet.
  • Aromatics. None is identified. The aromatic content allowed or required in a SAF blend under ASTM D7566 is not found (standard not read).

How much could become jet fuel (order of magnitude). Taking the peak area shares as mass shares, a working assumption 2.5.0, about 18 % to 27 % of the sample is made of hydrocarbons in the jet fuel carbon range:

  • low estimate, 17.69 %: only the hydrocarbons identified with a high library match, in the 46.3 % of the sample that evaporates at 350 °C 2.5.g.8;
  • high estimate, 27.1 %: all the named hydrocarbons with 8 to 16 carbon atoms 2.5.g.9.

These shares are before treatment (reading by Claude, not quantified): hydrogenation, the removal of the lightest compounds to meet the flash point and the purification would lower them; cracking the heavy part of the sample could raise them. They apply to one unidentified sample and are an indication for discussion with a refiner, not a yield to put in the financial model.

Regulatory status. Under ReFuelEU Aviation, SAF include "recycled carbon aviation fuels", subject to the definition of Directive (EU) 2018/2001, a lifecycle emissions savings threshold and certification 2.4.r. The minimum SAF share is 6 % from 2030, 20 % from 2035 and 70 % from 2050 2.4.r. Whether a plastic pyrolysis route is approved under ASTM D7566, and the threshold of Article 29a(2), are not found (not read).

Conclusion. The sample contains a large share of olefins with 9 to 13 carbon atoms 2.2.n2.5.g.52.5.g.4, a carbon range that boils within the jet fuel range. These compounds are candidates for a jet fuel cut after hydrogenation, fractionation and purification. The analysis does not show that the oil is a SAF: the sample is unidentified, the shares are peak areas, half of the sample is heavier than jet fuel, and the regulatory pathway is not confirmed.
