# 1. Executive Summary

Urban Rig Europe will deploy a patented, continuous superheated-steam pyrolysis system that converts mixed, unsorted municipal and industrial waste into four marketable oil fractions, biochar, and recoverable metals — with zero dioxin emissions. The company is the European operational arm of OneWorld Corporation (Osaka, Japan), which owns and manufactures the technology.

The first deployment targets France's Île-de-France region, where 6.2 million tonnes of waste are generated annually, 55% of which is incinerated or landfilled. EU Circular Economy mandates (65% recycling, max 10% landfilling by 2035) create regulatory urgency that no member state currently meets. Urban Rig addresses this gap by transforming waste management from a cost centre into a revenue-generating operation.

A single URC-2000 plant (200 T/day nominal capacity, 140 T/day actual weight, 46,200 T/year) generates €16.1M in annual revenue from five streams: gate fees (33%), pyrolysis oil (39%), char/biochar (9%), recovered metals (4%), and carbon credits (15%). At steady state, EBITDA reaches €8.9M (55% margin) on total CAPEX of €63.8M. Simple payback is approximately 7 years, with annualised ROI on equity of 17.6%.

The technology is not a concept: it has been tested at industrial scale (Asada Shokai, Noda, Japan, 2022), independently certified for oil quality (NKKK) and emissions (KANSO Technos, 5–50× below French incineration standards), and is operating in the field (Shandong Qufu, China).

*Source: UR-Financial-Model-Restructured-V3.xlsx; Asada Shokai test report; KANSO Technos emissions report; NKKK certification.*

# 3. Market Opportunity

## 3.1 The Global Waste Problem

The World Bank estimates 2.01 billion tonnes of MSW were generated worldwide in 2016, projected to reach 3.4 billion tonnes by 2050. In Europe, Eurostat reports 225 million tonnes in 2021 (513 kg per capita), with only 49.6% recycled or composted, 27.5% incinerated, and 22.9% still landfilled. The EU Circular Economy Package mandates 65% recycling by 2035 and maximum 10% landfilling — targets no member state currently meets.

*Source: World Bank "What a Waste 2.0" (2018); Eurostat Municipal Waste Statistics (2023).*

## 3.2 France — Primary Target Market

France generates 38.5 million tonnes of MSW annually (580 kg per capita, above the EU-27 average of 505 kg). Treatment relies heavily on incineration (38.1%) and landfilling (18.1%), with recycling at only 26.9%. Approximately 21.6 million tonnes per year is either incinerated or landfilled — waste that could be redirected to resource recovery.

French tipping fees provide strong economic incentive: landfilling costs €140–180/tonne, incineration €80–120/tonne. Urban Rig's dual revenue model (gate fees + product sales) fundamentally changes waste economics from pure cost to profit generation.

*Source: ADEME 2022; Ministry of Ecological Transition.*

## 3.3 Île-de-France — Initial Deployment Region

The Île-de-France region (12.3 million population, 18% of France) generates 6.2 million tonnes/year (17,000 tonnes/day), with 37% going to incineration and 18% to landfill. Paris-Charles de Gaulle Airport alone produces an estimated 80,000–100,000 tonnes/year of mixed waste. The region's density, infrastructure constraints, and sustainability pressure make it an ideal first market.

*Source: ORDIF (Île-de-France Regional Waste Observatory); Annex B.*

## 3.5 Competitive Landscape

Conventional incineration generates dioxins, CO₂, and toxic bottom ash (machefer). Material recovery is only 5–10%. Safe incineration plants are prohibitively expensive and large-scale. Current processing relies on tipping fees alone with no resource recovery revenue, making waste management a pure cost centre.

Mechanical recycling is limited: 76% of plastics in French MSW are either not recycled or have very low recyclability. PP has a 90% non-recycling rate, PS 95%, and composite plastics 100%.

Urban Rig's competitive advantage is threefold: it processes unsorted mixed waste (no pre-treatment required), generates multiple revenue streams from outputs (not just tipping fees), and achieves emissions 5–50× below regulatory limits without expensive filtration systems.

*Source: Annex A — Current Status Analysis.*

# 4. Technology

## 4.1 Process Overview

Urban Rig is a patented superheated-steam pyrolysis and carbonization system. Operating continuously at 600°C in a completely anoxic (oxygen-free) reactor environment, it converts mixed, unsorted waste into four marketable oil fractions, industrial-grade charcoal (carbide), and recoverable metals and glass.

![Illustration 2](/home/steph/Documents/cowork/online/supporting-documents/urban-rig/Illustration 2.png){ width=100% }

The core of the system is a horizontal, multi-zone reactor through which waste is continuously conveyed. Three buckets move as one batch through seven temperature zones every 90 minutes, enabling 24-hour continuous throughput of 48 batches (17 units of 3 buckets).

*Source: OneWorld Corporation technical documentation; Urban Rig product brochures.*

## 4.2 Key Process Advantages

**No Sorting Required.** Mixed waste — including mud, non-resin materials, metals, pesticides, and food residue — is loaded directly into transport buckets. The only preparation is one day of storage to reduce surface moisture.

**Handles Any Waste Condition.** The URC-2000 is specifically engineered for carbonizing waste with high moisture content, a challenge that conventional pyrolysis systems cannot handle.

**Processes PVC and All Halogenated Plastics.** The catalytic layer adsorbs chlorine, while steam neutralises hydrochloric acid. Recent testing confirms this extends to all halogenated compounds — including brominated and fluorinated plastics used in fire-retardant applications.

**Precision Automation, Minimal Staffing.** The entire 7-process cycle is automatically operated by microcomputer control. A URC-2000 (200 T/day nominal) operates with approximately 15 staff.

*Source: Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG; UR2000-JP technical specification; Asada Shokai test report.*

## 4.3 Four Marketable Oil Fractions

The 7-zone continuous process separates recovered oil into four distinct fractions by temperature-controlled condensation:

| **Zone** | **Temperature** | **Share** | **Oil Type** | **Calorific Value** | **Primary Uses** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 | 150°C | ~10% | Light naphtha (C5–C8) | 44–46 MJ/kg | Petrochemical feedstock; Industrial solvent |
| Zone 2 | 300°C | ~80% | Gas/diesel oil (C8–C20) | 43–47 MJ/kg | Recycled diesel (NKKK certified); SAF co-processing; Industrial fuel |
| Zone 3 | 450°C | ~10% | Heavy oil (C20+) | 40–45 MJ/kg | Heavy fuel oil; Bitumen/asphalt feedstock |
| Zone 4 | 600°C | Traces | Asphaltenes | ~32 MJ/kg | Solid fuel (carbide); Biochar |

Oil quality certified by NKKK: flashpoint 46°C, sulfur <0.2%, chlorine <100 ppm, calorific value 43.27 MJ/kg — comparable to fossil diesel at 42.6 MJ/kg.

*Source: URC-2000 brochure; Asada Shokai report p.28; NKKK certified test report.*

## 4.4 Environmental Performance

**Zero Dioxin Emissions.** The reactor operates in a completely anoxic state — dioxins and CO₂ are not generated. This is a fundamental process advantage, not a filtration afterthought.

**Emissions Far Below Regulatory Limits:**

| **Pollutant** | **Urban Rig Measured** | **French Incin. Limit** | **Factor Below** |
|---|---|---|---|
| HCl | <1 ppm | 10 mg/m³ | >10× |
| SOx | <1 ppm | 50 mg/m³ | >50× |
| NOx | 15 ppm | 200 mg/m³ | >13× |
| Particulates | <0.002 g/m³ | 10 mg/m³ | >5× |

*Source: KANSO Technos independent exhaust gas analysis report.*

**Optional PRIMO Exhaust Upgrade.** For sensitive urban or airport environments, the PRIMO (PUREM 30.8) exhaust purification module performs DeNOx, DeSOx, dust and odour removal — enabling near-zero atmospheric impact.

**Integrated Water Purification (SEA CORAL).** Ceramic purification system enables closed-loop water management, eliminating wastewater discharge.

**Low Noise and Odour.** Thermal decomposition occurs entirely inside enclosed equipment. Operating noise is comparable to a cooling tower.

## 4.5 Safety

**No Explosion Risk.** No oxygen in the reactor means no risk of explosion or ignition — a fundamental safety advantage over conventional thermal treatment.

**Corrosion Protection.** The catalytic layer, steam neutralisation, and non-oxidising atmosphere protect equipment from all halogenated compounds, ensuring long equipment life.

## 4.6 All Outputs Are Marketable

Every output is a saleable resource: fuel oil (four fractions), industrial-grade charcoal (carbide at ~25 MJ/kg, comparable to coal), and recovered metals in non-oxidised condition. This contrasts with conventional incineration, which produces toxic bottom ash requiring expensive hazardous landfilling. Urban Rig generates no toxic residue.

## 4.7 FORCE 9 Companion Technology

For specialised applications, the FORCE 9 far-infrared catalytic reduction facility can detoxify incinerated ash and carbide containing heavy metals at approximately 400°C. Treated waste becomes usable as fertilisers, soil conditioners, and construction materials.

*Source: Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG.*

# 6. Feedstock Versatility

Urban Rig's anoxic superheated steam process profitably treats waste streams considered difficult or impossible to recycle through conventional means. This opens entire market segments that existing technologies cannot serve.

**Municipal and Household Waste** — General household waste (unsorted), food waste, mixed packaging and composites.

**Industrial and Manufacturing Waste** — Mixed polymer scrap, paint and coating waste, automotive shredder residue (ASR), fibre-reinforced plastics (FRP/GRP), multi-layer packaging (Tetra Pak, coffee pouches).

**Tyres and Rubber** — End-of-life tyres (vulcanised rubber cannot be melted or mechanically recycled). Urban Rig breaks down tyre waste into oil, carbon black, and steel.

**Medical and Sanitary Waste** — Contaminated plastics (less than 5% currently recycled), single-use devices, PPE, pharmaceutical blister packs. Complete thermal sanitisation at high temperature.

**Aviation and Quarantine Waste** — ICAO-regulated aircraft cabin waste requiring sanitisation or incineration. Urban Rig meets sanitation requirements while recovering oil and charcoal.

**Agricultural Waste** — Livestock waste, agricultural film, crop waste, pesticide-contaminated packaging.

**Other Streams** — Wire cord waste (non-oxidised copper recovery), textile waste, electronic waste plastics (brominated casings), mattresses and foam, nappies, marine and fishing waste.

*Source: Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG; UR2000-JP; industry reports.*

# 9. Go-to-Market Strategy

## 9.1 Phase 1 — Pilot Deployment (Years 1–3)

One URC-2000 unit at CDG Airport or a similar high-profile location. 200 T/day nominal capacity. Objectives: prove technology in European regulatory environment, secure ICPE permits, build stakeholder relationships, generate auditable operational data.

Target partners: ADP (Paris Airports), ADEME (grant funding), local waste authority.

## 9.2 Phase 2 — Regional Expansion (Years 3–7)

5–10 units across Île-de-France and major French cities (Lyon, Marseille, Lille, Toulouse). 1,000–2,000 T/day total capacity. Target: 5% of French incineration capacity replacement.

## 9.3 Phase 3 — European Scale-Up (Years 7–15)

Expansion to South Korea, UK, Southern Europe, and other target markets. Each additional URC-2000 replicates the proven operational and financial model. The configurable financial model adapts to local feedstock, pricing, and regulatory conditions.

## 9.4 Engagement Process

Discovery (2–4 weeks) → Joint scenario development (4–8 weeks) → Independent technical validation (2–4 months) → Commercial structuring and financial close → Equipment manufacture and commissioning (12–18 months to operations).

# 10. Risk Analysis

## 10.1 Technology Risk

**Mitigation:** Industrial-scale testing completed (Asada Shokai, 2022). Independent certification of oil quality (NKKK) and emissions (KANSO Technos). Operational plant in China. The URC-2000 is manufactured equipment, not a prototype.

## 10.2 Regulatory Risk

**Mitigation:** Urban Rig emissions are 5–50× below French incineration limits. Classification as pyrolysis (not incineration) may allow more favourable permitting. ICPE dossier preparation and DREAL consultation built into project timeline and CAPEX (€150K allocated).

## 10.3 Market and Pricing Risk

**Mitigation:** Revenue is diversified across five streams — no single stream exceeds 39% of total. Oil pricing modelled at three scenarios (€480–895/T). Gate fees are contractual and predictable. Carbon credit prices are conservative (€50/T CO₂ vs EU ETS trading at €50–80).

## 10.4 Feedstock Supply Risk

**Mitigation:** MSW volumes are large, stable, and municipally contracted. Île-de-France generates 17,000 T/day — one URC-2000 requires only 140 T/day (0.8% of regional supply). Multiple feedstock segments provide diversification.

## 10.5 Operational Risk

**Mitigation:** Automated 7-stage process reduces human error. Minimal staffing requirement (~15 FTE). 330 operating days/year assumption includes 35 days planned maintenance. Contingency provisions built into both CAPEX (5%) and OPEX (2% of CAPEX annually).
