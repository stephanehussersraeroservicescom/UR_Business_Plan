# 3. Project Description

## 3.1 The 200-Ton Continuous Urban Rig Unit

### System Overview
The Valorea facility will feature Urban Rig's **continuous steam-cracking system** with a processing capacity of **200 tons per day** of mixed waste. Unlike batch systems, the continuous configuration allows:
- 24/7 operation with minimal downtime
- Automated waste feeding
- Continuous byproduct extraction
- Reduced labor requirements
- Consistent output quality

### Process Flow
```
Mixed Waste Input → Thermal Processing (600°C, oxygen-free) → Output Separation

Outputs:
├── Liquid Oil (sustainable fuel) - 85% of plastics/organics
├── Synthesis Gas - 15% (used to power the process)
├── Carbon Black - solid residue from pyrolysis
└── Recovered Metals - non-oxidized, high-value
```

## 3.2 Installation Site Near Charles de Gaulle Airport

### Site Requirements

- **Infrastructure access**:

**Proximity to CDG Airport**:
- Access to airport-generated waste (catering, packaging, operations)

**Regional Waste Access**:
- Central location in Île-de-France waste generation zone

**Offtake Logistics**:
- Close to refineries and industrial buyers
- Good transport connections for product distribution
- Access to export terminals if needed

## 3.3 Waste Intake Strategy

### Accepted Waste Types

The Urban Rig system can process a wide variety of waste streams:

#### Municipal Solid Waste (MSW)
- **Mixed household waste** (unsorted)
- **Commercial waste** from businesses and offices
- **Food waste** (including packaging-contaminated organics)

**Composition** (typical MSW):
- Organics/Food: ~47%
- Paper/Cardboard: ~33%
- Plastics: ~20%

#### Plastics (All Types)
- **Clean and dirty plastics**
- **Mixed plastic packaging**
- **Laminated and multi-layer materials** (Tetrapak, chip bags, coffee pods)
- **Films and flexible packaging**

#### Airport-Specific Waste
- **Catering waste**: Food + packaging from aircraft
- **Duty-free and retail packaging**
- **Baggage wrapping films**
- **Airport operations waste**

#### Industrial Waste
- **Manufacturing residues**
- **Packaging waste from logistics operations**
- **End-of-life products (non-hazardous)**

### Waste Exclusions
- Hazardous materials (batteries, electronics with hazardous components)
- Large metal objects (pre-screened)
- Inert materials (concrete, bricks) in large quantities
- Medical/infectious waste (unless pre-treated)

### Intake Capacity and Scheduling
- **Design capacity**: 200 tons/day continuous
- **Annual throughput**: ~73,000 tons/year (assuming 365-day operation with maintenance downtime)
- **Delivery schedule**: Coordinated with waste suppliers to ensure consistent feed

## 3.4 Products and Byproducts

### Primary Output: Sustainable Oil

**Characteristics**:
- Energy-rich liquid hydrocarbons
- Similar to crude oil or diesel precursor
- Can be refined or used directly in industrial boilers
- Heating value: ~40-42 MJ/kg

**Yield**:
- ~85% of plastic content by weight
- ~30-40% of total mixed MSW input (depending on composition)

**Market Applications**:
- Feedstock for refineries
- Industrial fuel for cement kilns, steel production
- Chemical precursor for plastics production (circularity)
- Potential for conversion to biodiesel-equivalent fuels

**Estimated Revenue**: Market-linked to crude oil and fuel prices

---

### Secondary Output: Carbon Black / Biochar

**Characteristics**:
- Solid carbon residue from organics and plastics
- High energy content: ~25-30 MJ/kg (comparable to coal)
- Fine powder or granular form

**Yield**:
- ~15-20% of input weight

**Market Applications**:
- **Fuel**: Substitute for coal in industrial processes
- **Soil amendment**: Biochar for agriculture (improves water retention, carbon sequestration)
- **Activated carbon production**: After treatment, used in filtration
- **Cement additive**: Partial replacement for traditional fuels

**Environmental Benefit**:
- Long-term carbon sequestration when used as biochar
- Displaces fossil carbon (coal) when used as fuel

**Estimated Revenue**: €50-150/ton depending on quality and application

---

### Tertiary Output: Recovered Metals

**Characteristics**:
- Non-oxidized metals (preserved due to oxygen-free process)
- Includes ferrous (steel, iron) and non-ferrous (aluminum, copper)

**Sources**:
- Steel from packaging and containers
- Aluminum from Tetrapak, foil packaging, cans
- Copper from wires and electronics in waste

**Yield**:
- ~5-10% of MSW input (depending on metal content)

**Market Applications**:
- Scrap metal recycling markets
- High value due to non-oxidized state

**Estimated Revenue**: Market rates for scrap metals (~€200-500/ton for mixed metals)

---

### Process Energy: Synthesis Gas

**Characteristics**:
- ~15% of pyrolysis output is gaseous hydrocarbons
- Used directly to fuel the thermal process

**Function**:
- Makes the system largely energy self-sufficient
- External energy (70 kg gas/ton from plastic pyrolysis) supplements during startup or high-moisture feedstock
- Reduces operational costs dramatically

---

### Water Recycling

**System Design**:
- Integrated ceramic filtration system
- Condensation and reuse of process water
- Minimal freshwater consumption

**Discharge**:
- Clean, treated water meeting French discharge standards
- Potential for 100% recycling in closed-loop configuration

## 3.5 Integration with Local Waste Management Systems

### Relationship to Existing Infrastructure

Urban Rig **complements, not replaces**, existing recycling:

| Waste Stream | Traditional Path | With Urban Rig |
|--------------|------------------|----------------|
| Clean recyclables (glass, clean paper, sorted plastics) | → **Recycling facilities** | → **Recycling facilities** (unchanged) |
| Contaminated/mixed plastics | → Incineration or landfill | → **Urban Rig** → Oil/Carbon |
| Mixed organics (food in packaging) | → Incineration or landfill | → **Urban Rig** → Oil/Carbon |
| Residual MSW (post-sorting) | → Incineration or landfill | → **Urban Rig** → Recovery |

**Value Proposition**:
- Reduces dependency on incineration (40-59% of current waste)
- Eliminates landfilling need for processed streams (15-30% currently landfilled)
- Recovers value from waste that current systems reject
- Enables higher overall circular economy performance for the region

### Commercial Model

**Tipping Fees**:
- Charge waste suppliers for accepting their waste (similar to landfills/incinerators)
- Competitive with or below current alternatives
- Provides base revenue stream

**Product Sales**:
- Additional revenue from oil, carbon, and metals
- Market-driven pricing with long-term offtake agreements where possible

**Net Economic Model**:
- Dual revenue streams: tipping fees + product sales
- Lower operational costs due to energy self-sufficiency
- Strong margin potential
