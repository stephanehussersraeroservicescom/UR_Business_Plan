# 1. Problem Statement: The Challenge We're Solving

## 1.1 The Waste Crisis in Île-de-France

The Île-de-France region, home to over 12 million people and Europe's busiest airport hub, generates **millions of tons of mixed waste annually**. Despite France's commitment to environmental leadership and circular economy principles, the current waste management system faces critical challenges:

### Current Waste Volumes
- **Municipal Solid Waste (MSW)**: Several million tons per year in Île-de-France
- **Airport-related waste**: Significant volumes from Charles de Gaulle Airport operations, including:
  - Catering waste from international flights
  - Packaging and logistics materials
  - Aviation industry byproducts
- **Industrial waste**: Manufacturing, logistics, and commercial sectors

### Where Does This Waste Go Today?

According to current data (see [Annex A](../supporting-documents/annex-a-current-status.md) for detailed statistics):

| Waste Type | Recycled | Incinerated | Landfilled |
|------------|----------|-------------|------------|
| Paper & Food (Organics) | ~45% | ~40% | ~15% |
| Plastics & Resins | ~26% | ~59% | ~15% |
| Metals | ~70% | ~20% | ~10% |

**The result**: Over **50% of waste** is still being incinerated or landfilled, representing lost economic value and significant environmental impact.

## 1.2 The Limitations of Current Solutions

France's existing waste management infrastructure, while advanced compared to many countries, faces fundamental limitations:

### 1.2.1 Traditional Recycling: Only Handles Clean, Sorted Materials

**The Reality**:
- Only accepts **clean, pre-sorted** materials
- **Plastics recycling rate**: Just 26% (and dropping to ~21% when accounting for losses)
- **Contaminated materials are rejected** - sent to incineration or landfill
- High logistics costs for collection, sorting, cleaning
- Material quality degrades with each recycling cycle

**What Gets Left Behind**:
- Mixed/dirty plastics: laminated packaging, food-contaminated containers
- Low-grade paper: wet, soiled, or printed with problematic inks
- Composite materials: Tetrapak, multi-layer packaging
- Organic waste mixed with other materials

### 1.2.2 Incineration (Waste-to-Energy): Environmental and Economic Costs

**Current Practice**:
- **40-59% of waste streams** are incinerated
- Reduces volume but produces:
  - Toxic bottom ash (20-30% of input weight)
  - Dioxins and other air pollutants
  - CO₂ emissions without carbon capture
  - **Metals are oxidized and lost** or recovered in degraded form

**The Problem**:
- Does not align with circular economy goals
- Requires complex and expensive pollution control systems
- Permanently destroys materials that could be recovered
- Public resistance to new incineration facilities

### 1.2.3 Landfilling: The Ultimate Dead-End

**Environmental Impact**:
- **Long-term methane emissions** (a greenhouse gas 25x more potent than CO₂)
- Groundwater contamination risks from leachate
- Permanent consumption of valuable land
- **Zero material or energy recovery**

**Economic Reality**:
- Landfill capacity is limited and decreasing
- Tipping fees are rising
- Social opposition to new landfill sites

### 1.2.4 Composting: Limited Scope

**Current Limitations**:
- Only effective for **source-separated organic waste**
- Requires significant space and time (weeks to months)
- Cannot handle contaminated or mixed organic waste
- Emissions of CO₂ and methane during decomposition
- Odor management challenges in urban areas

## 1.3 The Gap: What's Missing in the Current System

### The "Unrecoverable" Waste Stream

There is a **massive gap** in France's waste hierarchy: **30-40% of MSW cannot be effectively processed** by traditional recycling, composting, or beneficial reuse systems.

This includes:
- **Mixed plastics** (dirty, laminated, multi-material)
- **Contaminated organics** (food waste mixed with packaging)
- **Low-grade paper** (wet, printed, composited)
- **Composite packaging** (Tetrapak, coffee pods, chip bags)
- **Residual industrial waste** (mixed production waste)

**Today's "solution" for this waste**: Incineration or landfill.

**The consequence**: Loss of valuable resources, continued environmental harm, and missed economic opportunities.

## 1.4 Growing Regulatory and Environmental Pressure

### 1.4.1 EU and French Policy Mandates

France and the EU have set ambitious targets that current infrastructure cannot meet:

**EU Circular Economy Action Plan**:
- Target: **65% recycling rate** for municipal waste by 2035
- Maximum 10% landfilling by 2035
- Reduction in incineration without energy recovery

**French Waste Policy (Loi AGEC - Anti-Waste Law)**:
- End single-use plastics
- Increase recycling and reuse rates
- Reduce waste generation by 15% by 2030
- Promote circular economy models

**Carbon Neutrality Goals**:
- France aims for carbon neutrality by 2050
- Waste sector emissions must be drastically reduced
- Methane from landfills and CO₂ from incineration must be eliminated or captured

### 1.4.2 The Compliance Gap

**Current recycling rates fall short**:
- Plastics: 26% (target: 50%+ by 2025)
- Organics: 60% composted/digested (but excludes mixed streams)
- Overall MSW recycling: 42.7% (target: 65% by 2035)

**Without new technologies**, France will not meet these targets.

### Strategic Location Benefits

**1. Waste Access**:
- Airport generates diverse, high-volume waste streams
- Access to regional MSW from Île-de-France
- Logistics infrastructure already in place

**2. Visibility and Demonstration**:
- International showcase for France's environmental leadership
- High-profile location attracts stakeholders and investors
- Model for other European airports and cities

**3. Regulatory Alignment**:
- Strong local support for circular economy initiatives
- Existing industrial zoning near airport
- Proximity to policymakers and environmental agencies

**4. Economic Opportunity**:
- Recovered oil can supply local refineries or industrial users
- Carbon and metals have established markets
- Reduces waste transport costs for the region
