## F.1 National Overview

### F.1.1 Waste Generation Statistics

**Total Municipal Solid Waste** (2020)[1]:
- **38.5 million tons/year** (national total)
- **580 kg per capita/year** (vs EU-27 average of 505 kg)[1]
- **Annual growth**: ~0.5-1.0% (declining due to waste prevention policies)[1]

**Population Context**:
- **Total population**: 67.8 million (2023)
- **Urban**: 81% (predominantly urban nation)
- **Île-de-France**: 12.3 million (18% of national population)

---

### F.1.2 Waste Composition (France National Average)

| Component | % of MSW | Tons/Year (millions) | Notes |
|-----------|----------|---------------------|-------|
| **Organic/Food Waste** | 32% | 12.3 | Includes food scraps, garden waste[1] |
| **Paper & Cardboard** | 22% | 8.5 | Declining due to digitalization[1] |
| **Plastics** | 11% | 4.2 | Growing; packaging main source[1] |
| **Glass** | 13% | 5.0 | High per-capita consumption[1] |
| **Metals** | 3% | 1.2 | Aluminum, steel packaging[1] |
| **Wood** | 3% | 1.2 | Furniture, pallets, construction[1] |
| **Textiles** | 3% | 1.2 | Fast fashion increasing volumes[1] |
| **Inerts (rubble, ceramics)** | 4% | 1.5 | Construction-related[1] |
| **Other** | 9% | 3.5 | Electronics, hazardous, misc.[1] |

**Seasonal Variations**[1]:
- **Summer**: +15-20% in tourist areas (Côte d'Azur, Paris)
- **Holiday periods**: +10% food waste (Christmas, New Year)
- **Autumn**: +20% garden waste (leaf collection)

---

### F.1.3 Current Treatment Methods (France, 2020)

| Method | % of Total | Tons/Year (millions) | Trend |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|-------|
| **Recycling (material)** | 26.9% | 10.4 | ↑ Target: 55% by 2025[1] |
| **Composting/Organic Recovery** | 15.8% | 6.1 | ↑ Growing slowly[1] |
| **Incineration with Energy Recovery** | 38.1% | 14.7 | ↔ Stable, controversial[1] |
| **Landfilling** | 18.1% | 7.0 | ↓ Declining; target: <10% by 2035[1] |
| **Other/Unaccounted** | 1.1% | 0.4 | Illegal dumping, lost waste[1] |

**Combined "Valorization" Rate** (Recycling + Composting + Energy Recovery)[1]:
- **80.8%** (2020)
- **Target**: 65% material recycling + composting by 2035 (EU Waste Framework Directive)[2]

**Key Insight**: While France has 80.8% "valorization", only **42.7%** is true material recycling/composting. **38.1% is still incinerated**[1] (destroys materials, generates CO₂).

### F.2.3 Île-de-France Treatment Infrastructure

**Incineration Plants**: 14 facilities[3]
- **Total capacity**: ~2.5 million tons/year
- **Actual throughput**: ~2.3 million tons/year (2019)[3]
- **Energy produced**: 1,200 GWh electricity + district heating[3]
- **Notable facilities**[3]:

- **Trend**: Closure of landfills; Île-de-France sends waste to other regions

**Recycling Centers**[3]:
- **Material sorting centers**: 10 facilities
- **Composting platforms**: 8 sites
- **Capacity**: ~1.8 million tons/year combined

## F.3 Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG) Airport

### F.3.1 Airport Waste Generation

**CDG Annual Passengers** (pre-COVID): 76.2 million (2019)[4]
**CDG Annual Passengers** (2023 recovery): ~65 million[4]

**Estimated Waste Generation**:
- **Total waste**: ~80,000-100,000 tons/year
- **Per passenger**: ~1.2-1.5 kg/passenger (including terminal, aircraft, cargo, operations)

**Breakdown by Source**:

| Source | % of Total | Tons/Year | Composition |
|--------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Aircraft cabin waste** | 40% | 32,000-40,000 | Food trays, packaging, magazines, duty-free[4] |
| **Terminal waste** | 30% | 24,000-30,000 | Retail, food courts, passenger waste[4] |
| **Catering operations** | 15% | 12,000-15,000 | Food prep waste, packaging[4] |
| **Cargo & logistics** | 10% | 8,000-10,000 | Packaging, pallets, damaged goods[4] |
| **Airport operations** | 5% | 4,000-5,000 | Maintenance, offices, grounds[4] |

---

### F.3.2 CDG Waste Challenges

**International Waste Regulations**[5]:
- **ICAO Annex 18**: International aircraft waste must be treated as potentially hazardous
- **EU Regulation 1069/2009**: Animal by-products from international flights must be incinerated or sterilized[5]
- **Result**: Most international aircraft waste **must be incinerated**, cannot be conventionally recycled

**Composition Challenges**:
- **Highly mixed**: Food + packaging + plastics + textiles all combined
- **Contaminated**: Food residues on all materials
- **Multi-lingual labeling**: Difficult to sort
- **International debris**: Diverse waste from global origins

**Current Treatment**[4]:
- **~70-80%** incinerated (including mandatory international waste)
- **~15-20%** recycled (terminal-source separated waste only)
- **~5-10%** composted (limited organics programs)

---

### F.3.3 CDG Area Waste Opportunity

**Geographic Scope**:
- CDG Airport: 3,200 hectares
- Surrounding municipalities (10 km radius):
  - Tremblay-en-France, Roissy-en-France, Gonesse, Le Bourget, Aulnay-sous-Bois, Sevran
  - Combined population: ~200,000

**Total Addressable Waste** (CDG + surrounding area):
- Airport: 80,000-100,000 tons/year
- Municipal (surrounding): 100,000-120,000 tons/year
- Industrial/logistics (Roissy logistics hub): 50,000-80,000 tons/year
- **TOTAL**: ~230,000-300,000 tons/year

**Urban Rig Deployment Scenario**:
- **200 tons/day unit**: Processes 70,000 tons/year
- Could handle: 100% of airport waste + portion of municipal/industrial
- **Multiple units**: 3× 200-ton units could process entire addressable market

## F.4 French Regulatory Framework

### F.4.1 National Waste Policy

**Loi AGEC (Anti-Waste Law for a Circular Economy, 2020)**[6]:

**Key Targets**[6]:
- Reduce waste generation by **15% by 2030** (vs 2010)
- **100% plastic recycling** by 2025 (aspirational; likely to miss)
- End single-use plastics:
  - Plastic bags banned (2016)
  - Single-use plastic tableware banned (2021)
  - Plastic packaging for fruits/vegetables <1.5kg banned (2022)
- Reduce landfilling to **10% by 2035**
- **65% material recycling** by 2035 (EU directive)

**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**[6]:
- 13 EPR streams (packaging, electronics, textiles, etc.)
- Producers fund collection and recycling

---

### F.4.2 ICPE Classification (Environmental Permitting)

**Relevant Classifications for Urban Rig**[7]:
- **2770**: Traitement de déchets (waste treatment)
  - Threshold: >10 tons/day = **Authorization** regime (most stringent)
- **2971**: Installations de méthanisation (if biogas production)
- **2910**: Combustion (if considered thermal treatment)

**Authorization Process**[7]:
1. **Dossier preparation**: 6-9 months
   - Environmental impact study (Étude d'impact)
   - Risk assessment (Étude de dangers)
   - Technical design files
2. **Public inquiry**: 1-2 months
3. **Regulatory review**: 6-9 months (including public inquiry)
4. **Prefectural decree**: Issued if approved
5. **Total timeline**: **12-24 months** typical

**Emission Limits** (indicative, set case-by-case)[7]:
- **Particulates**: <10 mg/Nm³
- **NOx**: <80 mg/Nm³
- **SOx**: <50 mg/Nm³
- **CO**: <50 mg/Nm³
- **Dioxins**: <0.1 ng TEQ/Nm³
- **Heavy metals**: Strict limits (varies by metal)

---

### F.4.3 Air Quality Standards

**Paris Air Quality Regulations**[8]:
- Strict due to recurring air quality issues
- **ZFE (Low Emission Zone)**: Restrictions on vehicle emissions
- Industrial facilities face enhanced scrutiny

**Continuous Emissions Monitoring**[7]:
- Required for waste treatment facilities >3 tons/hour
- Real-time data transmission to DREAL (regional environmental authority)

## F.5 Market Analysis - France

### F.5.1 Waste Treatment Costs (Tipping Fees)

**Île-de-France Average Tipping Fees** (2023)[9]:

| Treatment | €/ton | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| **Landfilling** | 140-180 | Declining capacity increases prices[9] |
| **Incineration (WTE)** | 80-120 | Most common; varies by facility[9] |
| **Sorting/Recycling** | 60-100 | For pre-sorted materials[9] |
| **Composting** | 40-80 | Source-separated organics[9] |

**Taxes**[10]:
- **TGAP (General Tax on Polluting Activities)**:
  - Landfilling: €65/ton (2023)[10]
  - Incineration: €15/ton (2023), rising to €25/ton by 2025[10]
  - Recycling: €0/ton

**Impact**: Economic incentive to avoid landfilling and incineration

---

### F.5.2 Product Markets (France)

**Sustainable Oil/Pyrolysis Oil**[11]:
- **Potential buyers**:
  - TotalEnergies (refineries in Île-de-France)
  - Petroineos (Lavéra refinery)
  - Industrial users (cement: LafargeHolcim, Vicat)
- **Price**: Linked to Brent crude (€60-100/barrel = €400-670/ton)[11]
- **Market size**: France imports ~60 million tons crude oil/year; pyrolysis oil can blend or replace feedstock

**Biochar/Carbon**[11]:
- **Agricultural market**:
  - France: 28 million hectares farmland
  - Organic farming growing (10% of farmland, target: 15% by 2030)
  - Biochar demand: 50,000-100,000 tons/year potential (currently <5,000 tons/year)
  - Price: €200-500/ton (certified biochar)[11]
- **Industrial fuel**:
  - Cement kilns (LafargeHolcim, Vicat, Cementys)
  - Coal replacement: €80-150/ton[11]
- **Activated carbon feedstock**: €300-800/ton[11]

**Recovered Metals**[11]:
- **Scrap market**: Well-established
- **Ferrous**: €200-350/ton
- **Aluminum**: €1,200-1,800/ton
- **Copper**: €7,000-9,000/ton

---

### F.5.3 Carbon Credits (France/EU)

**EU ETS (Emissions Trading System)**[12]:
- **Price**: €80-100/ton CO₂e (2023-2024)
- **Waste sector**: Not currently included, but proposed expansion
- **Potential eligibility**: If EU ETS expands to waste, Urban Rig could generate credits

**Voluntary Carbon Markets**[12]:
- **Price**: €20-50/ton CO₂e (varies by project type, certification)
- **Standards**: Verra (VCS), Gold Standard
- **Biochar credits**: Potential for soil carbon sequestration credits

**Urban Rig CO₂ Savings**:
- **Per ton waste processed**: ~0.35 tons CO₂e avoided (vs incineration)
- **200-ton/day facility**: ~25,000 tons CO₂e/year
- **Potential revenue**: €500,000-€2.5M/year (if credits available)[12]

## F.6 Competition and Alternatives (France)

### F.6.1 Existing Waste-to-Energy Players

**Major Operators**[13]:

**SYCTOM** (Paris metropolitan waste agency):
- Public agency serving 85 municipalities (5.6M people)
- Operates 3 incineration plants
- Ivry-Paris XIII replacement: €1.3 billion investment
- Committed to incineration model (large sunk costs)

**Suez** (private waste management):
- Operates recycling centers, transfer stations
- Some incineration capacity
- Developing "advanced recycling" (chemical recycling) pilots

**Veolia** (private waste management):
- Major player in France
- Focus on traditional recycling + WTE
- Exploring pyrolysis and gasification

---

### F.6.2 Emerging Technologies (France)

**Chemical Recycling / Pyrolysis Competitors**[14]:

**Plastic Energy** (UK-based, operating in France):
- Pyrolysis of plastic waste
- Plant in Limay (Île-de-France region)
- Capacity: ~15,000 tons/year (small)
- Focus: Clean plastic waste only

**Carbios** (French biotech):
- Enzymatic plastic recycling (PET focus)
- Pilot plant operational
- Different technology (biological, not thermal)

**Enerkem** (Canadian):
- Gasification of waste to methanol/ethanol
- Exploring projects in Europe
- Requires pre-sorted waste

**Urban Rig Advantages**:
- **Accepts mixed, unsorted waste** (vs competitors requiring clean feedstock)
- **Continuous operation** (vs batch systems)
- **Energy self-sufficient**
- **Modular** (faster deployment than mega-facilities)

## F.8 Urban Rig Deployment Scenario: CDG Airport Case Study

### F.8.1 Project Specifications

**Capacity**: 200 tons/day (continuous)
**Location**: Industrial zone near CDG Airport (Tremblay-en-France or similar)

**Feedstock Sources**:
- **CDG Airport**: 80,000 tons/year (priority for international waste requiring destruction)
- **Surrounding municipalities**: 40,000-50,000 tons/year (residual MSW)
- **Logistics/industrial**: 20,000-30,000 tons/year
- **Total**: ~140,000 tons/year (200% of single unit capacity → potential for 2 units)

---

### F.8.2 Financial Projections (CDG Case, 200-ton unit)

**Capital Expenditure**:
- Equipment: €10-12M
- Site, civil works: €4-6M
- Permits, engineering: €2-3M
- **Total CAPEX**: **€16-21M**

**Operating Costs** (annual):
- Personnel (15 FTE @ avg €65k loaded): €975k
- Maintenance + UR support: €600k
- Utilities: €250k (minimal due to self-power)
- Insurance, admin, compliance: €450k
- **Total OPEX**: **€2.3M/year**

**Revenue** (annual, steady state - 70,000 tons/year):

| Source | Quantity | Price (€/ton) | Revenue (€M) |
|--------|----------|---------------|--------------|
| **Tipping fees** | 70,000 tons | €100 | €7.0 |
| **Oil sales** | 21,000 tons | €500 | €10.5 |
| **Biochar sales** | 12,000 tons | €150 | €1.8 |
| **Metal sales** | 5,000 tons | €300 | €1.5 |
| **TOTAL** | | | **€20.8M** |

**EBITDA**: €20.8M - €2.3M = **€18.5M/year** (89% margin)

**Simple Payback**: ~1.0-1.3 years (equipment cost/annual EBITDA)

**IRR**: 50-80%+ (exceptionally strong)

**Note**: French labor costs are higher than global average, but dual revenue model (tipping + products) provides strong economics.

---

### F.8.3 Environmental Impact (CDG Case)

**Annual Benefits**:
- **Waste diverted**: 70,000 tons/year from incineration
- **GHG reduction**: 25,000 tons CO₂e/year (vs incineration baseline)[16]
- **Equivalent**: Removing 5,400 cars from roads
- **Material recovery**: 95% (vs 10-15% from incineration)

**Air Quality**:
- **Replaces incineration emissions** from equivalent waste:
  - Avoided: NOx, SOx, particulates, dioxins
- **Urban Rig emissions**: <10% of incineration levels
- **Net benefit**: Significant improvement in local air quality

**Alignment with Goals**:
- **France Loi AGEC**: Reduces incineration, increases circularity[6]
- **EU Circular Economy Action Plan**: Contributes to 65% recycling target[2]
- **Paris Air Quality**: Reduces pollution sources

## F.9 Key Success Factors (France)

### F.9.1 Regulatory
✅ **Strong policy support** (Loi AGEC, EU directives)[6]
✅ **Economic incentives** (TGAP taxes on landfilling/incineration)[10]
⚠️ **ICPE permitting** (12-24 months, requires comprehensive dossier)[7]
⚠️ **Public acceptance** (need for transparent communication)

### F.9.2 Economic
✅ **High tipping fees** (€80-180/ton makes dual revenue model very attractive)[9]
✅ **Strong product markets** (oil, biochar, metals)[11]
✅ **Financing availability** (green finance, ADEME grants)[15]
⚠️ **Labor costs** (higher than global average)

### F.9.3 Market
✅ **Large addressable market** (6.2M tons/year in Île-de-France alone)[3]
✅ **Incineration dependency** (37% creates substitution opportunity)[3]
✅ **Declining landfill capacity** (drives demand for alternatives)[3]
⚠️ **Established incumbents** (Suez, Veolia, SYCTOM have infrastructure and contracts)[13]

## F.10 Recommendations for France Deployment

### Phase 1: Pilot (Years 1-3)
- **1 unit** at CDG or similar high-profile location
- **Capacity**: 200 tons/day
- **Focus**: Prove technology, secure permits, build stakeholder relationships
- **Partners**: ADP (CDG), ADEME (grant), local waste authority

### Phase 2: Regional Expansion (Years 3-7)
- **5-10 units** across Île-de-France and major cities (Lyon, Marseille, Lille, Toulouse)
- **Total capacity**: 1,000-2,000 tons/day
- **Target**: 5% of French incineration capacity

### Phase 3: National Rollout (Years 7-15)
- **50-100 units** nationwide
- **Become standard solution** for residual waste
- **Integration**: With existing waste management systems
