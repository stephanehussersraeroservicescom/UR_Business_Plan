## E.4 The Soiled Plastics Problem

### E.4.1 Impact of Contamination

| Issue | Impact | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| **Food residue contamination** | Renders packaging non-recyclable | [Collecte-Eco](https://www.collecte-eco.fr/emballages-soulies-recyclage-sante/) |
| **Cross-contamination** | One soiled item can ruin entire bale | Citeo |
| **Sorting rejection** | Contaminated items sent to incineration | ADEME |

### E.4.2 Examples of Problematic Items

| Item | Problem | Typical Fate |
|------|---------|--------------|
| **Greasy yogurt pots** | Food residue | Incineration |
| **Pizza boxes (cardboard)** | Oil saturation | Incineration |
| **Food film/wrap** | Multiple contamination | Incineration |
| **Takeaway containers** | Mixed food residue | Incineration |

**Key Insight for Urban Rig**: These contaminated plastics are **perfectly suitable for pyrolysis**:
- Thermal processing at 600°C **destroys organic contaminants**
- No pre-washing or cleaning required
- Full hydrocarbon recovery achieved regardless of contamination

## E.8 MSW vs. Pure Plastics - Comparative Analysis

### E.8.1 Yield Comparison

| Parameter | MSW (30% plastic) | Pure Plastics (PE/PP/PS) | Source |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------------------|--------|
| **Oil yield from plastic fraction** | 72-85% | 95-100% | Annex D |
| **Oil yield from total input** | 21-26% | 72-82% | Calculation |
| **Carbon residue** | 15-25% | 5-15% | Lab tests |
| **Recovered metals** | 3-8% | 0% | Process data |
| **Syngas (self-consumption)** | 10-15% | 8-12% | Lab tests |

### E.8.2 Economic Comparison

| Factor | MSW | Pure Plastics |
|--------|-----|---------------|
| **Gate fee (tipping fee)** | €80-150/ton | €0-50/ton |
| **Feedstock availability** | Unlimited (municipal contracts) | Limited (requires collection) |
| **Pre-treatment required** | Minimal (shredding only) | Sometimes sorting/washing |
| **Oil quality** | Variable (depends on input) | Consistent, high quality |
| **Contaminant risk** | Chlorine, metals possible | Low |
| **Revenue model** | **Dual: gate fees + product sales** | Product sales only |

### E.8.3 Strategic Recommendation

| Strategy Element | Recommendation | Rationale |
|------------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Base feedstock** | MSW (30% plastic) | Guaranteed supply, gate fee revenue |
| **Supplementary** | Pure plastic lots | Higher yields, quality premium |
| **Flexibility** | Adjust mix by market | Optimize for oil price vs gate fees |

## E.9 Regulatory Tailwinds

### E.9.1 Favorable Policy Developments

| Regulation | Impact | Timeline | Source |
|------------|--------|----------|--------|
| **IDF incineration capacity -9%** | Increased demand for alternatives | 2024 | Syctom |
| **IDF landfill capacity -56%** | Major treatment gap | By 2028 | Syctom |
| **EU recycling target 55%** | France must double current rate | By 2030 | EU Directive |
| **PPWR content requirements** | Recycled plastic demand surge | 2026+ | EU PPWR |
| **Loi AGEC single-use ban** | Plastic reduction | Progressive to 2040 | [Loi AGEC](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/loi-anti-gaspillage-economie-circulaire) |

### E.9.2 Market Opportunity Creation

The combination of:
- **Declining incineration/landfill capacity**
- **Increasing plastic waste volumes**
- **Ambitious recycling targets**

Creates a **structural market gap** that technologies like Urban Rig can fill.
