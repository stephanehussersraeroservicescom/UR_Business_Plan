## 1. TARGET REFINERIES FOR URBAN RIG INTEGRATION

### Priority Target: TotalEnergies Gonfreville (Normandy)

**Location**: Gonfreville-l'Orcher, Normandy (150 km from CDG)

**Capacity**: 253,000 barrels/day

**Why This is the PRIME TARGET:**

**Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) Production**:
- Started SAF production in 2024
- Target: 160,000 tons SAF/year by 2025
- €70 million investment to process up to 100% circular economy waste
- **Signed 10-year agreement with Air France-KLM**: 1.5 million tons SAF through 2035

**Critical Need for Circular Economy Feedstocks**:
- SAF is made from "waste and residues from the circular economy"
- Co-processing these feedstocks in existing refinery units
- **Urban Rig pyrolysis oil = perfect circular economy feedstock**

**Direct CDG-Air France Connection**:
- Air France is based at CDG
- Air France needs SAF at CDG for all departing flights
- TotalEnergies Gonfreville supplies this SAF
- **SYNERGY**: Urban Rig at CDG processes waste → oil → Gonfreville → SAF → back to CDG for Air France

**Environmental Ambition**:
- CO₂ reduction: 75-90% over full lifecycle vs fossil fuels
- Part of TotalEnergies' net-zero by 2050 commitment
- Normandy platform has 14 decarbonization projects underway

**Current Technology Gap**:
- Relying on external waste/residue suppliers for feedstock
- No on-site advanced pyrolysis technology
- **Urban Rig fills this gap**: Direct supply of high-quality pyrolysis oil

**URL**: https://totalenergies.com/media/news/press-releases/totalenergies-begins-producing-sustainable-aviation-fuel-its-normandy

## 2. CDG LOCATION ADVANTAGES FOR WASTE COLLECTION

### Geographic & Logistics Advantages

**Multi-Department Coverage**:
- Val-d'Oise: Roissy-en-France, Épiais-lès-Louvres
- Seine-et-Marne: Mauregard, Le Mesnil-Amelot, Mitry-Mory
- Seine-Saint-Denis: Tremblay-en-France
- **Positioned at intersection of 3 departments = access to 3 waste catchment areas**

**Major Transportation Infrastructure**:
- **A1 Autoroute**: Major logistics axis for Île-de-France region
- Direct highway connections to Paris (25 km), Normandy refineries (150 km)
- Rail connections: TGV station on-site
- **1.9 million tonnes cargo handled (2024)** - massive logistics infrastructure already in place

**Proximity to Waste Sources**:
- Central Île-de-France location (12.3 million population)
- 25 km from Paris city center
- Direct access to northern Paris suburbs (high waste generation)

### Airport-Specific Waste Streams

**CDG Airport Waste Generation**:
- **80,000-100,000 tons/year total waste**
- **76.2 million passengers** (2019 pre-COVID), 65 million (2023 recovery)

**Waste Composition by Source**:

| Source | % | Tons/Year | Characteristics |
|--------|---|-----------|-----------------|
| **Aircraft cabin** | 40% | 32,000-40,000 | Meal trays, packaging, duty-free, magazines, mixed plastics |
| **Terminal operations** | 30% | 24,000-30,000 | Food courts, retail, passenger waste, high plastic content |
| **Catering operations** | 15% | 12,000-15,000 | Food prep waste + packaging (often contaminated) |
| **Cargo & logistics** | 10% | 8,000-10,000 | Packaging, pallets, damaged goods, EPS, films |
| **Airport operations** | 5% | 4,000-5,000 | Maintenance, offices, grounds keeping |

**Key Advantage**:
- **International flight waste = complex mixed waste stream** ideal for Urban Rig
- Cannot be recycled conventionally due to:
  - Food contamination
  - Mixed materials (paper + plastic laminates)
  - International sanitary regulations (must be destroyed, not recycled)

**Aéroports de Paris (ADP) Environmental Goals**:
- Net-zero emissions target
- Circular economy commitments
- **Strategic partner potential**: ADP could co-invest in Urban Rig facility

### Healthcare/Medical Waste (DASRI)

**DASRI Context**:
- DASRI = Déchets d'Activités de Soins à Risques Infectieux (Infectious Healthcare Waste)
- Highly regulated in France
- Requires specialized treatment (typically incineration)

**Val d'Oise Medical Infrastructure**:
- Multiple hospitals and clinics in Val d'Oise department
- ABARIS: Specialized DASRI collection company operating in Val d'Oise since 1993
- Séché Healthcare, Ecodas: National DASRI processors

**DASRI Treatment Process**:
- Collection → Disinfection/sterilization → Incineration → Disposal
- **Current method: 100% incineration** (CO₂ intensive, no material recovery)

**Urban Rig Opportunity for DASRI**:
- **Pre-sterilized DASRI** (after autoclave treatment) could be processed by Urban Rig
- Convert post-treatment medical waste (mostly plastics: IV bags, syringes, PPE) into valuable oil
- **Challenges**:
  - Regulatory approval required (strict DASRI regulations)
  - Must demonstrate equivalent or better treatment than incineration
  - Potential concerns about residual contamination
- **Advantage**:
  - Material recovery vs. complete destruction
  - Lower CO₂ emissions vs. incineration
  - Hospitals seeking circular economy solutions for non-infectious medical waste

**Estimated DASRI Volume (Val d'Oise region)**: 5,000-10,000 tons/year (rough estimate for catchment area)

**NOTE**: DASRI integration requires additional regulatory work and may be Phase 2 opportunity after proving MSW/commercial waste processing.

### Composite Waste Sources

**Wind Turbine Blades**:
- France: 20 GW installed wind capacity (2023)
- First-generation blades reaching end-of-life (2020-2030)
- **Estimated blade waste: 50,000-100,000 tons/year by 2030** (France-wide)
- Current disposal: 90% landfilled
- **Problem**: No satisfactory recycling solution exists
- **CDG advantage**: Central location for collecting blades from northern France wind farms

**Automotive & Aerospace Composites**:
- Carbon fiber panels, fiberglass components
- Aerospace industry concentration in Île-de-France (Airbus, Safran, Thales)
- Automotive manufacturing waste (Renault, PSA/Stellantis nearby)

**EPS & Expanded Foam Waste**:
- Massive volumes from logistics operations around CDG
- Packaging for electronics, appliances, pharmaceuticals
- Currently landfilled or incinerated (very low density, high transport cost)
- **Urban Rig advantage**: Accepts low-density EPS, converts to high-value oil

**Estimated Composite Waste (Regional)**: 10,000-20,000 tons/year accessible from CDG location

### Multi-Stream Revenue Optimization

**CDG Site Enables Diversified Feedstock Strategy**:

| Waste Stream | Volume (tons/year) | Gate Fee Potential | Technical Challenge | Strategic Value |
|--------------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **MSW (Île-de-France)** | 50,000-70,000 | €80-120/ton | Low (proven) | Base load, stable revenue |
| **Airport waste** | 20,000-30,000 | €100-150/ton | Low | High-visibility, ADP partnership |
| **Industrial/Logistics** | 30,000-50,000 | €80-120/ton | Low | Packaging-rich (high plastic yield) |
| **DASRI (post-sterilized)** | 5,000-10,000 | €150-250/ton | High (regulatory) | Premium pricing, differentiation |
| **Composites (wind blades)** | 10,000-20,000 | €100-150/ton | Medium (size reduction) | Unique market, no competition |
| **EPS/foams** | 5,000-10,000 | €80-120/ton | Low | High oil yield, logistics synergy |

**Total Addressable Waste (CDG Catchment)**: 120,000-200,000 tons/year

**Equipment Sizing Rationale**:
- **2 × 100 tons/day units**: 73,000 tons/year combined (allows one unit maintenance while other operates)
- **OR 1 × 200 tons/day unit**: 73,000 tons/year (single larger unit, higher efficiency, lower CAPEX)

**Operational Flexibility**:
- Multiple waste streams = resilience to market changes
- Can optimize feedstock mix based on:
  - Gate fees offered
  - Product oil quality desired (plastic-rich vs. mixed MSW)
  - Seasonal availability (e.g., wind blade decommissioning campaigns)

## 3. REFINERY INTEGRATION STRATEGY

### Recommended Primary Partnership: TotalEnergies Gonfreville

**Integration Model**: Waste-to-SAF Supply Chain

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    CIRCULAR WASTE-TO-SAF LOOP                    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

CDG Airport + Region                Urban Rig Facility              TotalEnergies Gonfreville         Air France at CDG
─────────────────                   ──────────────────              ─────────────────────────         ─────────────────

• Airport waste (80-100k tons)  →   • 2×100T or 1×200T          →   • Co-processing with              → SAF used in
• Île-de-France MSW             →   • Continuous pyrolysis      →     bio-feedstocks                    Air France flights
• Industrial/logistics waste    →   • 600°C, oxygen-free        →   • Existing refinery units         → departing from CDG
• Composites, EPS               →                               →   • SAF production                  →
                                    ↓
                                 PYROLYSIS OIL                       1.5M tons SAF commitment         Closes loop:
                                 • 20-25k tons/year                  through 2035 (Air France-KLM)    Waste from CDG →
                                 • Circular feedstock                                                  SAF back to CDG
                                 • 75-90% CO₂ reduction
```

**Benefits for TotalEnergies**:
- Secure, local supply of circular economy feedstock
- Meets "waste and residues" requirement for SAF regulations
- Strengthens Air France-KLM partnership (waste from their hub → SAF for their planes)
- Reduces reliance on external feedstock suppliers
- ESG/circular economy storytelling: "Your waste becomes your fuel"

**Benefits for Air France-KLM**:
- Direct connection between operations and SAF supply
- Circular economy credentials ("our airport waste becomes our jet fuel")
- Meets EU SAF mandates (2% by 2025, 6% by 2030, 20% by 2035)
- Passenger communication opportunity: "fly on recycled fuel"

**Benefits for Urban Rig**:
- Guaranteed long-term offtake for pyrolysis oil (linked to Air France 10-year SAF contract)
- Premium pricing potential (SAF feedstock premium vs. industrial fuel)
- Strategic partnership with major integrated oil company
- Technology validation with Tier 1 partner
