# The Problem

The global waste crisis is accelerating. The World Bank estimates 2.01 billion tonnes of MSW were generated worldwide in 2016, projected to reach 3.4 billion tonnes by 2050. In Europe, Eurostat reports 225 million tonnes in 2021 (513 kg per capita), with only 49.6% recycled or composted, 27.5% incinerated, and 22.9% still landfilled.

*Source: World Bank "What a Waste 2.0" (2018); Eurostat Municipal Waste Statistics (2023).*

## The French Context

France generates 38.5 million tonnes of MSW annually (580 kg per capita, above the EU-27 average of 505 kg). Treatment relies heavily on incineration (38.1%) and landfilling (18.1%), with recycling at only 26.9%.

*Source: ADEME 2022; Ministry of Ecological Transition (Ministry of Ecological Transition).*

The Île-de-France region alone produces 6.2 million tonnes per year (17,000 tonnes/day), with 37% going to incineration and 18% to landfill. The EU Circular Economy Package mandates 65% recycling by 2035 and a maximum of 10% landfilling --- targets France is far from meeting.

*Source: ORDIF (Île-de-France Regional Waste Observatory) regional statistics; EU Directive 2018/851.*

## The Limits of Current Solutions

Conventional incineration generates dioxins, CO$_2$, and various atmospheric pollutants. It produces toxic bottom ash (machefer - boiler slag) and fly ash that require controlled landfilling, destroying material value in the process. Material recovery from waste-to-energy incineration is only 5--10%.

Mechanical recycling is limited: 76% of plastics in French MSW are either not recycled or have very low recyclability. PP has a 90% non-recycling rate, PS 95%, and composite plastics 100%. Even PET, the most recycled plastic, sees 34% go to incineration or landfill.

*Source: Annex A --- Current Status Analysis.*

Safe incineration plants are prohibitively expensive and large-scale. Current processing relies on tipping fees alone with no resource recovery revenue, making waste management a pure cost centre.

# The Solution: Urban Rig Technology

Urban Rig is a patented superheated-steam pyrolysis and carbonization system that transforms the waste management paradigm from cost centre to profit centre. Operating in a completely anoxic (oxygen-free) reactor environment, it converts mixed, unsorted waste directly into four marketable oil fractions, industrial-grade charcoal (carbide), and recoverable metals and glass.

*Source: OneWorld Corporation technical documentation; Urban Rig product brochures.*

## Core Process Advantages

### No Sorting Required --- Direct Batch Input

Urban Rig does not require waste to be sorted, cleaned, or pre-treated before processing. Mixed waste --- including mud, non-resin materials, metals, pesticides, and food residue --- is loaded directly into transport buckets. The only preparation is one day of storage to reduce surface moisture. Superheated ultra-fine steam vaporizes and carbonizes all organic content while metals are extracted in a non-oxidized state, preserving their purity.

*Source: OneWorld Corporation, Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG, p.2; Urban Rig ENG, p.2.*

### Handles Any Waste Condition

The system processes muddy, wet, and contaminated feedstock directly. This includes general household waste, food residue with high moisture content, sludge, soil, and agricultural waste. The URC-2000 is specifically engineered for carbonizing waste with high moisture content, a challenge that conventional pyrolysis systems cannot handle.

*Source: UR2000-JP technical specification, p.2; Urban Rig ENG, p.4.*

### Mixed PVC and All Halogenated Plastics

Urban Rig processes multicomponent materials including crimped film, PVC, vinyl, and mixed resins, extracting high-quality oxygen-free recycled oil even from PVC waste. The catalytic layer within the reactor adsorbs chlorine, while steam neutralises hydrochloric acid that would otherwise corrode equipment. Latest testing demonstrates that this capability extends to all halogenated compounds --- including brominated and fluorinated plastics typically used in fire-retardant applications --- making Urban Rig uniquely suited to process waste streams that no other technology can safely handle.

*Source: Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG, p.2 & p.14; Asada Shokai test report, 2022.*

Only the lightest naphtha (boiling point < 150°C) is captured in Zone 1.

## Patented Thermal Transfer System

Urban Rig's patented thermal system delivers superior conductivity, condensation, and heat transfer performance in a highly controllable manner. The ultra-fine particle technology achieves combined spread, convective, and condensation heat transfer that exceeds conventional pyrolysis approaches. This results in finer temperature control and measurably better yields across all feedstock types.

*Source: OneWorld Corporation patented technology.*

## Precision Automation --- Minimal Staffing

The entire 7-process cycle --- drying, heating, carbonization, oil extraction, and three-stage cooling --- is automatically operated by microcomputer control from the loading slot through to the unloading slot. Three buckets move as one batch through seven zones every 90 minutes, enabling 24-hour continuous throughput of 48 batches (17 units of 3 buckets). This level of automation means that a 200 tonne/day plant can operate with minimal personnel, dramatically reducing operational costs compared to conventional waste processing facilities.

*Source: UR2000-JP, p.4 & p.7; Urban Rig ENG, p.4.*

## Safety

### No Explosion Risk

Vaporised oil components are never oxidised because no oxygen is present in the reactor. Consequently, there is no risk of explosion or ignition at any point in the process. This represents a fundamental safety advantage over conventional thermal treatment methods.

*Source: UR2000-JP, p.3.*

### Corrosion Protection for All Halogenated Compounds

The catalytic layer adsorbs chlorine from PVC processing, while steam neutralises hydrochloric acid that would otherwise corrode the reactor. The non-oxidising atmosphere further protects all equipment surfaces. Recent testing confirms this protection extends to all halogenated compounds (chlorinated, brominated, and fluorinated plastics --- including those commonly used in fire-retardant applications), ensuring long equipment life even with challenging feedstock.

*Source: Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG, p.14; UR2000-JP, p.3.*

## All Outputs Are Marketable

Every output from the Urban Rig process is a saleable resource: fuel oil (four fractions), industrial-grade charcoal (carbide), and recovered metals and glass. The carbide has a calorific value of approximately 25 MJ/kg --- comparable to coal (23--30 MJ/kg) --- and can be used as solid fuel, including to power the Urban Rig system itself. Metals are recovered in non-oxidised condition, yielding high-purity copper and other materials for direct recycling.

This contrasts sharply with conventional incineration, which produces toxic bottom ash (machefer - boiler slag) and fly ash requiring expensive hazardous landfilling. Urban Rig generates no such toxic residue --- the entire output stream has positive market value.

*Source: UR2000-JP, p.3; Annex A --- Current Status Analysis; Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG, p.18.*

## Massive Volume Reduction

Urban Rig achieves carbonization reduction to approximately 25% of input volume (from roughly 12 tonnes input to 3 tonnes carbide). Critically, unlike incineration residue, the vast majority of this reduced volume is reusable: carbide serves as high-calorie solid fuel, metals and glass are directly recyclable. Even compared to incineration --- which also reduces volume but produces toxic, non-reusable machefer (boiler slag) --- Urban Rig delivers a far higher proportion of marketable outputs from the same reduction ratio.

*Source: UR2000-JP, p.7; Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG, p.16.*

## Versatility --- Processing Hard-to-Recycle Waste

Urban Rig's anoxic superheated steam process is uniquely capable of safely and profitably treating waste streams that are considered difficult or impossible to recycle through conventional mechanical or chemical means. This versatility opens entire market segments that existing waste technologies cannot serve.

### Municipal & Household Waste

- General household waste (all components, unsorted)
- Food waste and organic residues (high moisture content)
- Mixed packaging and composite materials

### Industrial & Manufacturing Waste

- Industrial plastic scrap --- mixed polymer streams, production off-cuts, contaminated rejects that mechanical recyclers refuse
- Paint and coating waste --- solvent-based paints, lacquers, varnishes, and dried paint residues containing heavy metals and volatile compounds
- Automotive shredder residue (ASR) --- the mixed plastic, rubber, foam, and textile fraction remaining after metal extraction from end-of-life vehicles, typically landfilled due to its complexity
- Fibre-reinforced plastics (FRP/GRP) --- glass-fibre and carbon-fibre composites from aerospace, marine, and construction sectors, which cannot be mechanically recycled
- Multi-layer packaging --- laminated films combining aluminium, PE, and paper (e.g. Tetra Pak, coffee pouches) that defeat conventional recycling

### Tyres & Rubber Waste

End-of-life tyres represent one of the world's most persistent waste challenges: vulcanised rubber cannot be melted or mechanically recycled, and their complex crosslinked structure combined with steel, textile, and chemical additives makes them extremely difficult to process. An estimated 1 billion waste tyres are generated globally each year. Urban Rig's anoxic thermal decomposition breaks down tyre waste into recoverable oil, carbon black (carbide), and steel --- turning an environmental liability into three saleable resources.

### Medical & Sanitary Waste

Healthcare facilities generate vast quantities of contaminated plastics --- less than 5% of which are currently recycled. Urban Rig's high-temperature anoxic process provides complete thermal sanitisation, safely destroying pathogens, pharmaceutical residues, and biological contaminants while recovering oil and charcoal. Applicable waste streams include:

- Single-use medical devices, syringes, IV bags, and tubing
- Surgical gowns, drapes, and disposable PPE
- Pharmaceutical blister packs and contaminated packaging
- Laboratory waste and biological cultures (after autoclaving)

### Sanitation-Regulated Waste (ICAO Category)

International regulations (notably ICAO and EU Regulation 1069/2009) require that aircraft cabin waste from international flights be sanitised or incinerated to prevent the spread of animal and plant diseases. This "catering waste" from aircraft --- along with similar quarantine waste from ports and international logistics --- currently goes straight to high-cost incineration with zero resource recovery. Urban Rig's high-temperature anoxic process meets sanitation requirements while simultaneously recovering valuable oil and charcoal from this waste stream.

### Agricultural & Organic Waste

- Livestock waste --- yielding animal-derived drainage and oil
- Agricultural film and silage wrap (contaminated LDPE that recyclers reject)
- Flower and crop waste
- Pesticide-contaminated containers and packaging

### Other Hard-to-Recycle Streams

- Wire cord waste --- with non-oxidised copper recovery (pure copper)
- Deposition film waste (aluminium/PE composites) --- yielding mineral oil
- Textile waste, synthetic leather, and carpet (mixed-fibre materials that cannot be mechanically separated)
- Electronic waste plastics --- brominated flame-retardant casings from WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment)
- Mattresses and furniture foam --- polyurethane foam with fire retardants, a major landfill contributor
- Nappies and absorbent hygiene products --- mixed plastic, cellulose, and superabsorbent polymers, representing up to 4% of household waste
- Marine and fishing waste --- nets, ropes, and mixed ocean plastics contaminated with salt, biological matter, and UV degradation

*Source: Urban Rig Recycling Solutions ENG, p.18; Urban Rig ENG, p.5; UR2000-JP; Waste Dive healthcare plastics report; EU Horizon Magazine on tyre pyrolysis.*

Treated waste becomes usable as fertilisers, dehydrating agents, soil conditioners, and construction materials. This technology can even treat radioactively contaminated soil.

**By-product value**:
- **Char/biochar** (~41.4 T/day at full capacity, 13,673 T/year): All char sold commercially at EUR 100/T (fuel briquettes) or EUR 350/T (certified agricultural biochar)
  - Annual revenue: €1.4M (briquette scenario, used in base case) to €4.8M (agricultural biochar scenario)

  - Not used for internal heating (off-gas recovery too variable)
  - Energy content helps understand commercial product value

**Rationale**: This approach maximizes product revenue while ensuring reliable, predictable operations. The off-gas and char energy data are valuable for understanding process efficiency and product quality, but are not required for heating supply.
