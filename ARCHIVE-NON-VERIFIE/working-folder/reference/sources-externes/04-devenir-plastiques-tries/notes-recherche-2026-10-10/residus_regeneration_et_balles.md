## 1. Recycler (régénérateur) residues: quantity per tonne of input, composition, destination, cost

### Takeaway
Plant-level survey data (JRC, 2019 plants) put reprocessing yields at 63-90 % for PET, 70-88 % HDPE, 53-85 % PP and only 50-55 % for films, i.e. **10-37 % of rigid input and ~45-50 % of film input leave the recycler as residue**; the only EU-level statement on their fate is "final disposal (incineration or landfill)" or RDF. I found **no French recycler document (ICPE, DREAL, RSE report) that publishes residue tonnages, composition or disposal cost**; the only French cost hooks are regulatory (capped-price priority access to ISDND for residues of "performing" recycling plants, décret 2021-838) and German/Swiss proxies (EBS/cement kiln, ~150 €/t paid by the holder).

**Cost to the residue holder (France, regulatory; Germany/Switzerland proxies)**
- Décret n° 2021-838 du 29 juin 2021: priority admission to ISDND of "déchets et résidus de tri issus d'installations de valorisation de déchets performantes" (covers sorting refus and recycling residues), at a capped price equal to the 12-month average of non-capped prices "pondérée par la quantité de déchets réceptionnés sur la période considérée", excluding intra-group prices, and not below the landfill cost defined in art. 10 of directive 1999/31/CE; performance is evidenced by a third-party accredited attestation. No €/t figure in the text. **[SP]** (law firm blog summarising the decree) — [Landot Avocats, 01/07/2021](https://blog.landot-avocats.net/2021/07/01/isdnd-encadrement-des-prix-plafonnes-applicables-aux-refus-de-tri-et-aux-dechets-du-recyclage/)
- Germany, EBS chain (ALBA background paper, file dated 11/01/2023, latest data cited 2019/2021): the waste holder pays the EBS producer **150 €/t** for household-like commercial waste; the EBS producer pays the cement plant **50 €/t** to take the fuel ("negative price" for the cement plant); EBS 12-18 MJ/kg; **1.16 Mt** of plastic waste replaced fossil fuels as EBS in cement or power plants in Germany in 2019. **[PD]** — [ALBA, Hintergrundpapier Ersatzbrennstoff (230111)](https://www.alba.info/fileadmin/user_upload/Corporate_Site/PDF/hintergrundpapiere/230111_Hintergrundpapier_Ersatzbrennstoff.pdf)

Gate fees for energy recovery are in Annex A4 (not read). **[M]** — [Volk et al. 2021](https://www.itc.kit.edu/downloads/J%20of%20Industrial%20Ecology%20-%202021%20-%20Volk%20-%20Techno%e2%80%90economic%20assessment%20and%20comparison%20of%20different%20plastic%20recycling%20pathways%20.pdf)

### Inferences
- Taking the JRC plant range and PRE averages together, a French rigid-packaging recycler generates roughly 150-350 kg of residue per tonne of bale input, and a household-film recycler roughly 450-500 kg [calcul]. The non-polymer part (moisture, dirt/food, paper labels, glue, washing sludge) cannot be separated from the polymer part (PE/PP labels and caps, multilayers, PVC, fines) with public data.
- Because PE/PP caps and labels float in the PET swim-sink step, a large share of PET-line residue is polyolefin. That fraction is a plausible pyrolysis feedstock, but no source quantifies it.
- The holder's cost benchmark for these residues in France is the incineration/ISDND price (Saumur 292-364 €/t incl. transport; Sarreguemines ~153 €/t, see §3) or CSR. In Germany the benchmark is the ~150 €/t paid to an EBS producer (ALBA). Recyclers' own disposal costs are not published.

### Gaps
- No French recycler (Paprec, Suez, Veolia, Indorama, Plastipak/APPE, Sorepla, Valorplast members) publishes residue tonnage per tonne of input, residue composition, washing-sludge quantity or disposal cost in the documents I read. ICPE inspection reports read (SGT, Paprec Verdun) do not contain the waste tables; these would be in the prefectoral orders (e.g. art. 5.1.7 of SGT's draft) or the annual GEREP waste declarations, which I did not find online.
- No peer-reviewed measurement of PET washing sludge (kg/t, moisture, ash) was found with the searches run.
- The PRE State of Play reports for PET 2024, HDPE/PP 2025 and films 2025 were not found in a readable version with residue destination data. EuPC, Petcore, EPBP and COTREP gave no residue data in the searches run.

## 2. French centres de surtri (Ruffey-lès-Beaune, Épinal, Mende, Thiverval-Grignon): input, outputs, rejects, reject destination

### Takeaway
The four Citeo-funded surtri centres total **100 kt/yr** of plastic input capacity (Ruffey 30 kt, Épinal 25 kt, Mende 15 kt, Thiverval-Grignon 30 kt from end 2026), sorting into up to 7 flows at 95-98 % purity. **No document read gives a reject rate or reject tonnage.** The only documented reject destination is at Ruffey-lès-Beaune: the 2023 prefectoral order authorises an on-site **CSR unit that accepts the surtri centre's "refus de tri"**.

### Cited Findings

**Ruffey-lès-Beaune (21), Bourgogne Recyclage**
- Inaugurated Friday 19/01/2024 (press release dated 22/01/2024): "Sa capacité de sur-tri atteint 30 000 tonnes par an"; 15 optical sorters, 7 plastic categories; investment 26 M€ incl. 20 M€ from Citeo; "95% des pots de yaourts qui entrent dans le centre de surtri de Bourgogne Recyclage sont captés" (capture rate, not a reject rate); company-wide "taux de valorisation de plus de 90% des déchets collectés auprès de ses clients" (not specific to the surtri line). No reject rate, no output tonnages. **[PD]** — [Citeo, CP Inauguration Bourgogne Recyclage](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Citeo_CP-Inauguration-Bourgogne-Recyclage.pdf)

### Inferences
- Surtri inputs are the "flux développement" bales, whose floor price is 0 €/t (§3). Since output purity targets are 95-98 % and Citeo's Observatoire measured flux développement bales at 92 % average purity (background file), surtri rejects are probably at least 5-10 % of input. That would be 5-10 kt/yr across 100 kt of capacity [calcul, unverified assumption]. No source states this.
- At Ruffey, the rejects appear to stay on site and be turned into CSR, which makes them unavailable as merchant feedstock. For the other three sites the destination is undocumented.

### Gaps
- Reject rate, reject tonnage, reject composition and destination for all four centres, and output tonnages per flow. The ICPE annual waste declarations and the prefectoral orders for Épinal (SUEZ), Mende (EMC) and Thiverval-Grignon (Sepur) were not found or read. The Ruffey inspection report found by search ([Géorisques inspection](https://www.georisques.gouv.fr/webappReport/ws/installations/inspection/tWSWL5KnEna3xq4Akz7qY1msnaQpn6H2)) was not read.

## 3. Low-grade bale grades and prices (who pays whom)

### Takeaway
**France:** low-grade household plastic flows (films/"plastiques souples", mix PE/PP/PS, PS, opaque PET, PET trays) have a **contractual floor of 0 €/t** ex-sorting centre under Valorplast, with transport paid by the buyer and loading by the collectivité. Actual monthly prices are published only on the members' e-Valorplast platform. **UK (letsrecycle 2024):** pots-tubs-trays bales went **negative, down to -£50/t (seller pays), from August to December 2024**, and mixed bottles fell to £0-40/t. **Germany:** DKR 350/352/310 absolute prices sit behind the EUWID paywall. The free bvse monthly reports give only monthly changes, e.g. PE commercial mixed film -15 to -17 €/t in July 2025, and say mixed-plastics recycling was "increasingly filling warehouses" (August 2025). **NWE reject bales:** according to ICIS, they turned positive for the first time in 2021 but stayed negative in Scandinavia and the Netherlands. Platts (since 12/03/2025) and ICIS now publish NWE mixed-plastic-waste bale prices, behind paywalls.

### Inferences
- In France, the 0 €/t floor means the collectivité neither earns nor pays for low-grade bales; any market value below zero is absorbed by Valorplast/Citeo's mutualised system, so **the true market price of these grades is hidden from public view**. The UK PTT series is the clearest public evidence that comparable bales traded at negative prices (-£50 to 0/t) in late 2024.
- A process-ready (washed, shredded, ≥ 86 % PO) pyrolysis feed, as specified by Platts, is a different product from a raw DKR 350 or "flux développement" bale. Prices for the former can be positive even when raw mixed bales are at or below zero.
- The weak 2025-2026 recycled-polymer market (bvse reports, the Quantafuel closure in §4) points to downward pressure on low-grade bale values in 2025-2026. No absolute figure supports this.

## 4. Who buys hard-to-recycle mixed plastic bales and residues in Europe, and on what documented terms

### Takeaway
The documented buyers are **energy users** (cement kilns via EBS/CSR, MSW incinerators, CSR boilers), which are **paid by the holder** (Germany: ~150 €/t to the EBS producer, who in turn pays ~50 €/t to the cement plant), and **pyrolysis plants**, whose feedstock contracts (Mura/Geminor/Elite, Quantafuel/Geminor, TotalEnergies/Citeo/Paprec) **do not disclose prices**. The pyrolysis outlet is contracting: Viridor shut Quantafuel's Skive plant in mid-2026, citing non-viability.

### Cited Findings
- **Cement kilns / EBS (Germany):** waste holder pays 150 €/t to the EBS producer; the producer pays the cement plant 50 €/t; 1.16 Mt of plastic waste used as EBS in cement and power plants in 2019. **[PD]** — [ALBA Hintergrundpapier Ersatzbrennstoff](https://www.alba.info/fileadmin/user_upload/Corporate_Site/PDF/hintergrundpapiere/230111_Hintergrundpapier_Ersatzbrennstoff.pdf)
- **Cement kilns / KVA (Switzerland):** non-recycled share of municipal mixed plastics goes "als Brennstoff in Kehrichtverbrennungsanlagen oder Zementfabriken". **[PD]** — [Frenkendorf](https://www.frenkendorf.ch/public/upload/assets/1434/Kunststoffsammlung.pdf?fp=1)
- **On-site CSR (France):** Ruffey-lès-Beaune CSR unit accepts the surtri centre's refus (AP 11/12/2023). **[PD]** — [Géorisques AP n° 1749](https://www.georisques.gouv.fr/webappReport/ws/installations/document/Lft2OgbD9CYowm32AaoRogigWafnhWIr)

### Inferences
- For a hard-to-recycle residue holder in France or Germany, the reference outlet is energy recovery, which the holder pays for (~150 €/t in the German EBS example, ~153 €/t incineration at Sarreguemines, 292-364 €/t at Saumur incl. transport). A new pyrolysis or fuel outlet competes against those prices, and against reduced TGAP for "performing" sorting residues (8 €/t in 2026, background).
- Pyrolysis operators prefer polyolefin-rich, PVC-screened material (ICIS RDF reject spec ≤ 0.5 % PVC; Platts spec PVC ≤ 1 %, PO ≥ 86 %). Raw surtri or recycler residues containing PET, PS, paper, moisture and fines would need pre-treatment before reaching those specs, which reduces any gate fee they could earn.

### Gaps
- No documented price or gate-fee terms for Plastic Energy (Seville/Almería, Grandpuits), Pyrum (tyre-focused), BASF ChemCycling partners, Fuenix, OMV ReOil, Mura or Quantafuel. All feedstock contracts found are silent on price.
- No 2024-2026 public French CSR or cement-kiln gate fee specific to plastic residues (the background file has FNADE 2023 modelled 40-50 €/t and Normandie 2021 10-30 €/t).
