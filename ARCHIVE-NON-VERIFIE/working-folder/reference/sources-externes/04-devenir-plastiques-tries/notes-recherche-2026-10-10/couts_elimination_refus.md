## Consolidated table: disposal cost per tonne by country and route (verified items only)

### Takeaway
Published, checkable absolute gate fees exist for the UK (WRAP, letsrecycle index), for some French public syndicates (Syctom, Haganis/Metz), for some German public plants (Kreis Recklinghausen/AGR, Bremen) and for one Swiss contract (Bülach–ERZ Zürich). Taxes are fully verifiable for FR, NL, Flanders, AT, ES and the UK. France still has **no published national price series for refus de tri, ISDND or CSR**: FEDEREC indices give only year-on-year variations.

### Inferences
- [calc, indicative only] **France, 2026, UVE route for refus de tri:** public tariffs read are 65,66 €/t excl. TGAP (Metz, 2025) to 129,4 €/t (Syctom "anomalies de tri", 2024). TGAP of 8 (performant residues), 16 or 29 €/t is added on top. Fees charged to private holders are higher: AMORCE's 2021 average was 133 €/t for private clients against 76 €/t for public clients (background). Saumur's 364 € HT/t (2023) includes transport and is an outlier.
- **UK:** EfW (£92-130) costs less than landfill plus tax (£146-156). RDF export (£90-140 plus about £15 baling) is roughly at EfW price, so it is a marginal outlet.
- **Germany:** public-plant MVA fees read are about 150-186 €/t including BEHG. BEHG makes plastic-rich residues more expensive: see the Germany section.

### Gaps
- No unit price (€/t) for refus de tri from any BOAMP/TED award notice. The notices found (Valor'Aisne 26-5061, Saint-Étienne 2023.00865) give only annual totals or scoring formulas.
- No absolute gate fees for NL, AT or IT (2023-2026), nor for Walloon taxes after 2016 (the current consolidated text, in force from 22/05/2026, could not be fetched).
- Conversion to € is left to the report writer; no exchange rate was sourced.

## France: prices paid for refus de tri at UVE, ISDND and CSR; TGAP reduced-rate conditions; CSR market 2023-2026

### Takeaway
Two French public tariffs were read: Syctom 2024 charges **129,4 €/t for sorting residues ("anomalies de tri")**, against 109,4 €/t for OMr; Haganis/Metz charges 65,66 €/t plus TGAP for incineration in 2025. FEDEREC's 2026 indices show incineration fees for business waste up **+3,6 to +10,7 €/t** (excl. TGAP and transport) and CSR fees paid to consuming plants up **+4,6 % to +15,7 %**, but give no absolute levels. The 8 €/t TGAP rate needs **PCI ≥ 9 MJ/kg** and residue rates below stream-specific caps (35 % for household packaging), under the arrêté of 20/02/2023.

### Inferences
- A plastic-rich refus from single-stream business plastics sorting qualifies for 8 €/t TGAP only if residues stay ≤ 10 % of inputs. Household packaging refus qualifies at ≤ 35 %, and both need a 12-month average PCI ≥ 9 MJ/kg. Plastic-rich refus usually exceed 9 MJ/kg, but no measured PCI was read here.
- The arrêté of 2023 was made under the old Code des douanes article. Whether it still applies, or has been re-issued, under CIBS L. 433-90 was **not verified**.
- The CSR route is not cheap for the holder. The holder pays production (40-115 €/t across sources, some old) plus a fee to the consuming plant (FNADE model 40-50 €/t, 2023), and that fee rose 4,6-15,7 % in 2025. On these figures the total is comparable to or above UVE tariffs. This is an inference across sources of different years.

### Gaps
- No BOAMP/TED award with a published €/t for refus de tri treatment; the award notices found give only totals.
- Not reached: SIREDOM, SIGIDURS, SIETREM, SMITOM deliberations; any AMORCE UVE price survey newer than DT139 (2021 data); AMORCE or FNADE observed ISDND prices for 2024-2026.
- No observed absolute CSR prices for 2023-2026 (FEDEREC publishes only % changes).
- Syctom 2025/2026 tariffs not found, and the TGAP treatment of Syctom tariffs not stated.
- The Autorité de la concurrence opinion 20-A-09 (2020) is in the background only.

## UK: WRAP Gate Fees Report and market prices

### Takeaway
WRAP 2025/26 (published 3 July 2026): median non-hazardous landfill gate fee **£34/t excluding tax**, plus the **£126.15/t** landfill tax. EfW medians rose in all categories; the latest residual EfW median actually read is **£121/t** (2024/25). ## EU ETS: inclusion of municipal waste incineration (status 2026) and national carbon taxes

### Takeaway
On **17 July 2026** the Commission proposed bringing non-hazardous waste incineration and co-incineration (> 3 t/h) into the EU ETS. Surrender obligations would phase in from **2031**: 25 % in 2031, 50 % in 2032, 75 % in 2033 and 100 % in 2034. Member States could opt out up to reference year 2035 if they meet 2 of 3 conditions. Monitoring and reporting has applied since 2024. Inclusion from 2028 was **not** proposed.

### Cited Findings
- Commission proposal of 17 July 2026 amending Directive 2003/87/EC — [Loyens & Loeff, 1/9/2026](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/european-commission-proposal-extension-of-the-eu-ets-to-waste-incineration/). `[SECONDARY, law firm]`
  - a new Annex I activity for « installations for non-hazardous waste with a capacity exceeding 3 tonnes per hour »; hazardous waste stays excluded;
  - surrender obligations on 25 % of verified emissions for 2031, 50 % for 2032, 75 % for 2033, 100 % from 2034;
  - a Member State may exempt installations up to reference year 2035 if it meets at least two of three conditions: a national carbon tax above the average EU ETS auction price; on track for the municipal recycling targets; on track for the landfill target. The request is due by 31/7/2029;
  - methane monitoring and reporting for landfills.
- Carbon Pulse, 17/7/2026: « bringing municipal waste incineration into the EU Emissions Trading System (ETS) from 2031 », with a delayed phase-in, temporary national opt-outs and support for carbon capture — [Carbon Pulse](https://carbon-pulse.com/?p=532614), paywalled. `[SECONDARY]`
- CEWEP/ESWET event, 26/3/2026 — [slides](https://www.cewep.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/FULL-Slides_CEWEPESWET-Event-WasteEUETS_26-3-26.pdf). `[INDUSTRY]`
  - the revised Directive (OJ 16/5/2023), art. 30(7): the Commission « shall assess the feasibility of including municipal waste incineration installations in the EU ETS »; the 2022 Council text had a 2028 option and MRV from 1/1/2024;
  - municipal waste incineration emissions in 2024: 85 Mt CO2e, of which 39,4 Mt fossil (47 %) and 45,6 Mt biogenic (53 %);
  - CEWEP internal estimate: a 52 €/t average gate-fee increase across the EU27 at 100 €/t ETS (about 4,2 bn €/yr);
  - 9 of 24 countries have incineration and/or CO2 taxes; NL, DK, DE, SE and LT are named as having a carbon cost.
- National carbon costs read: DE BEHG 45 (2024), 55 (2025), 55-65 €/t CO2 (2026), see the Germany section; NL CO2 levy €103,7/t CO2 (2026), see the previous section.

### Inferences
- For a 2026-2030 business plan, the EU ETS does not apply to waste incineration. National CO2 costs do apply now in DE and NL; France has none (only TGAP).
- From 2031 the ETS cost would scale with fossil carbon, so it hits plastic-rich residues hardest. The CEWEP +52 €/t is an average across waste mixes.

### Gaps
- The COM number of the 17/7/2026 proposal and the Commission's own text were not read; the details come from a law-firm summary.
- No complete EU table of national incineration tax rates for 2025-2026 was found (CEWEP and EEA tables not read).
