# Measured composition of sorting residues (refus de tri, LVP-Sortierreste, PMD residue, MRF residuals) — what a tonne actually contains

Research date: 2026-10-10. WebSearch/WebFetch only. All quotes are as returned by the fetch tool from the cited document. Ratings used: **primary measured** (measured with stated method), **primary measured – method undisclosed**, **primary declared** (operator/permit figures or specifications, no sampling protocol), **modelled**, **secondary**. "[calc]" = my arithmetic from cited figures.

**Structural caveat that applies everywhere:** the French yellow bin (collecte sélective, CS) contains paper/cardboard and, in many areas, everything is co-collected. The German/Austrian LVP (Gelber Sack/Gelbe Tonne), Dutch PMD and Belgian P+MD streams exclude paper and glass by design. Residues from those streams are therefore expected to be more plastic-rich than French refus. Spanish yellow-bin rejects are LVP-like (no paper). Québec single-stream is paper-inclusive like France.

## Q1. France — what measured characterisations of refus de tri (household separate collection) exist, and what do they show?

### Takeaway
Only one national composition of French CS refus was found in public sources: the Citeo/OCAPEM 2025 synthesis (12 % identified plastic packaging, 15.7 % fibres, 70.9 % "autres"). Its method for that table is **not described** in the synthesis. The 200 kg / 2 days / 1 centre caveat in the background notes applies to the separate fines test, not to the composition table. A French standard protocol for characterising refus does exist (NF X30-472, 2014), but no published dataset produced under it was found. **No measured PCI, chlorine, moisture, ash or polymer split for French CS refus was found in any public source read.**

### Cited Findings
**OCAPEM 2025 (Citeo), "Amélioration du tri des petits emballages", synthesis dated November 2025**: [OCAPEM-2025](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Synthese-du-Rapport-OCAPEM-Amelioration-du-tri-des-petits-emballages.pdf)
- Table 3 (p. 4), "Composition moyenne des refus de tri en France": Fibreux 15.7 %; Plastiques rigides 7.5 %; Plastiques souples 4.5 %; Acier 0.4 %; Alu 1.2 %; Autres 70.9 % (sum 100.2 %, rounding). "Autres" covers fines, nested items, glass and non-packaging, per the note above the table.
  - **Wording correction:** the fetch tool rendered this as "imbricated materials". That points to French "imbriqués" (nested items), **not "imbrûlés"** (unburnt) as written in the background README. Check against the PDF.
- Table 3 has an "Echantillon" column with the value "4605,7". **The synthesis gives no unit and no method for Table 3**: no sample mass, number of centres, dates, operator or protocol. Rating: **primary measured – method undisclosed**. Representativeness cannot be assessed.
- The only sampling method described is for the **fines** test (§1.1.3, p. 5): "Les caractérisations ont été réalisées sur un centre de tri partenaire de l'étude" and "Les 200 kg ont été prélevé sur deux journées sur un seul centre de tri" (50 mm screen). This corrects the background README, which attached this caveat to the composition table.
- Refus by size class (Figure 2, p. 4): >100 mm 57 %; 50–100 mm 26 %; 20–50 mm 8 %; 0–20 mm 9 %. So about 17 % of refus is below 50 mm [calc].
- "Sur la fraction 20-100, environ 45% des refus ne sont pas valorisables" (p. 4).
- "Les fines représentent environ 4% des entrants d'un centre de tri" (p. 9). National figures: 750,000 t of refus and 125,000 t of fines (p. 9). The text does not say whether the fines are counted inside the 750 kt.
- Capture rate of recyclables by size (Table 4, pp. 5–6): 81.7 % (>100 mm), 67.4 % (50–100), 24.0 % (20–50), 0 % (0–20). The extracted column alignment is uncertain, and the document says unreliable values are highlighted in red (the colour is not visible in the extraction). Quote: "Il n'est pas possible de conclure sur les fractions en petites quantités sans risquer de faire de grosses erreurs" (p. 6).

**AFNOR NF X30-472, "Déchets ménagers et assimilés – Caractérisation des refus de tri"**: [AFNOR norminfo](https://norminfo.afnor.org/norme/nf-x30-472/dechets-menagers-et-assimiles-caracterisation-des-refus-de-tri/104473)
- Published 12 April 2014, confirmed 30 November 2019, next review 12 April 2029.
- Scope (verbatim): "Le présent document propose des règles pour prélever et caractériser de manière régulière, dans les centres de tri, des refus de tri de déchets ménagers et assimilés collectés sélectivement." It excludes residual household waste, biowaste and bulky waste.
- Sample mass and sorting categories are in the paid standard, not on the public page.

**Citeo/ADEME "Étude sur l'organisation du tri" (synthesis, March 2019)**: [CITEO-ADEME-2019](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2021-09/Etude%20tri%20surtri_Synthese_Citeo%20Ademe_mars2019.pdf)
- Contains no refus composition. Figure 2 (p. 7) shows "Indésirables" ("erreur de tri de l'habitant") of 4,000 t in 45,500 t of CS per million inhabitants, about 8.8 % [calc].
- This is a 2022 projection, an "Estimation réalisée à partir des données de l'étude prospective" (footnote 5). Rating: **modelled**.

**Citeo infographic (December 2025)**: [CITEO-INFOG-2025](https://citoyens.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/251218_CITEO_Infog_EMBALLAGES.pdf)
- "24 %" taux de refus de tri en France, with no year or method given. It lists three categories of residue with no percentages: "ERREURS DE TRI DES CITOYENS", "EMBALLAGES TROP PETITS", "EMBALLAGES SANS FILIÈRE DE RECYCLAGE".
- Destination: "Valorisation énergétique (incinération) ou centre d'enfouissement (en minorité)". Rating: **secondary**.

**Citeo CEPOM 2020–2021**: [CITEO-CEPOM](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/20230117_Etude_CEPOM_Citeo.pdf)
- Characterises residual household waste (OMr) and street bins, **not refus**: 304 samples, about 60 t, 12 regions, February 2020 to August 2021, sampled at transfer or treatment sites (pp. 4–5).
- Cited only as a contrast. This is what a well-documented French characterisation looks like, and nothing comparable was found for refus.

### Inferences
- The OCAPEM table is the only public national composition of French CS refus. Without a method it cannot be used as a design basis. The 12 % plastic figure is a **floor for identified plastic packaging only**: the 70.9 % "autres" includes non-packaging items (which can include plastics such as toys or household goods) and nested items (which can include plastics).
- With 57 % of refus above 100 mm and 26 % at 50–100 mm, most refus mass is coarse. Fines below 20 mm (9 %) would be mostly inert and organic and could be screened off before pyrolysis, but no composition of the coarse fraction was found to show how much plastic screening would gain.
- Because a standard protocol (NF X30-472) exists, French sorting-centre operators and Citeo likely hold unpublished data produced under it. Requesting the **full OCAPEM report** (not the synthesis) from Citeo, and NF X30-472 results from target sorting centres, is the fastest route to verifiable data.

### Gaps
- No public measured PCI/LHV, chlorine, moisture, ash or polymer split (PE/PP/PS/PET/PVC/multilayer) for French CS refus.
- The method, sample mass, number of centres and year behind OCAPEM Table 3 are not given in the synthesis. The unit of "4605,7" is unknown.
- No ADEME study characterising CS refus was found. MODECOM data cover OMr, not refus. No SYCTOM, ORDIF or AMORCE refus composition was found; the searches returned tender notices and local press only.
- No French academic thesis with refus composition was retrieved. A DUMAS student thesis was blocked by bot protection: [DUMAS](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-01662804v1/document).

## Q2. France — plastic-rich related streams (bale non-target, surtri rejects, DAE rejects, pulper rejects) and CSR specifications

### Takeaway
French data on plastic-rich side streams are **bale-purity measurements**: well documented (Citeo Observatoire de la qualité: over 1,400 characterisations, over 115 centres) but they describe bales, not rejects. Surtri rejects are named only qualitatively ("PVC/films trop petits"). No measured composition of DAE sorting rejects was found. The CSR permit files and operator presentations give **specifications** (PCI 12–18 MJ/kg as received for boiler CSR, above 20 MJ/kg for cement; Cl 0.5–1.2 % dry), not measurements of the refus that go into them.

### Cited Findings
**Citeo Observatoire de la qualité 2025 (published April 2026)**: [CITEO-OBQ-2025](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/OBQ-2025-VDEF.pdf)
- Campaign: "1434 caractérisations réalisées en 2025, couvrant 116 centres de tri et 16 recycleurs" (pp. 2–3, 7). Operators: "Pour la campagne 2025, les caractérisations ont été réalisées par les bureaux d'études Austral, V2R et Terra" (pp. 6–7). Sites chosen "de manière à obtenir des résultats représentatifs de la production nationale". Rating: **primary measured**.
- Films PE/PP: mean 88.8 % target-material content (standard 90 %), 110 characterisations (p. 31).
- Plastique rigide en mélange: 92.3 % (standard 90 %), 89 characterisations, of which 67 compliant, 18 "qualité approchante" and 4 "sous-qualité" (p. 32).
- Flux rigides à trier (monoflux, the bales sent to surtri): 92 % (standard 90 %), 20 characterisations (p. 33).
- Surtri rejects appear only in a flow diagram: "Refus de tri (PVC/films trop petits)" and "Refus de tri (PVC/films hors PE/trop petits)" (pp. 40–41). There are **no figures**.
- Plastic-rich paper-mill stream: the polyolefin fraction of pulper rejects (refus de pulpeur) is "un gisement estimé à environ 100 000 tonnes par an en France" (p. 21, Valomat project).
- 2023 campaign (from background, not re-fetched): PE/PP/PS bales 88.1 %, films PE 82 %, flux développement 92 %; 123 centres, 1,559 characterisations ([CITEO-OBSQUAL-2023](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2024-04/Citeo%20-%20Observatoire%20de%20la%20qualit%C3%A9%202023.pdf), pp. 29–34).

**NEA (Neste Energie Avenir) CSR boiler plant, Lannemezan (65)**: environmental authorisation file, section E3, rev. A, 15/10/2022 (SVD 94; CSR prepared by PSI Environnement): [ICPE-LANNEMEZAN-2022](https://www.hautes-pyrenees.gouv.fr/contenu/telechargement/13874/95409/file/e3-01220545-105-aut-me-1-033-a-origine.pdf)
- Inputs: refus de tri from DAE, collecte sélective, packaging and paper, and déchetterie bulky waste. Excluded: "Les déchets servant à la préparation des CSR ne comprendront pas de refus de tri d'ordures ménagères" (p. 4).
- CSR specification, Table 1 p. 4 (min / mean / max):
  - "PCI sur brut 12 MJ/kg 13 MJ/kg 18 MJ/kg"
  - "Humidité sur brut 10% 15% 25%"
  - "Teneur en cendres sur sec à 815 °C 5% 15% 25%"
  - "Chlore (Cl) sur sec 1 g/kg (0,1%) 7 g/kg (0,7%) 10 g/kg (1%)"
  - "Somme halogénés (Br+Cl+F+I) sur sec 12 g/kg (1,2%)"
  - N 0.7 / 1 / 1.2 % dry; density 100 / 150 / 250 kg/m³
- The file states CSR is produced "conformément à l'arrêté du 23 mai 2016". It gives **no plastics/paper/fines shares and no sampling method**. Rating: **primary declared (specification)**.

**Paprec, ATEE CSR webinar (June 2021)**: [PAPREC-ATEE-2021](https://atee.fr/system/files/2021-06/4.%20PAPREC-ATEE%20Webinaire%20CSR%2006%202021.pdf)
- Cement CSR (slide 5): "PCI >20Mj, CL<0,5%, H2o <15% Ash<15%".
- Boiler CSR (slide 6): "12,5<PCI<15 CL<1,2 % H2o <25% Ash<25%", and a stricter variant "CL<0,8% H2o <15% Ash<15%".
- A plastics reject stream is described as "Pci >24 Non Valo de Paprec Plastics" with no share given.
- Wood B and fibres are flagged as the chlorine and biogenic-CO₂ sources ("CL & CO2: Bois B, fibreux").
- No measured compositions. Rating: **primary declared (specification)**.

### Inferences
- Bale purity data give an indirect picture of plastic-rich side streams. A tonne of "plastique rigide en mélange" or flux-développement bale holds about 920 kg of target plastic packaging and about 80 kg of non-target [calc]. These bales have buyers (see the background verification note: zero-floor prices). The rejects of surtri centres downstream of them ("PVC/films trop petits") are likely PVC-enriched. That matters for pyrolysis, but **no number was found**.
- The CSR specifications show what the French market expects from refus-derived fuel: 12–18 MJ/kg as received for boilers, and Cl at most 0.7–1.2 % dry. A pyrolysis feed that is plastic-enriched beyond this (above 20 MJ/kg) would compete with cement-kiln CSR (Paprec spec PCI above 20 MJ/kg, Cl below 0.5 %).
- Refus de pulpeur (about 100 kt/yr of polyolefin fraction) is a candidate plastic-rich stream absent from the annex. Its composition was not found.

### Gaps
- No measured composition of French **DAE** sorting rejects was found.
- No measured composition of **surtri** rejects. Citeo and the surtri operators are the data holders.
- A Suez CSR ICPE file in Aisne that may contain input characterisation was blocked by robots.txt: [SUEZ-AISNE-DDAE](https://www.aisne.gouv.fr/index.php/contenu/telechargement/35471/227255/file/14%20-%20E3790P02T01_SUEZ_CH-CSR_DDAE_E7.7_TT-DECHETS_D.pdf). It is worth retrieving manually.

## Q3. Germany / Austria — composition and fuel properties of LVP sorting residues (Sortierreste)

### Takeaway
Even in Germany, the Öko-Institut's 2022 LCA for the dual systems states that **no mean composition of LVP Sortierreste is available** from questionnaires or literature. It uses an operator proxy instead: about 46–49 % plastics, 15–18 % paper/board, 36 % other residues, 20 % moisture assumed, LHV 16–18 MJ/kg wet. The only lab-analysed LVP residue sample found (KIT) is much more plastic-rich: 76 % plastics, 10.6 % ash, 2.0 % Cl+Br (dry), LHV about 25 MJ/kg dry (calculated). That is one sample of unstated mass from an unnamed plant. No Leoben, TU Wien or RWTH dataset specific to LVP residues was retrieved.

### Cited Findings

- Fuel analysis of LVP sorting residues (LVP-SR), slide 7:
  - moisture 1.7 wt % "Nach Vorbehandlung" (after pretreatment, so not the as-received value)
  - ash (550 °C) 10.6 wt % dry
  - "Cl + Br … 2,0" wt % dry
  - Heizwert "ca. 25" MJ/kg dry
  - C 58.5, H 7.5, N below 1.0, S 0.1, O (by difference) 21.3 wt %
- Composition of the plant sample, slide 16: "Zusammensetzung der Stichprobe aus Sortieranlage"; "Kunststoffanteil = 76,1 %", "Pyrolysierbar = 16,6 %", "Nicht rezyklierbar durch Pyrolyse = 7,4 %".
- Rating: **primary measured** for the elemental analysis. The composition is a **single grab sample** of unstated mass from an unnamed plant; waste suppliers are acknowledged as PreZero, ARN B.V., Electrocycling and I.A.R. RWTH Aachen.

**KIT, "Chemisches Recycling kunststoffhaltiger Abfälle – Das Potenzial der Pyrolyse"** (same group; repository record): [KIT-PYRO](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000156454/150389031)

Its proxy gives 17.4 MJ/kg wet; total C 41.1 % with 0.70 kg fossil C per kg C (Doka 2020).
- No Sortierreste-specific chlorine value; 0.6 % Cl is given for the combined Wertstoffe (Table 3-7, p. 32).
- Destination: mainly energy recovery; EBS made from sorting and processing residues goes to cement works (pp. 21, 37).
- Rating: **modelled** (composition and properties).

- "Sortierreste 25 – 30 ----- energetisch" (p. 3), given as "Anhaltswerte" for modern LVP plants with NIR. Rating: **primary declared, old (2005)**.

**ZSVR factsheet "Sorting lightweight packaging" (Feb. 2026)**: [ZSVR-2026](https://www.verpackungsregister.org/fileadmin/files/Erklaermaterialien/Factsheet_Sorting_lightweight_packaging.pdf)
- No residue data. It states "modern sorting facilities achieve sorting rates of more than 95%", which refers to recovery of recyclables, not residue share.

**Austria / Montanuniversität Leoben**
- Selina Möllnitz's publication list (2019–2021) covers plastics in **mixed municipal and commercial waste** and SRF (Waste Management vols 103, 119, 121; Polymers 2021; doctoral thesis 2021), not LVP sorting residues: [LEOBEN-MOELLNITZ](https://pureadmin.unileoben.ac.at/portal/en/persons/selina-moellnitz(90229ccd-a59c-4904-a499-2ae5394dc322)/publications.html).
- Warchol (2020), MSc thesis, Leoben: plastics content of Austrian **Restmüll** 18 % (Steiermark 2014), 10 % (Oberösterreich 2019), 6 % (Salzburg 2019), mean 11 % (Table 1, p. 10). Commercial waste models: 10 / 6 / 20 % (Table 2, p. 11). Hand-sorting campaign at 2 plants with 3D/2D samples of about 230–660 kg each (Table 4, p. 28). Quote: "Diese Mischkunststoffe haben einen Störstoffanteil von durchschnittlich 10 %" (p. 15, citing Martens & Goldmann 2016). Source: [WARCHOL-2020](https://pureadmintest.unileoben.ac.at/portal/files/5731583/AC16066996.pdf). Rating: **primary measured**, but these are mixed-waste streams, not LVP residues.

### Inferences
- **What a tonne of German LVP Sortierreste contains**, two bounds:
  - Lobbe 2017, as-received plant residue, declared: about 460–490 kg plastics (PET ≥ 280, dark plastics ≥ 150–180, films below 30), 150–180 kg paper/board, at least 360 kg "Reststoffe" [calc].
  - KIT single sample, apparently after removal of coarse minerals and dried before analysis: about 760 kg plastics, 69 kg composites, 74 kg biomass, 23 kg textiles, 71 kg metals, 3 kg glass/mineral [calc].
  - The gap between the two (about 46–49 % vs 76 % plastics) shows how strongly the result depends on which residue stream is meant: classic "Sortierreste" vs pre-treated "Mischkunststoff/EBS-Vorprodukt" fractions.
- German LVP residues even at the low bound (about 46–49 % plastics) are roughly four times richer in **identified** plastics than French CS refus (12 %, OCAPEM). That is consistent with paper and glass being excluded from LVP at source. **The annex's 35–45 % plastics for French refus B1b looks like a German-LVP-type figure transplanted onto a paper-inclusive French stream.**
- Chlorine: 2.0 % Cl+Br (dry) in the KIT LVP-SR sample is 2–4 times the French CSR specifications (Cl at most 0.5–1.0 % dry). For pyrolysis this implies that dechlorination or pre-sorting of PVC is mandatory. If the 6 % Cl in condensate refers to this feed, chlorine transfers strongly to the oil.
- Moisture: the only figure for as-received LVP residue is an **assumption** (20 %, Öko-Institut). The 1.7 % from KIT is post-pretreatment.

### Gaps
- No measured German or Austrian dataset with multiple plants, documented sampling and a polymer split (PE/PP/PS/PET/PVC/multilayer) of LVP Sortierreste was found.
- Zeller et al. (2021), Chem. Ing. Tech. 93(11) (doi 10.1002/cite.202100102), was not accessible: the Wiley fetch permission was withdrawn. It should contain the full sample description.
- The Resources, Conservation & Recycling 2024 paper "Feedstock recycling of waste plastics in an oil refinery: Scenario development based on sorting and pyrolysis experiments" was blocked: [RCR-2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344924003082). Its authors, input stream and data are unverified.
- No UBA Texte with a measured LVP-residue composition was found. The abfallbewertung.org entry for "Sortierreste" (code 19 12 12) was blocked by robots.txt: [ABFALLBEWERTUNG](https://www.abfallbewertung.org/repgen.php?report=ipa&char_id=1912_Sort&lang_id=de&avv=&synon=&kapitel=2&gtactive=yes).
- No RWTH (Feil/Pretz), Fraunhofer, TU Wien or Conversio residue composition was retrieved.

## Q4. Netherlands / Belgium — composition of PMD / P+MD sorting residue

### Takeaway
The Dutch WUR system models rest on **one hand-sorted sorting-residue sample from 2014**. Its standard deviation was set arbitrarily to 15 % because only one sample existed. Later residue shares (14 % coarse + 5 % fine residue for 2017 PMD) are **model outputs**. No Belgian P+MD residue composition was found; Fost Plus has contracted a residue re-sorting plant, which suggests residues still hold recoverable material.

### Cited Findings

- "The composition of the sorting residue was determined with one sorting analysis, see annex C" (p. 7). Table C.1 column "Sorting residue – from separate collection", year "2014": "Since only one such sample had been analysed, the standard deviation was set to 15%."
- Residue rows retrieved (Table C.1, pp. 35–36), % of residue:

  | Row | % |
  |---|---|
  | PET bottle clear ≤ 0.5 L | 1.8 |
  | PET bottle coloured ≤ 0.5 L | 2.0 |
  | PET bottle clear > 0.5 L | 0.2 |
  | PE beverage bottles | 0.7 |
  | PET non-beverage bottles | 1.3 |
  | PE non-beverage bottles | 1.4 |
  | PP non-beverage bottles | 2.6 |
  | PET thermoforms | 1.1 |
  | PE thermoforms | 0.1 |
  | PP thermoforms | 0.1 |
  | **PVC thermoforms** | **0.9** |

### Inferences
- Dutch residue composition, the basis of widely cited WUR MFA work, rests on a single 2014 sample. Its residue still contained identifiable rigid packaging (at least 14 %), including PVC thermoforms (0.9 %).
- The Fost Plus/Vanheede contract signals that Belgian P+MD residues are being re-sorted. They would then become a competing destination for the plastic-rich part of residues, rather than a pyrolysis feed.

### Gaps
- The full WUR 2014 residue column (films, non-packaging plastics, paper, metals, moisture/dirt) was not retrieved.
- No Belgian (Fost Plus, OVAM) measured residue composition was found.
- No Dutch residue chlorine, LHV or ash data were found.

### Takeaway
The UK publishes quarterly **MRF input** sampling (target, non-target and non-recyclable shares) through the WRAP/Materials Facility reporting portal, but **no residue composition** was found. Switzerland's key federal study (KuRVe 2017) gives no measured residue composition.

**Switzerland, KuRVe (Carbotech AG / UMTEC, 13 July 2017, for BAFU)**: [KURVE-2017](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/dam/bafu/de/dokumente/abfall/externe-studien-berichte/studie-kurve.pdf.download.pdf/KuRVe_Bericht_%C3%B6ffentlich_Deutsch.pdf)

## Q6. Other measured datasets from adjacent systems (Spain, Québec)

### Takeaway
Measured data exist outside the core countries but are old (Spain, sampling in 2013–14) or not extractable from the public text (Québec 2018–2020). They are useful mainly to show how variable chlorine is (up to 6.3 % in one Spanish packaging fraction) and that well-designed multi-plant campaigns are feasible (Québec: 23 centres, 415 samples).

### Cited Findings
**Spain**: Garcés, Díaz, Sastre, Ordóñez, González-LaFuente, "Evaluation of the potential of different high calorific waste fractions for the preparation of solid recovered fuels", Waste Management 47 (2016) 164–173, doi 10.1016/j.wasman.2015.08.029: [GARCES-2016](https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65df87b090674e46e653da90)
- Packaging, bulky and end-of-life-vehicle wastes from a landfill operator, sampled November 2013 and April 2014.
- "The lower heating value of the waste fractions on wet basis varies between 10MJkg⁻¹ and 42MJkg⁻¹"; "One of the packaging wastes presents a very high chlorine content (6.3wt.%)", attributed to PVC pipe fragments.
- Rating: **primary measured, pre-2015 sampling (flag)**. Sample counts were not given in the abstract.

**Spain**: Adrados et al., "Pyrolysis of plastic packaging waste: A comparison of plastic residuals from material recovery facilities with simulated plastic waste", Waste Management 32(5) (2012) 826–832: [ADRADOS-2012](https://dsp.tecnalia.com/items/093030aa-f342-487f-aeb8-948375c292f1/full)
- Rejects contain PE, PP, PS, PVC, PET, ABS, aluminium, tetra-brik and film. The real reject sample gave higher gas and solid yields and less liquid than a simulated mixture.
- No composition percentages appear in the abstract. Rating: **primary measured, old (2012)**.

**Québec**: Éco Entreprises Québec & RECYC-QUÉBEC, "Caractérisation des matières sortantes des centres de tri 2018–2020": [RECYC-QC-2020](https://www.recyc-quebec.gouv.qc.ca/sites/default/files/documents/caracterisation-matieres-sortantes-centres-de-tri-2018-2020-rapport-final.pdf)
- 23 centres, 415 samples, June 2018 to December 2020. Rejects were sampled at "étapes subséquentes de tri" (not pre-sort).
- "Composition moyenne des rejets" (p. 4) lists Fibres, Métaux, Plastiques, Verre and Autres matières. The extracted values (37, 17, 14, 24, 1, 9) **could not be mapped to categories** from the text layer.
- "Généralement accepté 46 %" vs "Généralement refusé 54 %". Rating: **primary measured**; values to be read from the PDF chart.

### Inferences
- A pyrolysis project receiving residues with PVC fragments cannot rely on average chlorine values. Single-batch chlorine can reach several percent (6.3 % Cl in the Spanish packaging fraction; 2 % Cl+Br in the KIT LVP sample).
- The Québec campaign is a paper-inclusive single-stream analogue to French CS and is the best-documented multi-plant reject characterisation found. Its numbers should be read directly from the PDF before use.

### Gaps
- More recent Spanish (Ecoembes) or Italian (COREPLA "plasmix") reject characterisations were not searched, for lack of budget.

## Q7. Synthesis — what does a tonne of sorting residue contain? (evidence-weighted)

### Takeaway
**No dataset in any country meets the end user's bar** for this key pyrolysis parameter: multiple plants, documented sampling, polymer split, and a lab fuel analysis (PCI, Cl, ash, moisture). For French CS refus, the only national figure is 12 % identified plastic packaging (method undisclosed). For German LVP residues, figures range from about 46–49 % plastics (operator, declared) to 76 % (one KIT grab sample). Fuel properties come from one KIT sample (dry: about 25 MJ/kg calculated, 10.6 % ash, 2.0 % Cl+Br), from modelling (16–18 MJ/kg wet, 20 % water assumed), or from CSR specifications (France: 12–18 MJ/kg as received, Cl 0.1–1 % dry). A project-specific characterisation is required.

### Inferences
- **For the Urban Rig model parameter `composition.part_plastique`:**
  - For French CS refus (B1b), the only national evidence gives 12 % identified plastic packaging. A defensible range pending characterisation is perhaps 12 % (floor) to about 25–30 %, if a sizeable share of the 70.9 % "autres" turned out to be non-packaging or nested plastics. **The upper value is my assumption, not a measurement.**
  - The annex's 35–45 % matches German LVP residues (about 46–49 % declared), which come from a paper-free collection, and is not supported for French CS refus.
  - Plastic-rich feeds of 75–92 % plastics exist: pre-treated LVP residues (KIT), mixed-rigid and flux-développement bales (92 % target content, Citeo OBQ 2025), and probably surtri rejects and pulper-reject polyolefins. Their composition, however, is either measured on n = 1 or not published.
- **Fuel properties to plan around**, until measured:
  - LHV about 13–18 MJ/kg as received for mixed refus (CSR specs; Öko-Institut proxy).
  - About 25 MJ/kg dry for plastic-enriched residue (KIT, calculated).
  - Chlorine 0.6–2 % dry, with single-batch peaks of several percent (6.3 % Spain).
  - Ash about 10–25 % dry.
  - Moisture 10–25 % as received (spec range; 20 % assumed by Öko-Institut).
- **Recommended data acquisition:**
  1. Request the full OCAPEM report from Citeo (method behind Table 3).
  2. Request NF X30-472 results from 3–5 target sorting centres, plus the CSR preparation input analyses that operators produce under the arrêté of 23 May 2016.
  3. Commission a campaign on the target stream: multi-day composite samples per NF X30-472, plus lab analysis of PCI, Cl, ash, moisture and a polymer split (for example by NIR or FTIR on the plastic fraction).
  - The specific SRF lab standards to cite (e.g. EN 15359 / ISO 21640) were **not verified in this session**.

- No public dataset gives measured **moisture as received**, **measured (bomb calorimeter) PCI**, **chlorine** and **ash** together with composition for French refus.
- The DAE sorting-reject composition, surtri reject composition and regenerator residue composition are unpublished. The data holders are Citeo, the surtri operators, Valorplast/recyclers and CSR preparers.
