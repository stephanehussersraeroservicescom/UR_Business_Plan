# Annex 8 (v2): Non-recycled post-consumer plastic in France, plastic in residual waste and plastic unfit for recycling

*Urban Rig business plan, URC-2000 pyrolysis plant planned near Paris. Version 2, 10 October 2026. This annex replaces the March 2026 draft "Annex 8, Sector: Plastic Sorting Rejects (France)".*

The verifiable market of non-recycled post-consumer plastic in France has two parts of very different size. Residual household waste (OMR) contains **2,329,258 t of plastic a year, 15.8% of OMR by wet mass** ([ADEME, MODECOM 2024 open data](https://data.ademe.fr/data-fair/api/v1/datasets/omrcs-compo-jdd/lines?format=csv&size=100); 2024 campaign on 2023 tonnage; Measured, FACT). About **1.60 Mt of it is packaging** [calc] **and 0.50 Mt is bin bags**, and the total falls to about **1.66 Mt on a dry basis** [calc]. Most of it is burned or landfilled, at a treatment cost to local authorities of about **€270 M a year** [calc on [ADEME, cost reference 2023, fig. 33](https://librairie.ademe.fr/societe-et-politiques-publiques/8811-10587-referentiel-des-couts-du-service-public-de-gestion-des-dechets-en-france-hexagonale-donnees-2023.html); HYPOTHESIS]. Plastic collected for recycling but not recycled is far smaller. About **85 to 120 kt** of packaging is lost in sorting refus, and about **149 kt** is lost between the **473 kt** of plastic bales taken back and the **324 kt** counted as recycled in 2024 ([Citeo, Observatoire de la Reprise 2024, slide 5](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/20251006_Observatoire_reprise_2024_VDEF.pdf); [Citeo, annual report 2025, p. 29](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Rapport-dactivite-2025_Citeo.pdf); Declared, FACT). That is **about 235 to 270 kt a year**, and up to about 450 kt when non-packaging plastic and plastic carried into paper bales are counted [calc; HYPOTHESIS]. In Île-de-France, OMR holds an estimated **0.44 to 0.51 Mt of plastic** [calc], and the **146,368 t of sorting refus** ([ORDIF, Bilan DMA 2024, slide 17](https://www.ordif.fr/fileadmin/DataStorage/user_upload/00-home/Bilan_DMA_2024%40.pdf); 2024; Declared, FACT) contain about **18 to 44 kt of plastic** [calc]. Money reverses at the point of loss. A recycled tonne of mainstream plastic brings a local authority about **980 to 1,010 €/t** [calc], while a tonne of refus costs it about **120 to 160 € HT/t plus TGAP** at an incinerator ([SITOM Nord-Isère convention, art. 5.2](https://cc-plainedelain.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024-093-annexe-convention.pdf); 2024; Contract/Transaction, FACT). Low-grade bales have a contractual buyer at a **0 €/t floor** ([Valorplast contract, art. 11](https://clissonsevremaine.fr/fileadmin/Aggloh/Decisions_president/Annee_2024/03.2024-03_contrat_reprise_filiere_plastique_avec_Valorplast_executoire.pdf); 2024-2029; Contract/Transaction, FACT) and are allocated to new recyclers under multi-year contracts, so they carry no gate fee. The v1 "B1a" stream (670 kt in France, 135 kt in Île-de-France, over 95% plastic, paid for by its holder) appears in no source and is withdrawn. For Urban Rig, gate-fee feedstock exists but is poor in plastic (mixed refus) or small and unmeasured (surtri and regenerator residues). Plastic-rich feedstock must be bought at 0 €/t or more. The deciding data are held by operators and must be collected before the financial model is fixed.

## 1. Reading guide: scope, labels and conventions

This annex covers two streams of post-consumer plastic in France and Île-de-France. The first is plastic in mixed residual household waste (OMR). The second is plastic that is collected but is impure or unfit for recycling: sorting refus, low-grade bales, non-target material in bales, recycler and surtri residues, and packaging with no recycling route. For each stream the annex gives inflows and outflows, the money paid at each step, and the method behind each figure. **WEEE, end-of-life vehicle, construction, agricultural and textile plastics are outside the scope of v2.** Non-household packaging and commercial (DAE) residues appear only as context, where they compete for the same outlets.

| Reliability label | Meaning | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Measured | Weighing, sampling or laboratory analysis with a documented method | FACT |
| Declared | Figure declared by an operator, eco-organisation, authority or official statistic | FACT |
| Contract/Transaction | Signed contract, executed payment or executed aggregate flow | FACT |
| Published tariff | Official or published rate, price grid or tax rate | FACT |
| Modelled | Forecast, model, estimate or reconciliation made by the source | HYPOTHESIS |
| Secondary | Press, summary, or a source quoting another source | HYPOTHESIS until checked against the primary source |
| Unverified | Method or perimeter could not be checked | HYPOTHESIS |
| Dated | Data older than eight years (before 2018); added to another label | Class of the base label |
| [calc] | Derivation made in this annex, with inputs shown | HYPOTHESIS |
| Judgement | Engineering or commercial judgement by the authors | ASSUMPTION |

Price assessments read at the publisher (Platts, letsrecycle) are labelled Declared (price index). The same assessments quoted by a third party are labelled Secondary. Tonnages are wet, as collected, unless marked "dry". "€ HT" means excluding VAT. "kt" is a thousand tonnes. "Project register" marks a figure verified on 10/10/2026 in an earlier session and not re-read in this round. Page and slide numbers come from text extraction and may be off by one.

Short glossary. OMR: residual waste in the grey bin, collected by the public service, including some business waste. CS (collecte sélective): separate collection of packaging and paper in the yellow bin. Refus de tri: material rejected by a sorting centre. Surtri: secondary sorting of low-grade plastic bales. Flux développement: low-grade plastic flows sent to surtri. UVE: incinerator with energy recovery. ISDND: non-hazardous landfill. MBT: mechanical-biological treatment. CSR: solid recovered fuel. DAE: commercial and industrial waste. TGAP: French tax on waste disposal. Citeo: main producer-responsibility organisation for household packaging. Valorplast: main buyer of plastic bales in the Citeo system. ORDIF: Île-de-France waste observatory. Syctom: treatment syndicate for Paris and 81 communes.

## 3. Residual waste carries 2.33 Mt of plastic, two thirds of it packaging

### Fourteen measured categories, dominated by films and bin bags

MODECOM 2024 is ADEME's fourth national characterisation campaign, after 1993, 2007 and 2017 ([ADEME library, ref. 013089](https://librairie.ademe.fr/economie-circulaire-et-dechets/8784-modecom-2024-campagne-nationale-de-caracterisation-des-dechets-menagers-et-assimiles.html); published 19/12/2025; Declared, FACT). Field teams analysed 95 OMR samples from 50 communes in 2024, after removing 5 outliers. Sampling followed NF X30-413 and sorting NF X30-408. Moisture was measured on 20 samples and calorific value on 15 ([MODECOM 2024 summary, p. 11, 14 and 25](https://obdec-normandie.org/sites/obdec/files/2026-01/ADEME-MODECOM-2024-caracterisation-dechet-synthese.pdf); 2024; Measured, FACT). Results were extrapolated to **14,752,013 t of OMR in mainland France in 2023, or 223.46 kg per inhabitant** ([summary, p. 39](https://obdec-normandie.org/sites/obdec/files/2026-01/ADEME-MODECOM-2024-caracterisation-dechet-synthese.pdf); 2023; Declared, FACT). For the first time, household waste and business waste collected by the public service were characterised together ([Déchets Infos, 13/03/2024](https://dechets-infos.com/modecom-national-la-campagne-2024-est-lancee-4930699.html); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). ADEME attributes about 80% of OMR to households and 20% to businesses, on a 2017 basis ([ADEME, Déchets chiffres-clés 2023, fig. 24, p. 27](https://www.ecologic-france.com/images/medias/document/21269/dechetsdeeechiffres-cles-2023ademe.pdf); 2017; Declared, Dated, FACT). The 2.33 Mt is therefore plastic in the public-service grey bin. It is not household plastic only.

### Moisture cuts the tonnage by 29% on a dry basis

Measured moisture is **35.8% (± 4.8) for the film fraction and 20.1% (± 2.8) for other plastics** ([ADEME open data, omrhum](https://data.ademe.fr/data-fair/api/v1/datasets/omrhum/lines?format=csv&size=100); 2024; n = 20; Measured, FACT). ADEME asks for these values to be read "avec beaucoup de prudence" (with great caution); the 2017 values were 27.9% and 18.0% ([summary, table 1, p. 23](https://obdec-normandie.org/sites/obdec/files/2026-01/ADEME-MODECOM-2024-caracterisation-dechet-synthese.pdf); Measured, FACT). On a dry basis, plastic in OMR falls to **about 1.66 Mt**, packaging to about 1.15 Mt and non-packaging to about 0.51 Mt (Table 3). "Dry" still includes adhering food and dirt. The calorific value of the film fraction is **27,808 J/g of dry matter (± 2,940)**, against 35,489 J/g in 2007 ([summary, table 2, p. 24](https://obdec-normandie.org/sites/obdec/files/2026-01/ADEME-MODECOM-2024-caracterisation-dechet-synthese.pdf); 2024; Measured, FACT). The value for the other plastic fractions did not extract cleanly and must be read on the PDF.

The holder of plastic in OMR is the local authority. It pays the treatment syndicate or operator per tonne of OMR, with no price signal specific to plastic. National median net costs in 2023, TGAP included, were **107 €/t for incineration (n = 306), 138 €/t for landfill (n = 197) and 156 €/t for MBT with composting (n = 44)** ([ADEME, cost reference 2023, fig. 33, p. 36](https://librairie.ademe.fr/societe-et-politiques-publiques/8811-10587-referentiel-des-couts-du-service-public-de-gestion-des-dechets-en-france-hexagonale-donnees-2023.html); 2023; Declared, FACT). At these medians, treating the plastic in OMR costs about **€169 M (incineration), €74 M (landfill) and €26 M (MBT), or about €270 M a year** before collection [calc: tonnages of Table 5 × median costs; HYPOTHESIS]. Incinerators recover part of their cost through energy sales of 5 to 46 €/t ([AMORCE, DT139, p. 17](https://amorce.asso.fr/publications/performances-recettes-et-couts-des-unites-de-traitement-thermique-des-dechets-donnees-2020-2021-dt139/download); 2021; Declared, FACT). Citeo pays local authorities an energy-recovery support for packaging left in OMR of **€15.0 M in 2024 and €9.9 M in 2025** ([Citeo, annual report 2025, p. 28](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Rapport-dactivite-2025_Citeo.pdf); Contract/Transaction, FACT). That is at most about 6 €/t of plastic packaging in OMR [calc: €9.9 M ÷ 1,595,388 t; the support covers all materials; HYPOTHESIS]. The State pays the EU own resource on the packaging part (section 6).

## 4. Île-de-France burns about 0.4 Mt of plastic a year without a regional measurement

Île-de-France collected **3.2 Mt of OMR in 2024, or 258 kg per inhabitant** for 12,419,961 inhabitants ([ORDIF, Bilan DMA 2024, slides 2 and 26](https://www.ordif.fr/fileadmin/DataStorage/user_upload/00-home/Bilan_DMA_2024%40.pdf); 2024; Declared, FACT). About **90% of residual waste goes to 18 incinerators**, 17 of which qualify as energy-recovery units. Their authorised capacity is 4,275,900 t, used at about 80% (same source, slides 15-16). ORDIF states that **no recent characterisation represents the whole region** (slide 18). The only regional composition dates from 2014 and shows 3% "recyclable plastics" ([ORDIF, DMA report 2022, p. 19-21](https://www.ordif.fr/fileadmin/DataStorage/user_upload/ORDIF_Rapp_DMA_2022_V1b.pdf); 2014; Declared, Dated, FACT). That category predates the extended sorting instructions and excludes most plastic.

## 5. Collected plastic loses 235 to 270 kt a year before it counts as recycled

Plastic collected for recycling leaves the chain at four points: in sorting refus, as non-target material in bales, as residue at surtri centres and recyclers, and as moisture and dirt removed before the EU calculation point. No French source measures these losses directly. They can be bounded from declared tonnages and from one measured composition.

### The household chain runs from 1.2 Mt placed on the market to 324 kt recycled

Citeo reports **1,165 kt of household plastic packaging placed on the market in 2024 and 1,174 kt in 2025**, with **324 kt and 346 kt recycled** ([Citeo, annual report 2025, p. 12-13 and 29](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Rapport-dactivite-2025_Citeo.pdf); Declared, FACT). Its Observatoire de la Reprise gives a 2024 "contributing" tonnage of **1,224 kt** ([Citeo, Observatoire de la Reprise 2024, slide 5](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/20251006_Observatoire_reprise_2024_VDEF.pdf); Declared, FACT). The two figures are not reconciled (section 7). Since 2023, Citeo counts tonnes "effectivement recyclées" at the EU calculation point, not tonnes accepted at the recycler's gate ([Citeo, key figures 2023, note 3](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/241223_CITEO_Fiche_Chiffres_cles-2023_Notre_Impact.pdf); Declared, FACT). Plastic bales taken back reached **473,033 t in 2024 (+8.8%)** and 434,580 t in 2023 ([Observatoire 2024, slide 5](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/20251006_Observatoire_reprise_2024_VDEF.pdf); [Observatoire 2023, slide 5](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2024-10/20240930_Observatoire_reprise_2023_VDEF.pdf); Declared, FACT). In 2024, **73.8% of plastic bales were processed in France and 26.2% elsewhere in Europe**, with practically none outside Europe ([Observatoire 2024, slide 6](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/20251006_Observatoire_reprise_2024_VDEF.pdf); Declared, FACT).

### Sorting refus total 800 kt a year and hold 12 to 30% plastic

The average refus rate of French sorting centres rose from **18.9% in 2018 to 23.0% in 2022, or 800,000 t of refus** ([Citeo, Cahier Collecte sélective, p. 19](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2024-11/CITEO_Cahier_Collecte%20s%C3%A9lective_nov%202024.pdf); 2022; Declared, FACT). OCAPEM gives 750,000 t without a year ([OCAPEM synthesis, table 5, p. 7](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Synthese-du-Rapport-OCAPEM-Amelioration-du-tri-des-petits-emballages.pdf); Unverified, HYPOTHESIS). The rate covers all materials, paper included. The only national composition of refus gives **7.5% rigid plastic packaging and 4.5% flexible plastic packaging**, 15.7% fibres and **70.9% "other"** (fines, nested items, glass, non-packaging) ([OCAPEM synthesis, table 3, p. 5](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Synthese-du-Rapport-OCAPEM-Amelioration-du-tri-des-petits-emballages.pdf); Unverified, HYPOTHESIS: mass, sites, dates and protocol are not published). Citeo estimates that **15 to 20% of the plastic entering sorting centres ends in refus** ([Citeo, ECT report 2020, p. 25](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2022-06/CITEO_RAPPORT%20ECT%202020.pdf); Modelled, HYPOTHESIS). Valorplast's director states that 55% of films are captured in sorting centres ([IFPEN synthesis, 02/07/2025](https://www.ifpenergiesnouvelles.fr/print/pdf/node/1904); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). MODECOM finds 19% of separate collection non-conforming in 2024, against 12.4% in 2017, and warns that this is not a sorting-centre refus rate ([summary, p. 27-28](https://obdec-normandie.org/sites/obdec/files/2026-01/ADEME-MODECOM-2024-caracterisation-dechet-synthese.pdf); Measured, FACT).

Two routes give **85 to 120 kt of plastic packaging in national refus** [calc: 750 to 800 kt × 12% = 90 to 96 kt; and 473 kt of bales ÷ (1 minus 0.15 to 0.20) minus 473 kt = 83 to 118 kt; HYPOTHESIS]. A MODECOM-based balance gives an upper bound for all plastic. In 2024, 691 kt of plastic entered separate collection and 473 kt of plastic bales left, about 90% of it target material. Up to **218 to 265 kt of plastic** therefore left the sorting stage other than as plastic bales [calc]; with 2023 bales, 256 to 300 kt [calc]. Part of it travels in paper bales. Citeo estimates the polyolefin fraction of paper-mill pulper rejects at **about 100,000 t a year**, from household and professional packaging ([Citeo, Observatoire de la qualité 2025, p. 20-21](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/OBQ-2025-VDEF.pdf); Modelled, HYPOTHESIS). Before that deduction, 218 to 300 kt is 27 to 37% of 800 kt of refus [calc]. The plastic share of refus is therefore bracketed by **12% (identified packaging, floor) and about 30% (all plastic, upper bound)** [calc; HYPOTHESIS]. The v1 range of 35 to 45% has no French source.

The latest national split of refus destinations dates from 2012: 75% landfill and 20% energy recovery ([SINOE, ITOM 2012, p. 7](https://www.sinoe.org/documents/consult-and-count-doc/doc/1200/rubrique/6); 2012; Declared, Dated, FACT). In territories with extended sorting instructions, refus went to energy recovery for 85 to 86% of the population covered in 2019-2020 ([Citeo, ECT report 2020, p. 4-5](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2022-06/CITEO_RAPPORT%20ECT%202020.pdf); Declared, FACT). That is a share of population, not of tonnage. The 2,738 kt of "refus de tri" landfilled in 2020 ([FNADE and SN2E, CSR model, section 3](https://www.fnade.org/ressources/documents/source/1/4855-FNADE-SN2E-Synthese-modele-economique-CSR-VDEF.pdf); 2020; Secondary, HYPOTHESIS) covers all origins, mainly commercial sorting. It cannot be compared with the 800 kt.

### About 149 kt a year is lost between the bale and the recycling count

The difference between plastic bales taken back (473 kt) and plastic recycled (324 kt) is **about 149 kt in 2024, or 31.5% of bales** [calc; HYPOTHESIS]. It combines non-target material and moisture in bales with process losses at surtri centres and recyclers. It is a declared-minus-declared difference, not a measurement, and the perimeters may differ (the Observatoire excludes overseas territories unless stated). In 2022 the gap was about 95 kt [calc: about 386.6 kt of bales minus about 292 kt recycled, the latter being 24.5% × about 1,191 kt from [Citeo, key figures 2022](https://notre-impact-2024.citeo.com/240418_Chiffres_cles_2022_FR_WEB.pdf), Declared]. From 2023 to 2024 bales rose 8.8% while recycled tonnage rose about 1% [calc: 2023 recycled taken as 27% × 1,190 kt from [CB News/AFP, 25/11/2024](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/88845), Secondary]. This fits the growing share of low-yield films and flux développement. Adding this post-bale loss to the sorting-stage loss gives **about 235 to 270 kt of collected packaging not recycled** on Citeo-based figures [calc: 85 to 120 + 149 kt], and **up to about 370 to 450 kt of all plastic** on the MODECOM-based balance [calc: 218 to 300 + 149 kt; years mixed; HYPOTHESIS].

No French recycler or surtri centre publishes residue tonnages. National references are aggregates. In 2020, **929 kt of plastic was prepared for recycling, 690 kt recycled and 440 kt of recycled raw material produced**, a loss of over 36% ([OPECST, note 39](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/media/16/organes/delegations-offices-cec/opecst/pdf/note-39-recyclage-plastique); 2020; Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). European yields declared by the industry are **73% for PET (2018), 71% for rigid HDPE and 69% for rigid PP (2023), and about 50% for household films (2018)** ([PRE, PET 2020, p. 19](https://www.plasticsrecyclers.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/pet-market-in-europe-state-of-play-2020.pdf); [PRE, HDPE and PP rigids 2025, section 5b](https://plasticker.de/docs/news/HDPE_REPORT_2023_screen_spreads_VF%20%281%29.pdf); [PRE, flexible films, p. 17](https://www.plasticsrecyclers.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/flexible-films-market.pdf); Declared, FACT). The Netherlands lost 106 kt of the 369 kt of plastic packaging sent to recyclers in 2024, or 29% ([Verpact, report 2024, p. 25-28](https://www.verpact.nl/sites/default/files/2025-09/VRP_toelichting_verslagform_2024_13.pdf); Declared, FACT) [calc]. German recyclers lost about 21% in 2023 [calc: 0.53 ÷ 2.54 Mt, [Conversio, Stoffstrombild 2023, p. 17](https://vci.de/ergaenzende-downloads/kurzfassung-stoffstrombild-2023.pdf), Declared]. The French gap of 31.5% is plausible against these references. French recycled-plastic output is itself uncertain: SRP members produced 623,560 t in 2024 at about two thirds of capacity ([SRP, Petcore Europe 2025](https://www.petcore-europe.org/images/2025/2025-presentation-petcore-europe-conference/1055-sebastien-malangeau-presentation-enjeux-srp-%2027-05-2025.pdf); Declared, FACT), while FEDEREC reports 796 kt recycled by French plants, 632 kt of it from France ([FEDEREC, key figures 2024, p. 26](https://federrec.com/FEDEREC/documents/Chiffresclesdumarchedurecyclage202410263.pdf); Declared, FACT).

The four Citeo-funded surtri centres total **100 kt a year** of input: Ruffey-lès-Beaune 30 kt, Épinal 25 kt, Mende 15 kt and Thiverval-Grignon (Yvelines) 30 kt from end 2026 ([Citeo, Observatoire de la qualité 2025, p. 5](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/OBQ-2025-VDEF.pdf); [Citeo, centres de surtri](https://www.citeo.com/le-mag/les-centres-de-surtri-le-nouveau-maillon-de-la-chaine-du-recyclage-en-france/); Declared, FACT). None publishes a reject rate. Citeo's flow diagram shows only "refus de tri (PVC/films trop petits)", with no figure (Observatoire de la qualité 2025, p. 40-41). At Ruffey, the prefectoral order lets an on-site CSR unit take the surtri centre's refus ([Géorisques, AP n° 1749, 11/12/2023, p. 5](https://www.georisques.gouv.fr/webappReport/ws/installations/document/Lft2OgbD9CYowm32AaoRogigWafnhWIr); Declared, FACT).

### One fifth to one quarter of household packaging has no recycling route

The official 2021 indicator classes **66% of household plastic packaging as recyclable, 15% as "route in development" and 19% as having no route expected by 2025**: 17% PVC, complexes and other resins, and 2% recyclable resins carrying disruptors ([ADEME, 3R decree indicators, p. 14, method p. 10](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/documents/ADEME_Bilan%20indicateurs%20d%C3%A9cret%203R-Synthese.pdf); 2021; Declared, FACT; 20 of the 81 points are Citeo estimates, Modelled). On about 1,190 kt, that is **about 226 kt with no route and 179 kt in development** [calc; HYPOTHESIS]. Citeo now reports **75% recyclability for 2025** ([Citeo, annual report 2025, p. 5](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Rapport-dactivite-2025_Citeo.pdf); Declared, FACT), which implies **about 294 kt not recyclable** [calc: 25% × 1,174 kt]. OPECST cites 35% non-recyclable, without year or source ([OPECST, note 39](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/media/16/organes/delegations-offices-cec/opecst/pdf/note-39-recyclage-plastique); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS).

Producers pay a higher tariff for these formats rather than a separate malus. In 2026, "complex packaging or other resins excluding PVC" pays 140.01 c€/kg and PVC 210.01 c€/kg, against 70.00 to 84.01 c€/kg for recyclable resins. Citeo describes the former as having no recycling route but energy recovery, and states that the PVC tariff "fait office de malus" ([Citeo, tariff guide 2026, p. 9 and 26-27](https://ecd-bo.citeo.com/ecd2/medias/citeo/public/fr/guide-du-tarif-des-emballages-menagers-2026.pdf); Published tariff, FACT). The 100% malus raised €12.5 M in 2025 ([Citeo, annual report 2025, p. 14](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Rapport-dactivite-2025_Citeo.pdf); Contract/Transaction, FACT), which corresponds to roughly 8 to 23 kt of packaging [calc: €12.5 M ÷ 0.54 to 1.61 €/kg, 2025 rates]. Citeo does not publish tonnages by tariff line. Under extended sorting instructions these packages go in the yellow bin. They end up in refus, as non-target material in bales, or in OMR. They are a composition attribute of the streams above, not an additional stream.

### Non-household packaging adds about 1 Mt of non-recycled plastic

About 2.4 Mt of plastic packaging is placed on the French market each year, about half of it household ([Stratégie 3R, p. 8](https://www.ecologique-solidaire.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/Consulter%20la%20Strat%C3%A9gie%203R%20pour%20les%20emballages%20en%20plastique%20%C3%A0%20usage%20unique.pdf); data year not stated; Declared, FACT). Eurostat records **2,428,987 t of plastic packaging waste generated in 2023, 625,275 t recycled (25.7%) and 1,213,829 t recovered for energy** ([Eurostat, env_waspac](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/env_waspac?geo=FR&waste=W150102); 2023; Declared, FACT; project register). That leaves **about 1.80 Mt not recycled, of which about 0.59 Mt was neither recycled nor recovered for energy** [calc]. Subtracting household packaging not recycled (0.84 to 0.90 Mt in 2024) leaves **about 0.9 to 1.1 Mt of non-household plastic packaging not recycled** [calc; years mixed; HYPOTHESIS]. FEDEREC members collected 711,314 t of plastics from businesses in 2024, all plastics included ([FEDEREC, key figures 2024, p. 25](https://federrec.com/FEDEREC/documents/Chiffresclesdumarchedurecyclage202410263.pdf); Declared, FACT). Valorplast's director cites about 500,000 t of plastics collected at déchèteries that are complex to recycle ([IFPEN synthesis](https://www.ifpenergiesnouvelles.fr/print/pdf/node/1904); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). No source gives the tonnage or plastic content of commercial (DAE) sorting residues.

### Île-de-France sends 146 kt of refus to incineration

Île-de-France collected **539,795 t of packaging and paper (excluding glass) in 2024, or 43.5 kg per inhabitant**. Its refus rate was **27%, or 146,368 t**, and **98% of refus were incinerated** ([ORDIF, Bilan DMA 2024, slides 3 and 17-18](https://www.ordif.fr/fileadmin/DataStorage/user_upload/00-home/Bilan_DMA_2024%40.pdf); 2024; Declared, FACT). ### Producers pay Citeo, Citeo pays local authorities, buyers pay for good bales

Producers pay Citeo by weight and by unit. Weight tariffs for plastics rose by a **uniform 30.8% from 2025 to 2026** [calc], for example from 53.52 to 70.00 c€/kg for clear PET bottles and from 107.03 to 140.01 c€/kg for complex packaging ([Citeo, tariff 2025, p. 22](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2025-04/20241219_MAJ_Tarif_EM_2025-FR.pdf); [tariff guide 2026, p. 27](https://ecd-bo.citeo.com/ecd2/medias/citeo/public/fr/guide-du-tarif-des-emballages-menagers-2026.pdf); Published tariff, FACT). Total contributions were **€1,460 M in 2025**, of which €1,119 M on weight, for all materials ([Citeo, annual report 2025, p. 13](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Rapport-dactivite-2025_Citeo.pdf); Contract/Transaction, FACT). The plastic share is not published; it is of the order of €0.7 bn [calc: 1,174 kt × 0.59 to 0.64 €/kg; HYPOTHESIS].

Citeo pays local authorities **776 €/t of plastic recycled in 2024** (725 €/t in 2023), one rate for all plastic flows, plus a performance bonus ([consultation note on the 2024 scale, p. 2](https://www.consultations-publiques.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/baremesoutiens2024note-consultation30oct2023.pdf); [GPSEO amendment, p. 6](https://gpseo.fr/sites/gpseo/files/document/2024-04/cc_2024-04-04_29.1_annexe_1.pdf); Published tariff, FACT). The 2025-2029 mainland rate was not found. Buyers paid on average **203.4 €/t (Valorplast "Filière" option) and 235.3 €/t ("Fédérations" option)** for mainstream plastic bales in 2024, plus 10.4 €/t of transport aid. **No price is published for films or flux développement** ([Observatoire 2024, slides 8 and 45](https://www.citeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/20251006_Observatoire_reprise_2024_VDEF.pdf); Contract/Transaction, FACT). Valorplast must offer a price "positif ou nul" and guarantees a **0 €/t floor for films, mixed PE/PP/PS, PS, opaque PET and pots and trays** ([Valorplast contract, art. 11](https://clissonsevremaine.fr/fileadmin/Aggloh/Decisions_president/Annee_2024/03.2024-03_contrat_reprise_filiere_plastique_avec_Valorplast_executoire.pdf); Contract/Transaction, FACT). A recycled tonne of mainstream plastic therefore brings a local authority about **980 to 1,010 €/t** [calc: 776 + 203.4 to 235.3; HYPOTHESIS]. The same tonne in refus costs it 120 to 160 € HT/t plus TGAP. The swing of more than 1,100 €/t explains why local authorities and Citeo push plastic into recycling outlets. The new 2025-2029 Citeo contract also "prévoit une reprise des refus de tri et du flux développement" ([Aix-Marseille-Provence, decision of 03/04/2025](https://deliberations.ampmetropole.fr/documents/metropole/deliberations/2025/04/03/DECISION/125563.pdf); Declared, FACT). Its terms were not found. They could make Citeo, not local authorities, the counterparty for plastic-rich refus.

### Holders pay 120 to 160 €/t to dispose of refus, plus TGAP

Once plastic is in refus, the holder pays. Public prices and contracts for incinerating refus or comparable waste in 2024-2026 range from **65.66 €/t (Metz) to 159 € HT/t (Aix-Marseille-Provence)** before TGAP, with 121 € HT/t at SITOM Nord-Isère and 129.4 €/t at the Syctom (Table 11). All-in costs including transport reach about 153 €/t at Sarreguemines in 2024 [calc: €89,896.07 ÷ 588.64 t, [annual report 2024, p. 18](https://www.agglo-sarreguemines.fr/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/rapport-annuel_OM_rla_exercice-2024.pdf), Declared] and about 157 €/t at the SMITOM Nord 77 [calc]. In 2021, incinerators charged on average 76 €/t to public clients and **133 €/t to private clients**, excluding transport ([AMORCE, DT139, p. 33](https://amorce.asso.fr/publications/performances-recettes-et-couts-des-unites-de-traitement-thermique-des-dechets-donnees-2020-2021-dt139/download); Declared, FACT). The realistic 2026 band for refus at an incinerator is **about 120 to 160 € HT/t before TGAP**, with Metz as a low outlier [calc; HYPOTHESIS].

TGAP in 2026 is **16 €/t for high-performance incineration, 8 €/t for high-calorific residues from performant sorting, and 69 €/t for landfill**, rising to 20, 10 and 85 €/t in 2030 ([Loi n° 2026-103](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000053508155); Published tariff, FACT). The reduced rate requires a 12-month average calorific value of at least 9 MJ/kg, a residue rate of at most 35% for household packaging and paper, and an attestation given to the incinerator ([arrêté of 20/02/2023, art. 5-6 and annex](https://aida.ineris.fr/node/40904); Declared, FACT). Its continuation under the new tax code was not verified. Citeo's energy-recovery support for packaging in refus (€12.0 M in 2025) is worth about 15 to 16 €/t of refus [calc: €12.0 M ÷ 750 to 800 kt]. CSR does not pay its producer either. The producer pays production (85 to 115 €/t) and a boiler fee (10 to 30 €/t observed in Normandy) ([Normandie, Info-déchet CSR, p. 6](https://neci.normandie.fr/sites/default/files/2024-04/02%20info-dechet%20V1.0.pdf); 2021; Declared, FACT). France consumed 370 kt of CSR in 2021 ([FNADE and SN2E, p. 1-4](https://www.fnade.org/ressources/documents/source/1/4855-FNADE-SN2E-Synthese-modele-economique-CSR-VDEF.pdf); Declared, FACT) and 510 kt in 2023, against 2.5 Mt of theoretical capacity ([Sénat, amendment I-980](https://www.senat.fr/enseance/2025-2026/138/Amdt_I-980.html); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS).

### The State pays the EU 0.80 €/kg on non-recycled plastic packaging

The State, not the waste holder, pays the EU **0.80 €/kg of non-recycled plastic packaging waste**, and France receives no lump-sum reduction ([Council Decision 2020/2053, art. 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/FR/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32020D2053); Published tariff, FACT). France paid about €1.25 bn in 2021 and in 2022, and **€1.564 bn in 2023**. The Senate estimated €1.498 bn for 2024 and €1.464 bn for 2025 ([Sénat, PLF 2025 note, p. 6 and 19](https://senat.fr/fileadmin/Commissions/Finances/2024-2025/PLF_2025/NP/Mission_Affaires_europeennes_NP_PLF_2025.pdf); 2021-2023 Contract/Transaction, FACT; 2024-2025 Modelled, HYPOTHESIS). The same note states that the contribution is not a tax paid by households or firms (p. 6). The 2026 finance bill proposed a tax on eco-organisations of 30 €/t of non-recycled plastic in 2026, rising to 150 €/t in 2030 ([Banque des Territoires, 16/10/2025](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/plf-2026-les-coups-de-gueule-et-cartons-rouges-du-reseau-amorce); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). The Senate voted to delete it on 30/11/2025 ([Sénat, amendment I-80](https://www.senat.fr/enseance/2025-2026/138/Amdt_I-80.html); Declared, FACT). Its fate in the final 2026 finance law was not verified.

### Pyrolysis feedstock is bought when clean and paid away when mixed

Clean plastic feedstock for pyrolysis is a bought commodity. Platts assessed "process-ready" mixed plastic waste bales at **€248.81/t delivered in North-West Europe in June 2025** ([S&P Global, 18/07/2025](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/blog/chemicals/071825-pyrolysis-oil-a-circular-solution-to-plastic-waste); Declared (price index), FACT). The specification is at least 86% polyolefins, at most 1% PVC and at most 5% organics and moisture, sorted, washed and shredded ([Platts methodology, 12/03/2025](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/pricing-benchmarks/our-methodology/subscriber-notes/031225-platts-launches-european-american-advanced-recycling-mixed-plastic-waste-assessments); Declared, FACT). Mixed rejects are paid away. ICIS reported that sorting plants paid **€90 to 150/t ex-works** to have reject bales removed, around 2021 ([Recycling Magazine, citing ICIS](https://www.recycling-magazine.com/?p=34663); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). A KIT model assumes refuse-derived fuel at -60 to +30 €/t and a feedstock purchase at 15 €/t ([Stallkamp et al. 2024, p. 108 and table 3](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000167839/152125757); Modelled, HYPOTHESIS). No French or European pyrolysis operator discloses its feedstock price. Viridor ceased its European chemical recycling at Skive in June 2026 ([plasticker, 17/07/2026](https://plasticker.de/Plastics_News_48841_Viridor_Plastic_recycling_operations_at_Quantafuel_suspended?seite=8); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). Among streams already separated from residual waste, mixed refus are the only large one near Paris whose holder pays. They are poor in plastic. A tonne of Île-de-France refus carries 120 to 300 kg of plastic on the evidence available [calc]. If Urban Rig took 46,200 t of refus and sorted out the non-plastic fraction, it would recover about **5.5 to 13.9 kt of plastic** and would have to dispose of **32 to 41 kt of residue** [calc: 46,200 t × 70 to 88%]. At a gate fee of 80 to 110 €/t (ASSUMPTION) and a disposal cost of 130 to 175 €/t for the residue, the net cash per tonne of input before oil revenue and processing cost lies between **-74 €/t and +19 €/t** [calc: 80 minus 0.88 × 175; 110 minus 0.70 × 130; HYPOTHESIS]. The gate fee on mixed refus therefore mostly pays for disposing of what is not plastic. This holds unless the whole stream goes to the reactor, which lowers oil yield and raises solid residue. The reduced TGAP of 8 €/t lowers the holder's incineration cost where its refus qualify, which narrows the room for a gate fee further. Refus are also tied to the Syctom's 2026-2029 sorting contracts and to incineration concessions. Entry windows appear when contracts end, for example end 2028 for the SITOM Nord-Isère convention ([convention](https://cc-plainedelain.fr/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2024-093-annexe-convention.pdf); Contract/Transaction, FACT).

Plastic-rich feed does not carry a gate fee. All Île-de-France refus together hold at most about 44 kt of plastic [calc], less than the plant's 46.2 kt of input. Low-grade bales are purer, but Valorplast takes them at a 0 €/t floor and pays transport. Citeo has allocated films and PS to identified recyclers for six to nine years, and roughly half of the film tonnage to chemical recycling ([Citeo, tender press release, 10/03/2023](https://bo.citeo.com/sites/default/files/2023-03/Citeo_CP_Appel%20d%27offres%20recyclage%202eme%20tour_10022023%20%281%29.pdf); Declared, FACT). TotalEnergies' Grandpuits plant in Seine-et-Marne started in March 2026 with 15,000 t a year of capacity for such films ([TotalEnergies, 19/03/2026](https://totalenergies.com/system/files/documents/totalenergies_cp-totalenergies-demarre-1ere-usine-france-recyclage-chimique-plastique_2026_fr.pdf); Declared, FACT). Urban Rig would be a buyer in this market, at 0 €/t or more, and about €250/t for washed, shredded polyolefin feed meeting the Platts specification. Surtri rejects and regenerator residues near Paris are plastic-rich, and their holders probably pay to dispose of them (judgement), but their tonnages are small, unpublished and partly kept on site. They should be pursued by direct request (section 11), not assumed.

Several regulatory shifts favour a new outlet, but mostly after 2028. The Île-de-France landfill ceiling falls to 1.04 Mt in 2031 and landfill TGAP rises to 85 €/t in 2030. Exports of plastic waste to non-OECD countries are banned from 21 November 2026, at least until 21 May 2029 ([European Commission, plastic waste shipments](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/waste-and-recycling/waste-shipments/plastic-waste-shipments_en); Declared, FACT). The Commission proposed on 17/07/2026 to bring waste incineration into the EU ETS from 2031 ([Loyens & Loeff, 01/09/2026](https://www.loyensloeff.com/insights/news--events/news/european-commission-proposal-extension-of-the-eu-ets-to-waste-incineration/); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). From 2031, however, Thermo-sur-Seine at Vitry-sur-Seine will need solid recovered fuel from refus, bulky waste and commercial waste. The plant is to deliver 1.6 TWh of heat a year, mainly from three 111 MW boilers burning CSR or waste wood with a calorific value of at least 12 MJ/kg; the dossier does not state the fuel tonnage ([Thermo-sur-Seine, concertation dossier, July 2026](https://www.debatpublic.fr/sites/default/files/2026-08/Thermo-sur-Seine_Dossier-de-concertation_Juillet2026_web.pdf); Declared, FACT). That implies at most about **430 to 565 kt of fuel a year**, CSR and waste wood together [calc: 1.6 TWh ÷ (3.33 to 4.17 MWh/t × 0.85 to 0.90); an upper bound, because electric boilers and river heat recovery supply part of the heat; HYPOTHESIS]. Plastic is what gives such fuel its calorific value, so Thermo-sur-Seine will compete for the same plastic-rich residues.

Pyrolysis oil sold as fuel does not count as recycling under EU rules. The Waste Framework Directive excludes reprocessing into materials used as fuels from the definition of recycling ([Directive 2008/98/EC, art. 3(17)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/FR/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32008L0098); Unverified, not re-read in this round). The single-use plastics implementing decision applies a mass balance with "fuel use excluded" ([BKV, 25/02/2026](https://bkv-gmbh.de/news-reader-4/implementing-decision-on-supd-published.html); Secondary, HYPOTHESIS). A fuel route therefore earns no Citeo recycling support and does not reduce the EU plastic own resource paid by the State.

For the financial model, v2 recommends three separate lines (ASSUMPTION). Mixed refus: a gate fee of 80 to 110 €/t, used only with a letter of intent from a holder, paired with a residue-disposal cost of 130 to 175 €/t on the non-plastic fraction. Plastic-rich bales: a purchase cost of 0 to 250 €/t, with no gate fee. Residues of surtri and regenerators: no volume until a holder confirms tonnage and composition.

## 12. Conclusion

The plastic mass, the plastic purity and the gate fees sit in three different places. The mass is in residual waste: 2.33 Mt nationally and about 0.4 to 0.5 Mt in Île-de-France, burned under long contracts at about 110 €/t. The purity is in low-grade bales, which have a buyer at 0 €/t and are allocated to recyclers on contracts running to about 2029 to 2032. The gate fees are in mixed refus, which are 70 to 88% non-plastic, so most of the fee pays for disposing of what is not plastic. No public source shows a stream that is at once plastic-rich, large and paid for by its holder. v1's central assumption, a 670 kt stream with all three properties, does not survive the evidence. Urban Rig's revenue model must therefore choose between buying plastic-rich feed and processing mixed waste with residue disposal. It cannot assume both a high plastic share and a gate fee.

Two findings change how the market should be read. First, MODECOM measures 0.5 to 1.0 Mt more plastic packaging in public-service waste than Citeo's producers declare placing on the market. The residual stream may therefore be larger than the recycling statistics suggest, partly because business packaging enters the grey bin. Second, the regulatory pressure that would make holders pay to divert plastic arrives mostly between 2029 and 2031: landfill ceilings, TGAP at 85 €/t and incineration in the ETS. Thermo-sur-Seine will compete for plastic-rich residues from 2031, and the 8 €/t reduced TGAP keeps incineration cheap until then. The decisive data already exist at the operators: TGAP calorific-value attestations, NF X30-472 characterisations, GEREP residue declarations and contract unit prices obtainable through CADA requests. Collecting them, with one characterisation campaign on the targeted stream, is the condition for fixing the plastic share, the calorific value, the chlorine content and the feedstock price in the financial model.
