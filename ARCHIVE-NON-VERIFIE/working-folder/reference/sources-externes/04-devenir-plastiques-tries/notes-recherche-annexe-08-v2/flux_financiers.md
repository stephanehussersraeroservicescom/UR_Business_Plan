## 1. Consolidated map: who pays whom, and how much per tonne, at each node of the French plastic chain

### Takeaway
Plastic packaging carries a large positive value while it is "packaging to be recycled" and a cost once it becomes a residue:
- The producer pays Citeo about **700-840 €/t** (2026; 2 100 €/t for PVC).
- Citeo pays the collectivité **776 €/t** of recycled plastic (2024).
- The buyer pays about **200-235 €/t** on average for mainstream bales, but **0 €/t (contractual floor)** for films, mixes, PS and pots/barquettes.

As soon as the same plastic falls into refus or residues, the holder pays:
- **about 120-160 €/t to an UVE**, plus TGAP of 8-16 €/t (2026);
- or **90-150 €/t** in older NWE market data for MRF reject bales.

On the pyrolysis side, the only published price for process-ready feedstock (≥ 86 % polyolefins, washed, shredded) is **positive, about €249/t delivered NWE (June 2025)**. The plant pays the supplier; it does not receive a gate fee.

### Inferences
- [calc] Gross value per tonne of mainstream plastic recycled by a mainland collectivité in 2024 is about 776 €/t (Citeo) + 203-235 €/t (sale) ≈ **980-1 010 €/t**, before the performance bonus.
  - The same tonne lost to refus costs about 121-160 €/t plus TGAP.
  - The swing of more than 1 100 €/t explains why collectivités and Citeo push plastic into recycling outlets, and not into energy-type outlets.
  - A pyrolysis-to-fuel plant is not a recycling outlet under the EU calculation rules (fuel is excluded). So it cannot capture the Citeo soutien. **This exclusion rule was not re-read in this pass.**
- For a fuel-pyrolysis plant near Paris, the two French price anchors are therefore:
  - **(a) residues (refus, surtri and regeneration residues):** the holder pays today about 120-160 €/t plus TGAP to an UVE, so a gate fee is conceivable but capped by that alternative;
  - **(b) low-grade bales (films, MIX PE/PP/PS, PS):** these have a contractual buyer at ≥ 0 €/t that also pays transport, so no gate fee is realistic there.
- Clean, process-ready polyolefin feedstock trades at a **positive** price (about €250/t delivered NWE in mid-2025). Pre-treatment (sorting, washing, shredding) is the bridge between "the holder pays 120-160 €/t" and "the plant pays 250 €/t".

### Gaps
- The node "regenerator → disposal outlet" (residues of PET/PE regeneration plants) has no published French €/t. FPR Limay (54 000 t/yr of PET bales input) lists waste categories without tonnages or costs ([DRIEAT, CSS 20/11/2025](https://www.drieat.ile-de-france.developpement-durable.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/presentation_france_plastiques_recyclage_-_css_bassin_du_20-11-2025.pdf)).
- The terms of Citeo's recycling tenders (films, PS) with chemical recyclers are undisclosed (see §6).

## 2. Eco-contribution: Citeo tariff per kg for household plastic packaging (2024, 2025, 2026), bonuses and maluses including "sans filière", and total contributions for plastic

### Takeaway
- Citeo's weight tariffs for plastics rose by a **uniform +30,8 % between 2025 and 2026** `[calc]`. Examples: PET clair 53,52 → 70,00 ct €/kg; PE film 58,87 → 77,01; complex/other resins 107,03 → 140,01; PVC 160,55 → 210,01.
- They rise again in 2027: PET clair 73,19; complex 161,01; PVC 256,14.
- There is no separate "sans filière" malus. The penalty is built into the per-kg tariff: complex or other resins pay about 1,8× a PE film, and PVC about 2,7× `[calc]`. Recyclability-disruptor maluses add 50-100 % in 2026.
- Total Citeo contributions were **1 460 M€ in 2025** (1 190 M€ in 2024), all materials including paper. The plastic share is not disclosed. No 2024 per-kg tariff was found.

## 4. Buy-back prices actually received for plastic bales by flux (2023-2026), and evidence of negative prices

### Takeaway
- French flux-level monthly prices are not public: e-Valorplast is members-only, and none of the RPQS read itemised plastics by flux.
- The best national figure is Citeo's 2024 Observatoire: **203,4 €/t (Filière option) and 235,3 €/t (Fédérations option)** for mainstream plastics excluding films and surtri flux, plus 10,4 €/t of transport aid.
- The **contractual floors (2024-2029) are 0 €/t** for films, MIX PE/PP/PS, PS, PET opaque and pots/barquettes; 160 €/t for PET clair; 90 €/t for PET foncé; 80 €/t for PEHD+PP.
- Negative prices are contractually excluded in France: Valorplast must offer « positif ou nul ».
- In the UK they are explicit in 2026:

## 6. What European pyrolysis / chemical-recycling plants pay or charge for their feedstock

### Takeaway
Clean, "process-ready" polyolefin feedstock is a **bought** commodity:
- Platts assessed it at **€248,81/t DDP NWE on average in June 2025** (spec: ≥ 86 % PO, sorted, washed, shredded, ≥ 75 % oil yield).

Low-grade rejects are **paid away** by their holder:
- ICIS, c. 2021: MRFs paid €90-150/t ex-works NWE to remove MRF reject bales, while PVC-screened bales traded at positive values below €100/t.

Academic models assume roughly zero feedstock cost: −60 to +30 €/t for RDF, with 15 €/t as base case.

No pyrolysis operator read here (Indaver, Plastic Energy/TotalEnergies, Mura, Quantafuel/Viridor) discloses feedstock prices or gate fees. The sector is under stress: Viridor ceased its European chemical recycling (Quantafuel Skive) in mid-2026.

- Background (not re-read): ALBA (Germany): holder pays about 150 €/t to the EBS producer, who pays about 50 €/t to the cement plant; Roland Berger 2022: feedstock « at low (or even negative) costs ».

### Inferences
- The market is bifurcated by quality:
  - **(a) process-ready PO-rich bales** (the Platts spec) command a positive price, about €225-250/t delivered NWE in mid-2025, competing with mechanical recyclers;
  - **(b) MRF rejects and refus-type material**, which holders pay to remove at roughly the local incineration or landfill avoided cost (ICIS: €90-150/t in NWE, c. 2021). In France in 2026 that avoided cost is about 120-160 €/t at UVE.
- A Paris-region pyrolysis plant can **charge** a gate fee only on category (b). But category (b) needs pre-treatment to reach a pyrolysable spec: the Platts spec allows 5 % organics/moisture and 1 % PVC. The net margin therefore depends on sorting and washing costs and yield losses, which none of the sources quantify.
- French low-grade sorted flows (films, PS) are already contracted by Citeo to identified recyclers, including the TotalEnergies/Paprec Grandpuits chemical route and Indaver, for 6-9 years from 2023 or 2026. A newcomer would compete for these through Citeo tenders as a **recycler** (output must count as recycling), not as a fuel producer.

## 7. The EU plastic own resource (0,80 €/kg of non-recycled plastic packaging): France's contribution and any pass-through to producers

### Takeaway
- France paid about **1,25 Md€ in 2021 and 2022, 1,564 Md€ in 2023**. The 2024 estimate is 1,498 Md€ (amendments cite 1,52 Md€ for about 1,9 Mt), and the 2025 PLF forecast is 1,464 Md€.
- The contribution is paid from the State budget, inside the PSRUE. Through 2025 it was **not passed on** to producers or households.
- The PLF 2026 (art. 21) proposed a "taxe sur les emballages plastiques non recyclés" charged to éco-organismes: **30 €/t in 2026 rising to 150 €/t in 2030**, about 30-37 M€ of revenue in 2026.
- The Senate voted to delete it (amendment I-80, adopted, 30/11/2025). Whether it survived in the final loi de finances 2026 could not be verified.

### Inferences
- [calc] 1,564 Md€ / 0,80 €/kg ≈ **1,96 Mt** of non-recycled plastic packaging in 2023 (all packaging, household plus commercial and industrial). This is consistent with the 1,9 Mt cited in amendments. It is much larger than Citeo's household non-recycled plastic: 1 174 − 346 ≈ 0,83 Mt in 2025 `[calc]`.
- Each extra tonne of plastic packaging recycled under the EU calculation rules saves the French State 800 €. Fuel from pyrolysis is not "recycling" (rule not re-read here), so a pyrolysis-to-fuel plant **does not reduce** this contribution, whereas a pyrolysis-to-feedstock route counted as recycling would.
- Had the PLF 2026 tax survived, éco-organismes would have faced 30 €/t (2026) to 150 €/t (2030) on non-recycled plastic. That is a modest incentive compared with the 800 €/t EU rate.
